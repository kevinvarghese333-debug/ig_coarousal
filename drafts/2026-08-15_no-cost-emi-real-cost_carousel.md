# Zero-Cost EMI Has A Cost

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No-cost EMI" / "zero-cost EMI" on purchases and credit cards.
- Central idea: No-cost EMI does not remove interest. It moves the cost
  into the product price, a lost cash discount, or a processing fee,
  which is exactly what RBI flagged when it called zero percent interest
  "non-existent" in 2013.
- Primary audience: Salaried 21-40 Indian professionals who use credit
  cards or checkout EMI options on phones, appliances and big-ticket
  purchases.
- Audience tension: They feel smart for avoiding interest, so the idea
  that the "free" option was never actually free creates real friction
  and curiosity.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares (a checklist people use at
  their next checkout, and a fact worth sending to whoever swears by
  no-cost EMI).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It gives a concrete, low-effort
  action (compare upfront price vs total EMI payout) and a specific,
  slightly shocking regulatory fact (RBI says zero percent interest does
  not exist) that people did not expect from a checkout button they use
  often.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "The EMI said zero cost. The math did not agree."
2. Contrarian hook: "RBI banned zero percent interest in 2013. The button still says zero cost."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You picked 'no-cost EMI' to save money. You paid the same amount anyway."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Zero-cost EMI has a cost. It is hidden, not removed."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "RBI says zero percent interest does not exist. The checkout button disagrees."

**Selected hook:** Zero-cost EMI has a cost. It is hidden, not removed.

**Why it wins:** It states the entire tension in seven words, using "hidden, not removed" to promise a specific mechanism reveal rather than a vague warning, which earns the swipe without giving the answer away.

## Caption

Every checkout screen has a version of this button now: pay a little more later, but pay "no cost" for the privilege of splitting it up. It sounds like a workaround. Mostly, it is a rename.

RBI told banks back in 2013 that zero percent interest is not a real thing; the cost shows up somewhere, usually the price you would have paid anyway or a discount that quietly disappears when you switch from "pay now" to "pay later." The lender is not doing you a favour. It is just choosing where to put the number.

None of this means EMI is always wrong. Spreading a genuine, necessary expense can be a sensible cash-flow decision. The problem is choosing it because the label says "free," not because you checked the total.

Next time you see that button, do the two-line maths before you tap it.

Have you ever compared the upfront price against the total no-cost EMI payout, and were they actually the same?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, all 9 slides identical dimensions.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background throughout. Gold #C9A84C for headlines, accents, highlight-card borders and the recurring motif. Off-white #F6F1E7 for primary body text. Slate #AEB7C2 for secondary/support lines and fine print. No red or green needed on this carousel; both mechanisms are neutral, not alarmist or celebratory.
- Typography: Editorial serif for headlines and the big claim moments (Slides 1, 3, 4, 7, 9). Clean sans serif for body copy, labels, receipt/checklist card text, source notes and slide numbers. Two typefaces only, per brand rules.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None required. No real bank, app, or retailer UI is depicted anywhere in the carousel, per the no-fake-interface rule. The phone-frame on Slide 2 is a generic empty outline, not a screenshot.
- Chart and data guidance: No charts needed. Data is carried through the receipt card (Slide 5) and the three-box mechanism row (Slide 4), both simple enough to read in under three seconds.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes should sit in the 80-120px range on Slides 1, 3, 7 and 9; body copy 30-40px on Slides 2, 4, 5, 6, 8. Keep all text at least 80px from every edge for the profile-grid crop.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain strong contrast (off-white or gold text on navy background throughout, no gold-on-navy body text below 30px since gold has lower contrast than off-white). Avoid conveying the receipt strikethrough as the only signal, pair it with the word "disappears" in the caption text.
- Animation notes: None planned. Carousel is fully static; the mechanism is clear without motion, and static performs well for this format per the design guide.
