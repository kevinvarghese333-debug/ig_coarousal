# “No-Cost EMI” Is a Pricing Trick, Not a Discount

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How "no-cost EMI" schemes actually work at Indian checkouts.
- Central idea: "No-cost EMI" is not free credit. The interest cost is usually funded by a cash or instant discount you quietly give up, so the sticker price you end up paying is the same either way, and losing the "pain" of paying in full often makes people spend more than they intended.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals, 21 to 40, shopping festive sales and e-commerce checkouts, who default to "convert to EMI" without checking what they are giving up.
- Audience tension: The checkout screen frames EMI as a free upgrade ("₹0 extra cost"). The viewer has never been shown the mechanism that makes that claim technically true but practically misleading.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (this is a decision framework people want to revisit before their next big purchase) and shares (a "myth a friend believes" that is genuinely useful to send along).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes a purchase decision most readers make often (phones, appliances, festive sales), gives a concrete checklist, and ends with a judgement question that invites a real answer, not a platitude.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "The ₹0 on your EMI screen is hiding a real number."
2. Contrarian hook: "'No-cost EMI' is a pricing trick, not a discount."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You didn't get a discount. You just stopped noticing the cost."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Someone always pays the 'waived' interest. It is usually you, earlier in the receipt."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "Zero percent interest does not exist. RBI said so in 2013."

**Selected hook:** "'No-cost EMI' is a pricing trick, not a discount."

**Why it wins:** It is a direct contrarian correction of a phrase almost every reader has tapped "yes" to at checkout, which creates instant personal relevance and curiosity about the mechanism without needing a number to land.

## Caption

That "₹0 extra cost" line at checkout is doing a lot of work. No-cost EMI is not free credit. In most cases, the interest you are not paying is funded by a cash or instant discount you quietly give up, so the final price ends up roughly the same either way.

The bigger cost is not even the money. Paying in full makes a price sting for one moment, and that sting is doing you a favour: it makes you pause. Split into ten quiet instalments, that moment disappears, and research on instalment payments has found people spend more once a cost feels smaller.

None of this means EMI is always wrong. It means the "no cost" label is doing marketing, not accounting. Before your next big purchase, ask what discount you are giving up, what the processing fee is, and whether you would still buy it at the full price today.

Would you still buy it if the discount was shown instead of hidden?

- Typography: Playfair Display Bold/Regular for headlines, DM Sans Regular/Medium/Bold for body, labels, source notes and slide numbers. Two typefaces only, per brand rule.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None used. No fake checkout UI, bank interfaces or screenshots were generated, per the no-fake-UI rule; all diagrams are code-rendered cards and flow arrows.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 4 uses a three-card flow diagram (not a chart) to show value moving from discount to interest. Slide 5 uses a receipt-style comparison table with a highlighted total row. Both show one figure prominently rather than many.
- Mobile-readability notes: Body text set at 30 to 38px, headlines 54 to 112px depending on line length; all text kept inside a 90px safe margin on every edge; slide-number and brand marker sit 64px from the bottom edge, clear of the outer crop zone.
- Accessibility notes: High contrast throughout (off-white/gold text on navy background, contrast ratio well above WCAG AA for large text); no information conveyed by color alone (the red/green accents on slides 5 and 6 are paired with explicit ₹ labels and words, not color-only signals).
- Animation notes: All nine slides are static. No slide's meaning depends on motion; each slide's first frame is its only frame.
