# The Loan App Cooling-Off Window

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How loan apps make small borrowing feel harmless, and the RBI rule
  that exists to undo that feeling before it costs you.
- Central idea: The loan app is built to feel instant. The Key Fact
  Statement is built to feel skippable. RBI gives you a short window to
  undo that gap, and almost nobody uses it.
- Primary audience: Salaried professionals and first-time borrowers, 21 to
  40, who have tapped or been tempted by an instant loan-app notification.
- Audience tension: "It was such a small loan, why does it feel like this
  now" versus not knowing a legal, penalty-free exit window existed.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (this is a checklist people will want to
  find again before their next loan-app tap) and comments (a judgement
  question about reading terms).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It converts a vague unease about
  loan apps into a specific, usable right. People send this to a friend who
  just took one, and they save it for the next time a notification shows up.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "There is a 3-day window most borrowers never use."
2. Contrarian hook: "The loan app is fast on purpose. The cancel option is not."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "The loan took 90 seconds. The regret took longer."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Loan apps are not built for you to read. They are built for you to tap."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "You can still cancel that loan app loan. Most people do not know that."

**Selected hook:** "You can still cancel that loan app loan. Most people do not know that."

**Why it wins:** It is specific, makes the reader check their own situation immediately, and promises a genuinely useful fact rather than a vague warning.

## Caption

That loan-app notification is designed to make "get money now" feel like the only decision in the room. It is not the only decision. RBI requires every digital lender to show you a Key Fact Statement before you sign, stating the real annual cost, not the EMI you see on the screen. It also gives you a cooling-off window after you sign: 3 days if the loan runs a week or longer, 1 day if it is shorter. Exit in that window and you pay only what you borrowed plus the proportionate interest. No penalty.

Most people never open the Key Fact Statement. Most people do not know the window exists at all. By the time the EMI starts to pinch, it has already closed.

This is not a warning to avoid loan apps. It is a reminder that speed and fairness are not the same thing, and that RBI already built you an exit ramp. Use it while it is open.

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background, Gold #C9A84C accent, Off-white #F6F1E7 primary text, Slate #AEB7C2 secondary/fine-print text. No red or green needed; this carousel is a mechanism explainer, not a loss/gain comparison.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headline claims and the oversized "3" on Slide 6; Playfair Display Regular for the Slide 3 and Slide 7 statement lines; DM Sans Medium/Bold for labels, checklist items and numerals; DM Sans Regular for fine print and source citations.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real app screenshots used anywhere. All notification cards, checklists and timelines are generic code-rendered illustrations, not copies of any specific lender's interface.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 6's timeline is the only data visual; it marks a single point (day 3) rather than plotting a full chart, since the mastermind file calls for highlighting one figure, not ten.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80 to 120px, body 30 to 40px, fine print kept to source citations only, never carrying the main idea. Tested for legibility at phone-thumbnail size.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background holds strong contrast throughout; gold accents used only for emphasis marks (ticks, dots, underlines), never as body text colour, to preserve contrast ratio.
- Animation notes: Entire carousel is static. Motion was not necessary to make any mechanism clearer, per the mastermind's "static can perform extremely well" guidance.
