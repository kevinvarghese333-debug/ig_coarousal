# No Cost EMI: The Interest Didn't Disappear. It Moved.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Why "No Cost EMI" is not actually a free decision.
- Central idea: No-cost EMI does not remove the interest. It moves the cost into a missing discount or a repriced sticker price, and RBI itself has said "zero percent interest" is not a real category.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals and first-time borrowers, 21 to 40, who see "No Cost EMI" at checkout on phones, laptops and appliances.
- Audience tension: The label sounds like a technical fact (zero interest). It is really a pricing decision, and most buyers never check the real number before tapping it.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares. This is built to be saved before a purchase and sent to someone mid-checkout.
- Why people may save, share or comment: The Slide 8 checklist is directly useful before a real purchase (save). The core reveal ("the discount disappears, not the interest") is the kind of specific, precise observation people forward to a friend who is about to buy on EMI (share). The caption question asks for a real experience, not an opinion (comment).

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "The 'No Cost EMI' button has a cost. It's just not on this screen."
2. Contrarian hook: "Zero cost EMI is not a free decision."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You picked No Cost EMI to save money. The store already priced that in."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "No Cost EMI: the interest didn't disappear. It moved."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "That 'No Cost' button can quietly cost you a discount you never saw."

**Selected hook:** No Cost EMI: the interest didn't disappear. It moved.

**Why it wins:** It names the exact mechanism instead of a vague warning, so it works as a standalone claim in the feed and creates one specific open loop, moved where, that the whole carousel is built to answer.

## Caption

Every checkout screen in India has that button now. No Cost EMI. Phone, laptop, fridge, sometimes even a course or a service package. It feels like a technical fact: zero interest, no catch. It is really a pricing decision dressed up as a technical one.

The retailer often prices the EMI plan off the full sticker price. The discount a cash buyer would get is frequently the same amount the "zero" interest would have cost anyway. RBI has been clear that zero percent interest is not treated as a real category. The money did not disappear. It moved.

None of this means EMI itself is a bad tool. Spreading a genuine purchase over a few months can be a reasonable choice. The problem is choosing it because a label said "free," not because you compared the real numbers first.

Next time that button appears, ask for the cash price before you tap it.

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18. Gold accent #C9A84C for the chip, arrows and dividers. Off-white #F6F1E7 for primary text. Slate #AEB7C2 for secondary text and source notes. Warning red and muted green are not used in this carousel, no warning or positive-signal moment calls for them.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headlines and the Slide 6 and 9 statements. DM Sans Regular and Medium for body copy and labels. DM Sans Bold, tabular figures, for the receipt-card numbers on Slides 2 and 5.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. Every visual is code-rendered (chip, checkout card, flow diagram, receipt cards, checklist). No real app screenshots or logos are used, so there is nothing to source-clear.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 4 uses a two-box flow diagram, not a chart, since the point is a cost moving from one place to another. Slide 5 uses a before/after receipt comparison rather than a bar chart, since there are only two states to compare.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headlines sized for legibility at arm's length on a phone. Body copy stays within the 30 to 40 px equivalent range. Safe margins of at least 80 px on every edge, wider on Slides 1, 3, 6 and 9 which rely on empty space.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast, off-white and gold text on navy. Do not use colour alone to carry meaning, for example the checklist on Slide 8 uses numbers and text together, not colour coding alone.
- Animation notes: Static by default across all 9 slides, per the design system's default. If a future edit adds motion, the single candidate is the Slide 4 arrow drawing from the "Interest cost" box to the "Missing cash discount" box, since that is the one moment motion would clarify rather than decorate.
