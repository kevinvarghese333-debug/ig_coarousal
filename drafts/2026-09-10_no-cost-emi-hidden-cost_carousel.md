# Zero-Cost EMI Is Not a Free Decision

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No Cost EMI" on credit cards and e-commerce checkouts
- Central idea: Zero-cost EMI does not remove the interest. It renames it
  and moves it into a lost discount, a processing fee, and GST on that
  fee, and it changes what people end up buying.
- Primary audience: Salaried 21-40 year old Indian buyers choosing EMI at
  checkout for phones, appliances and big-ticket purchases
- Audience tension: They believe "No Cost" means no cost, and feel smart
  for picking it. The carousel challenges that without shaming them.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (it is a decision rule people want to
  reuse at the next checkout) plus shares (a warning worth forwarding
  before a friend buys a phone)
- Why people may save, share or comment: It gives a concrete rule to
  apply next time, describes a checkout moment almost every reader has
  been in, and invites a specific, low-friction comment question

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Zero-cost EMI. Interesting choice of words."
2. Contrarian hook: "There is no such thing as a free loan."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You picked 'No Cost EMI' because it sounded free."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Someone is still paying your interest. It is not the bank."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "The 'No Cost' label is doing a lot of work at that checkout screen."

**Selected hook:** "Zero-cost EMI. Interesting choice of words."

**Why it wins:** It is short, mildly sceptical without being alarmist, and creates instant curiosity about what the "real" word should be, which is exactly the tension the carousel resolves.

## Caption

"No Cost EMI" is one of the most successful pieces of naming in Indian retail. It makes a loan sound like a feature.

The bank is not lending for free. It cannot, that is not how lending works. The interest still gets paid, usually by you, through a discount you quietly lose, a processing fee, and GST on that fee. The RBI said as much back in 2013: zero percent interest does not really exist.

None of this means EMI is bad. Spreading a genuine expense across months can be a sensible choice. The problem is choosing it because a label said "free," instead of comparing what you would actually pay either way.

Next time that badge shows up at checkout, do the one-minute math before you tap it.

Have you ever checked the full EMI total against the cash price, and found they were not the same?
