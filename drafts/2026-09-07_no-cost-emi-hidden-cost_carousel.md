# RBI Says Zero Percent Interest Does Not Exist

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: No Cost EMI / zero percent interest checkout offers
- Central idea: Zero percent interest does not vanish the cost of borrowing, it relabels it as a discount, a processing fee, or a foreclosure charge.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals aged 21 to 40 who buy phones, appliances, and gadgets on EMI, often at marketplace or store checkout.
- Audience tension: They believe "No Cost EMI" means the loan is genuinely free, and feel smart choosing it, when the cost has simply moved somewhere they did not check.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (this is a checkout decision rule people will want to reread before their next purchase) and shares (a myth people can correct for a friend or family member).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It corrects a specific, common belief with a regulator-backed fact, gives a concrete three-question checklist to use at checkout, and invites a comment about a purchase decision people can answer from memory.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "That '0% EMI' badge is doing some quiet work."
2. Contrarian hook: "Zero percent interest does not exist. RBI said so."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You paid zero interest. You still paid more."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The 0% is real. The 'free' is not."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "No Cost EMI has a cost. It just has a different name."

**Selected hook:** "RBI says zero percent interest does not exist."

**Why it wins:** It borrows regulator authority instead of a generic claim, states a specific and surprising fact in under ten words, and immediately creates the "wait, is that true?" reaction the design mastermind flags as the strongest swipe trigger.

## Caption

That "No Cost EMI" button at checkout feels like a free pass. It is not.

RBI has said the idea of zero percent interest does not really exist. What usually happens is simpler than it looks: the discount you would have gotten for paying cash gets used up to cancel out the interest instead. The bank still gets paid. You just do not see it labelled as interest.

On top of that, most No Cost EMI plans still carry a processing fee, often with GST added, and a fee if you close the loan early. None of that shows up in the "0%" on the button.

None of this means EMI is always a bad idea. It means the badge is not the full picture. Before you tap it, ask what the cash price would have been, what the processing fee is, and what closing early would cost you.
