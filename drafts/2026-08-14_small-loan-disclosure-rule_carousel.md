# Approved In 90 Seconds, Understood In Never

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How small-ticket loan apps stay easy to accept even after RBI's new disclosure rules.
- Central idea: RBI now forces lending apps to show the real cost of a loan on one page before you borrow, but the app is still designed to get you to tap accept before you read it.
- Primary audience: Salaried and gig-earning Indians aged 21 to 40 who have used, or are tempted to use, an instant small-ticket loan app.
- Audience tension: People feel informed because a disclosure exists, without realising the app's design still works against them reading it.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares, driven by a checklist people want to use before their next loan tap.
- Why people may save, share or comment: The practical rule on Slide 8 is genuinely useful before any small loan, the topic names a common private habit people rarely discuss, and the closing line is quotable enough to send to a friend who borrows casually.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: There is a page your loan app hopes you skip.
2. Contrarian hook: A lower EMI is not a lower loan.
3. Relatable-pain hook: Approved in 90 seconds. Understood in never.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: RBI made the loan app confess. It still wins.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Your loan app is legally honest now. That is not the same as safe.

**Selected hook:** Approved in 90 seconds. Understood in never.

**Why it wins:** It names the exact feeling of instant-loan borrowing in under one second, without any explanation needed, and sets up a tension the rest of the carousel resolves.

## Caption

Every borrower knows this feeling. A screen says approved, a button says accept, and the loan lands before the thinking does.

Since RBI's 2025 Digital Lending Directions, every regulated lender has to hand you a Key Fact Statement before you borrow. One page. The real annual cost, the recovery process, the cooling-off window, and the contact for a grievance if something goes wrong.

That is a genuine, useful change. Borrowers finally get the number that matters, in writing, before they sign anything.

But disclosure is not the same as attention. Most loan apps are built to move you from notification to acceptance in under a minute, and a fact sheet was never designed to compete with a big, bright accept button.

The rule protects you on paper. Reading it is still on you.

Before your next small loan, open that page and look for one number specifically: the APR, not the EMI.

What is the smallest loan that ended up costing you the most attention, not just money?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, all nine slides, RGB, sRGB.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background, gold #C9A84C accent and highlights, off-white #F6F1E7 primary text, slate #AEB7C2 secondary text. Warning red and muted green not needed for this carousel, no loss or gain framing that calls for them.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headline moments, Playfair Display Regular for supporting editorial lines, DM Sans Regular and Medium for body copy and labels, DM Sans Bold for the "Accept" block label and card headers.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No real app screenshots are used, all UI references are abstract code-rendered shapes to avoid implying any specific lender or app.
- Chart and data guidance: No chart needed, the "cost card" and the size-contrast comparison on slide 6 carry the data points visually.
- Mobile-readability notes: Keep all copy inside a 90px safe margin on every edge, avoid placing the slide-number label near the bottom corner where the profile-grid crop clips content.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain strong contrast between off-white or gold text and the navy background, avoid gold text smaller than 32px since it has lower contrast than off-white.
- Animation notes: Fully static carousel. Motion was considered for slide 6's size comparison but a static contrast reads instantly and does not need it.
