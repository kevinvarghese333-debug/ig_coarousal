# The Minimum-Due Trap

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Paying only the "Minimum Amount Due" on a credit card, on time,
  still triggers interest from your purchase date.
- Central idea: "On time" and "in full" are two different tests. Pay only
  the minimum due, and the bank can charge interest from the day you
  swiped, on the whole cycle, not just the part you did not pay.
- Primary audience: Salaried professionals aged 21 to 40 in India who use
  a credit card monthly and believe they are being responsible because
  they never miss a payment date.
- Audience tension: They feel safe because the app shows "payment
  received, on time." They do not realise the app rewarded the wrong
  behaviour.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (it is a decision rule) and shares
  (people forward it to a parent or friend who pays "something" every
  month).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It corrects a specific, common
  belief with a concrete rule they can check on their own next statement,
  and it is exactly the kind of thing people send into a family WhatsApp
  group with "see, this is what I meant."

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "You have never missed a credit card payment. So why is
   there interest on your bill?"
2. Contrarian hook: "Paying on time does not mean what you think it means."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You paid something before the due date. The bank
   still charged you interest from the day you swiped."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The minimum due button is not there to
   help you. It is there to keep you paying interest."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "This one line on your credit card bill decides
   whether you pay zero interest or interest from day one."

**Selected hook:** "You have never missed a credit card payment. So why is
there interest on your bill?"

**Why it wins:** It states a genuine contradiction the reader has likely
lived through, so it earns curiosity through recognition rather than
manufactured shock.

## Caption

You probably have a system. Pay something before the due date, keep the
account in good standing, move on. That system protects your credit
score. It does not protect your wallet.

Under RBI's credit card rules, the interest-free period only survives if
you clear the Total Amount Due in full. Pay only the Minimum Amount Due,
and the card issuer can charge interest from the date of each purchase in
that cycle, not from the due date, and not only on the part you did not
pay.

This is not a trick any single bank invented. It is how revolving credit
is built to work almost everywhere. The button that feels like the safe,
responsible choice is often the most expensive one available to you.

Next time your statement lands, look for the Total Amount Due first. The
minimum due is not a repayment plan. It is a floor.

Have you ever paid only the minimum and been surprised by the interest
that showed up next month?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text
  #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 used only on
  Slide 5's interest bracket, muted green #4B8B72 unused in this
  carousel (no positive signal to mark).
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headlines and hero numbers, DM
  Sans Medium and Bold for subheads and labels, DM Sans Regular for body
  and fine print. No more than two typefaces used anywhere.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real bank UI, no real screenshots,
  no logos. Every "notification," "card," "button," and "receipt" element
  is a generic, code-drawn shape so nothing on-slide misrepresents a real
  product or institution.
- Chart and data guidance: No chart in this carousel. The receipt and
  bracket illustration on Slide 5 functions as the single data visual and
  stays legible in under three seconds.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80 to 120 px, subheads 38 to
  52 px, body 30 to 40 px, fine print kept to source tags only, never to
  main claims. All text kept inside a safe margin roughly 90 px from every
  edge on the 1080 x 1350 canvas.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background gives strong
  contrast throughout. Gold and slate are used for emphasis, not as the
  only way information is conveyed, so the hierarchy still reads if a
  viewer cannot distinguish colour. Red appears once, on Slide 5, paired
  with a label, not colour alone.
- Animation notes: Static carousel by default, per the mastermind's
  guidance that static carousels can perform extremely well. No slide in
  this set required motion to be understood.
