# No-Cost EMI Has A Cost. It's Just Not Called Interest.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Why "no cost EMI" is not actually interest-free credit.
- Central idea: No-cost EMI does not remove the cost of credit, it moves it
  into a discount you quietly give up, so the "zero" only applies to the
  label, not the price.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals aged 21 to 40 buying
  phones, appliances or electronics on a credit card, who see "No Cost EMI"
  at checkout and read it as free money.
- Audience tension: People feel smart for "avoiding interest" while paying
  close to the same total amount they would have paid by forgoing a cash
  discount, so the choice feels safe when it is really just unexamined.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares, because this reframes a
  checkout habit nearly every reader has used, and it gives them language
  to explain it to someone else.
- Why people may save, share or comment: It hands them a specific decision
  rule to use next time they see "No Cost EMI," and it is the kind of
  correction people forward to a sibling or friend right before a big
  purchase.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: No-cost EMI has a cost. It's just not called interest.
2. Contrarian hook: "Zero percent interest" is not a real product. It never
   was.
3. Relatable-pain hook: You paid full price. In instalments. And still
   called it a discount.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: The bank isn't lending you money for
   free. Someone else is paying for it.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: That "0% interest" badge at checkout is doing a
   lot of hiding.

**Selected hook:** No-cost EMI has a cost. It's just not called interest.

**Why it wins:** It names the exact tension in nine words, is instantly
understandable without context, and creates a direct question the rest of
the carousel exists to answer.

## Caption

"No Cost EMI" sits right there at checkout, next to a green tick, telling
you this purchase costs nothing extra. Most of the time, that is not quite
true.

The mechanism is simple once you see it. Sellers often give a cash discount
to people who pay the full price upfront. Choose no-cost EMI instead, and
that discount is usually the first thing to go. Its value is what ends up
covering the interest the lender still needs to earn. RBI has said for
years that "zero percent interest" is not really a product, the cost just
moves somewhere else.

None of this means EMI is bad. It means the label is doing more marketing
than the maths. Before you tap that button again, check the cash price,
check the discount you are giving up, and check any processing fee.

If you had to pay the full amount today, in cash, would you still buy it?

That question is worth more than the "0%" badge.

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background, gold #C9A84C accent and rules,
  off-white #F6F1E7 primary text, slate #AEB7C2 secondary text and fine
  print, signal red #D94B45 used only on the fee/warning line in Slide 6,
  muted green #4B8B72 used only for the discount tick motif in Slide 2 and
  4.
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display Bold, or Liberation Serif
  Bold as the rendering fallback) for headlines and big numbers, clean sans
  (DM Sans, or DejaVu Sans / Liberation Sans as the rendering fallback) for
  body copy, labels, fine print and slide numbers. Only two typefaces used.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. All visuals are code-drawn
  (phone-frame outline, receipt cards, tick-box markers). No real app UI,
  no bank interface, no logos.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5 and 6 use a running receipt total rather
  than a chart, since the story is a single accumulating number, not a
  trend over time. Only one figure is highlighted per slide.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes target 80 to 110px, body copy
  30 to 38px, fine print used only once (Slide 5 disclaimer) and kept
  legible, never below a comfortable reading size. Safe margins of roughly
  70 to 90px on all sides keep text clear of the profile-grid crop.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background maintains strong
  contrast throughout. Red and green accents are used sparingly and never
  as the only signal, each is paired with explanatory text so colour is
  never the sole carrier of meaning.
- Animation notes: None used. The carousel is fully static, since the
  mechanism is explained clearly through copy, comparison cards and a
  running receipt motif without needing motion.
