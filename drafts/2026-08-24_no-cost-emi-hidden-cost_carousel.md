# Zero Interest, Someone Still Pays: The No Cost EMI Carousel

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How "No Cost EMI" actually works on everyday purchases like phones and appliances.
- Central idea: No Cost EMI does not remove the interest. It moves the interest into the cash discount you give up.
- Primary audience: Salaried professionals and first-time borrowers aged 21 to 40 who buy phones, laptops or appliances on EMI through e-commerce checkouts or in-store offers.
- Audience tension: The offer looks free and feels safe because the bill shows "0% interest," but the reader has never checked what they gave up to get that label.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares. This is a decision rule people will want to keep and send to someone about to buy a phone.
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes a routine checkout choice as a trade they are making unknowingly, gives a one-line question to ask before tapping "No Cost EMI," and invites a very specific, answerable comment about their own experience.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "No Cost EMI. So where did the interest go?"
2. Contrarian hook: "Zero-cost EMI is not a free decision."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You did not pay interest. You paid something else."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The 'No Cost EMI' button moves the cost. It does not remove it."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "Zero interest. Someone is still paying it."

**Selected hook:** "Zero interest. Someone is still paying it."

**Why it wins:** It states the twist as fact instead of a question, so it creates immediate tension (if interest is zero, who pays?) without sounding like a warning or a guru headline.

## Caption

You have seen this at checkout. Pay now, or choose No Cost EMI, same total either way, interest showing as zero. It looks like a free upgrade. Most of the time, it is a trade you made without noticing.

On many no-cost EMI offers, the interest gets funded by the discount you would have received for paying upfront. Say the discount instead of interest. The number on your bill does not move. Where it comes from does.

This has been flagged before. Regulators have objected to "zero percent interest" labelling in the past, for the same reason we are talking about today: the interest does not disappear, it just changes its name.

None of this makes No Cost EMI a bad choice. Spreading a big purchase can be sensible. The point is to choose it on purpose, knowing what you gave up, instead of tapping it because the number said zero.

Next time you are at checkout, what would you have paid for the same thing today?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2 for source notes and slide labels. Warning red and muted green not used in this carousel, since there is no true warning or positive-signal moment that needs them.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headlines and the largest claim lines, Playfair Display Regular for supporting lines that need an editorial feel, DM Sans Regular and Medium for body copy and labels, DM Sans Bold for the source note label and the "01/09" style slide markers.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No real bank interface, checkout screen or financial statement is reproduced. All checkout and card visuals are original, code-drawn, generic shapes, not a copy of any specific platform's interface.
- Chart and data guidance: No numeric chart is used because no single verified rupee or percentage figure was available for a worked example. The two-column card and flow diagram carry the mechanism instead of invented numbers.
- Mobile-readability notes: Keep all text at least 90 px from every edge. Headline sizes range 70 to 108 px depending on line length, body copy 34 to 44 px, source note and slide label 22 to 26 px.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast, off-white or gold text on navy background throughout, avoid gold-on-navy for body copy smaller than 30 px since contrast drops at small sizes, use pure off-white for body text instead.
- Animation notes: Carousel ships static. Slide 4's arrow and Slide 6's flow line are noted as optional future motion treatments only, not required for this delivery.
