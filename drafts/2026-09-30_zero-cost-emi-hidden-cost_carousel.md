# Zero-Cost EMI Is Not Always a Free Decision

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost or no-cost EMI on consumer purchases
- Central idea: The interest in a "zero-cost" EMI does not disappear. It moves into a discount, a price or a fee, so the real question is who pays and how.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21 to 40 who buy phones and appliances on card EMI
- Audience tension: The offer feels free, yet lenders do not lend for free
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (a checkout checklist), with comments as secondary
- Why people may save, share or comment: The three-question rule is easy to reuse at checkout, the myth is one a friend or family member believes, and the closing question asks for a real judgement

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Who actually pays for your “no-cost” EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: “Zero-cost” EMI is not always a free decision.
3. Relatable-pain hook: The ₹10,000 a month that felt like nothing.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: How “no-cost” EMI gets its cost back.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Check this before you tap “No-cost EMI”.

**Selected hook:** “Zero-cost” EMI is not always a free decision.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a phrase the viewer sees at every checkout, in a calm and specific way, without fear or a fake number.

## Caption

Zero-cost EMI is one of the most familiar buttons in Indian shopping. It is also one of the least examined.

The lender still needs a return on the money it advances. Often, that cost is met by a seller or brand discount. Sometimes it sits in a higher listed price, a processing fee, or the cash discount you gave up to choose the instalment plan. In 2013, RBI said that in zero-percent card EMI schemes the interest was often camouflaged as processing fees.

Terms differ by lender, card and merchant, so check the current terms before you tap. The ₹60,000 phone and ₹3,000 discount on the slides are illustrative numbers, not a real offer.

The habit is real, though. A smaller monthly number makes a large purchase feel lighter than it is.

This is education, not a recommendation for any product or person.

Cash discount or no-cost EMI: which would you pick on a ₹60,000 phone, and what would change your mind?
