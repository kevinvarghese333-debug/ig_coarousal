# No-Cost EMI Still Has a Cost

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Why zero-cost EMI is not always a free decision
- Central idea: "No-cost" EMI usually moves the interest into the price, so the cost shows up as a lost discount, fees or GST rather than an interest line.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances on EMI during sale season
- Audience tension: It feels free, so nobody checks the pay-in-full price.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (a decision rule) and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: One simple check works at any checkout, a friend or family member always believes "no-cost" means free, and the closing question invites personal stories.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Where is the cost hiding in "No-cost EMI"?
2. Contrarian hook: No-cost EMI is not free.
3. Relatable-pain hook: Phone: ₹30,000. EMI: ₹5,000 × 6. Feels free.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Who pays the interest on a no-cost EMI?
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Check this before you tap "No-cost EMI".

**Selected hook:** No-cost EMI. It has a cost. Just not on the EMI line.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a belief the viewer holds today and promises to show where the cost moved.
