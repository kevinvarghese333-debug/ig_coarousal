# No-cost EMI: Someone Is Still Paying

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: No-cost EMI in festive sales
- Central idea: "No-cost" EMI does not remove the interest. It moves it, and some cost can still reach the buyer.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried viewers, 21 to 40, buying phones and appliances on EMI
- Audience tension: The EMI feels small and free, but the real price is unclear
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A three-question checklist to save, a festive-sale warning to share, and a judgement question about convenience versus trap

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Who actually pays for "no-cost" EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost" EMI is a pricing choice, not a free loan.
3. Relatable-pain hook: Your EMI feels small. The price may not be.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: The interest did not vanish. It moved.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Before you tap EMI this festive season, check one number.

**Selected hook:** "No-cost" EMI. Someone is still paying.

**Why it wins:** It is about a thing the viewer sees in every sale and it creates a clear open question.

## Caption

Festive sale season is when "no-cost EMI" shows up on every product page.

Here is the part worth knowing. In most cases the lender is still charging interest. What changes is who appears to pay it. Often the seller adjusts the price with an upfront discount that cancels the interest, so the headline interest figure looks like zero.

What can still reach you: GST on the interest component, a processing fee, and charges if you close the loan early. Terms differ by lender, card and seller, so check the current terms on the checkout screen or the loan summary before you confirm.

The example in the carousel is illustrative. It assumes a ₹60,000 phone, 6 months and 15% a year. Your numbers will differ.

One habit that helps: ask for the price if you pay upfront today, and compare that number with the EMI price. They are not always the same.

Which is closer to your experience: no-cost EMI made a purchase easier, or made you buy something you would have skipped? Tell me in the comments.
