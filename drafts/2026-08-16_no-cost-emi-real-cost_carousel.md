# 'No Cost EMI' Was Never Actually Free

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How "No Cost EMI" schemes actually work
- Central idea: The "No Cost EMI" badge does not remove the cost of borrowing. It moves the cost into a discount you never got and a processing fee you now pay.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals, 21 to 40, who use credit-card EMI at checkout for phones, appliances and electronics
- Audience tension: They want the convenience of EMI without feeling like they are paying more, and they rarely compare the EMI price to the cash price
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments, with share potential from the myth-busting angle
- Why people may save, share or comment: The checklist is genuinely useful before the next big purchase, the mechanism reveal is a "wait, really?" moment worth sending to a friend, and the closing question invites people to share their own "free that wasn't free" story

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: The zero in "No Cost EMI" is not really zero.
2. Contrarian hook: "No Cost EMI" is not the same as free EMI.
3. Relatable-pain hook: You picked "No Cost EMI" to save money. You may have paid more.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: This is how "No Cost EMI" quietly makes money off you.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Do this maths before your next "No Cost EMI" purchase.

**Selected hook:** "No Cost EMI" was never actually free.

**Why it wins:** It is a direct contrarian correction of a phrase the reader has tapped without thinking, so it creates instant tension and a reason to find out where the missing cost went.

## Caption

You tap "No Cost EMI" because the math looks simple. Zero percent interest, split payments, no extra cost. Except the zero only applies to the number the bank shows you at checkout.

Here is what actually happens. The seller pays your interest to the bank upfront, then quietly removes the cash discount you would have got if you paid outright. The bank still collects a processing fee, and that fee usually carries GST. Nobody loses money in this chain except the person who did not compare prices.

In 2013 the RBI itself flagged this, calling the zero percent interest in these schemes "camouflaged" and passed on to customers through processing fees.

None of this means EMI is bad. It means "no cost" is a marketing label, not a financial fact. The fix is boring but effective: ask for the cash price before you tap EMI, then do the math.
