# No Cost EMI Is Not the Same as Zero Cost

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No cost EMI" schemes at checkout
- Central idea: Zero-cost EMI removes the interest line, not the cost. The cost moves into the price, a processing fee, or a discount you quietly stop getting.
- Primary audience: Salaried professionals and first-time borrowers aged 21-40 who see "No Cost EMI" at e-commerce checkout or in-store
- Audience tension: They know something feels slightly off about "free" credit, but they cannot name the mechanism, so they keep tapping it anyway
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (this is a decision rule people want to keep) and shares (it is the kind of thing you forward to someone about to buy a phone)
- Why people may save, share or comment: It hands them a specific question to ask before checkout, it names a regulatory fact most people have never heard, and it closes with a comment prompt about a live decision

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Zero-cost EMI is not the same as zero cost."
2. Contrarian hook: "The bank did not make interest disappear. It moved it."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You did not pay interest. You paid in a different way."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "No lender gives away money for free. Not even at checkout."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "RBI already told banks: free EMI does not exist."

**Selected hook:** "Zero-cost EMI is not the same as zero cost."

**Why it wins:** It is specific to a decision the reader has made recently, contradicts the badge they trust, and creates an immediate "wait, what happens to the interest then" question that only a swipe can answer.

## Caption

"No Cost EMI" is one of the most trusted badges at checkout, and one of the least explained. In 2013, RBI told banks plainly that a truly free EMI does not exist. That does not mean the schemes are illegal or always bad. It means the cost has to move somewhere else: into the listed price, into a processing fee, or into a cash discount you no longer receive.

None of this makes EMI wrong. It makes it a decision, not a default. The part worth noticing is not the rupee amount. It is the moment No Cost EMI quietly removes: the pause where you ask whether you can actually afford the thing today, in full.

Next time you see that badge, run the three checks in this carousel before you tap it.

Have you ever checked the cash price of something after already choosing the EMI option, and did the numbers match?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, RGB, one PNG per slide.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, slate secondary text #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 used only on the struck-through elements in Slides 1 and 5, muted green unused in this carousel.
- Typography: Editorial serif for headlines and the receipt card's "NO COST EMI" mark; clean sans serif for body copy, checklist rows, captions, source note and slide markers. Brand spec calls for Playfair Display and DM Sans; this run substitutes a local OFL-licensed serif/sans pair since the named font files are not present in `fonts/` (see final response for exact substitute names).
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No real bank interface, app screen or financial statement is depicted anywhere in this carousel, only original code-rendered receipt cards and comparison blocks.
- Chart and data guidance: No chart. The recurring receipt-card motif carries the data-reveal role instead, growing one line item per slide from Slide 4 to Slide 6.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay at 74-104px depending on line length, body copy 34-40px, slide marker and source note kept small and in the safe zone at least 60px from every edge.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy exceeds standard contrast minimums; gold accent is used for emphasis only, never as the sole carrier of meaning (also paired with strike-through or position cues); red is only used with a visual strike-through, not as a standalone signal.
- Animation notes: None. Fully static carousel; the receipt motif's growth across static slides substitutes for animation.
