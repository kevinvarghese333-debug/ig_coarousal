# No-Cost EMI Has A Cost

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: No-cost EMI on everyday purchases (phones, appliances, travel, credit cards).
- Central idea: No-cost EMI removes the visible interest number, but it does not remove the cost. The cost moves into a forgone cash discount, a fee, or a blocked credit limit.
- Primary audience: Salaried, 21-40, first-time credit card and EMI users buying phones, appliances, and travel on card-linked EMI.
- Audience tension: They chose EMI specifically to avoid paying interest, and feel financially smart for doing so. The carousel questions that feeling without shaming them.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares (a checklist-style decision rule people keep for their next purchase, and a mechanism worth forwarding to someone about to buy on EMI).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes something they use often (the "0% EMI" badge) and gives a concrete two-question check before the next tap. It is specific enough to forward to a sibling or friend mid-purchase.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "The badge says 0%. The bill says otherwise."
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost EMI has a cost. Just not one you can see."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You picked EMI to dodge interest. The bank still got paid."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Someone pays the interest on your 'no-cost' EMI. It is not the bank."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "That '0% interest' badge is doing you a favour. Read that again."

**Selected hook:** No-cost EMI has a cost. Just not one you can see.

**Why it wins:** It states the topic in the first four words, then opens a real A-vs-B gap (visible cost vs hidden cost) that only the next eight slides can close, without sounding alarmist.

## Caption

"No-cost EMI" sits on almost every checkout page in India right now, from phones to flights. The phrase does real work. It tells you there is no interest, so the decision feels safe.

The Reserve Bank of India has said, since 2013, that a genuinely interest-free retail loan does not exist. If a bank is not charging interest, the cost has moved somewhere else, usually into a discount you silently give up, or a fee folded into the plan.

There is also a quieter cost. If the EMI runs through a credit card, the full purchase blocks your credit limit for the whole tenure, not just this month's instalment. That shows up in your utilisation, and eventually in your score, even if you never miss a payment.

None of this means EMI is always wrong. It means the "0%" badge is a marketing choice, not a fact about your bill. Do the arithmetic before the tap.

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background, Gold #C9A84C accents and key numbers, Off-white #F6F1E7 primary text, Slate #AEB7C2 secondary/source text, Red #D94B45 used sparingly on the receipt card's "cost" rows, Green #4B8B72 used sparingly for the "paid on time" check mark on Slide 7.
- Typography: Editorial serif for headlines and big claims, clean sans serif for body, labels, source notes and the "01/09" slide counter. Numerals set in the sans serif at bold weight.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. Every visual element (badge stamp, price tag, comparison card, limit bar, receipt card, check/cross marks) is code-rendered geometry and type, not a screenshot, to avoid depicting any real app, bank interface or statement.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 6's credit-limit bar is the only chart-like element. It shows one filled proportion against one label, understandable in under three seconds. No axes needed since it is a single before/after fill, not a time series.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay large (roughly 78 to 110 px depending on line length), body copy stays at 34 to 40 px, and no text sits within roughly 70 px of any edge.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background keeps strong contrast throughout. Gold is used for emphasis and numbers only, never for full paragraphs, so colour is never the sole carrier of meaning.
- Animation notes: Carousel ships fully static. No slide's meaning depends on motion.
