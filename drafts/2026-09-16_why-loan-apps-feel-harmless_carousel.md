# The Loan Isn't the Product. The Speed Is.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How short-tenor loan apps make borrowing feel harmless.
- Central idea: Loan apps don't compete on cost. They compete on removing every moment where a borrower would normally pause, which is exactly why RBI had to legislate a pause back into the process.
- Primary audience: Salaried professionals aged 21 to 40 who have used, or been tempted by, an instant small-ticket loan app before salary day.
- Audience tension: The loan felt like a harmless, well-designed convenience. Slide 4 onward reframes that convenience as a deliberate design choice with a business incentive behind it.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (this is a practical rule people will want to check before their next loan-app notification) and comments (the closing question invites a real admission).
- Why people may save, share or comment: The cooling-off period and KFS/APR rule are things almost nobody knows they have. That is inherently shareable ("send this to the friend who just took a loan app EMI") and save-worthy (a rule to check before accepting).

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "A ₹5,000 loan. Ninety seconds. That's not an accident."
2. Contrarian hook: "Loan apps didn't make borrowing cheaper. They made it faster."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "Salary's ten days away. The app already knows."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The loan isn't the product. The speed is."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "RBI had to legally force a pause button into instant loans."

**Selected hook:** "The loan isn't the product. The speed is."

**Why it wins:** It is short, specific to the mechanism the whole carousel explains, and creates genuine curiosity (what does that even mean?) without needing a fear-based opener.

## Caption

Ten seconds to open the app. Ninety seconds to get approved. That speed is not a bug, and it is not really about you needing the money urgently. It is the product.

Most short-tenor loan apps are built the same way: a small first loan that feels harmless, a limit that rises automatically once you repay on time, and an approval process with no human in the loop to make you pause. None of that is illegal. It is just designed to remove the moment where you would normally think twice.

RBI's own response tells you the design worked a little too well. Regulated digital lenders now have to hand you a Key Fact Statement with the real APR, not just the EMI, and give you a short window, one to three days depending on the loan, to walk away by repaying only what you borrowed plus interest for those days. No penalty.

That window exists because someone decided borrowers needed it legislated, not assumed.

Have you ever checked the APR on a loan app, or only the EMI?

- Image or screenshot requirements: None. All visuals are code-rendered (phone-frame outlines, rupee-figure blocks, checklist rows, dividers). No fake app UI, no fake bank interface.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5's three-step flow is the only "chart," and it must be readable in under three seconds: three rupee blocks growing left to right with a one-word label under each.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80-120px, subhead 38-52px, body 30-40px, generous line spacing, nothing readable placed within roughly 60px of any edge.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain strong contrast (off-white/gold text on navy background exceeds standard contrast minimums). Avoid conveying meaning through colour alone; the escalation on slide 6 and warning tone rely on type size and copy, not colour.
- Animation notes: None planned. Static carousel; motion was not judged necessary to comprehension for this story.
