# The Loan App Doesn't Want You Doing The Maths

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How small loan apps are designed to make a loan feel effortless and harmless, and what RBI's digital lending rules already require in return.
- Central idea: A loan app's speed and simplicity are a design choice that benefits the lender's approval volume, not proof that the loan is cheap or safe, and the rules that protect a borrower exist precisely because most apps would rather that borrower not look too closely.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals aged 21 to 40 who have used, or been tempted by, an instant small-ticket loan app.
- Audience tension: The ease of a loan app feels like proof of trustworthiness, but ease and cost are two different things, and most borrowers never check the annualised cost or who the actual regulated lender is.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (a 3-point checklist people will want before their next loan) and comments (a personal, judgement-based question about past borrowing).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It is a practical checklist they can act on immediately, it is the kind of post someone sends to a sibling who just installed a loan app, and it invites people to admit or defend their own borrowing habits.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Your loan is approved in 90 seconds. Here's what that speed is hiding."
2. Contrarian hook: "A loan app's speed is not a favour. It is a strategy."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "Rs 5,000. No paperwork. Money in your account before your tea gets cold."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The loan app doesn't want you doing the maths."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "The easiest loan you will ever take might be the most expensive one."

**Selected hook:** "The loan app doesn't want you doing the maths."

**Why it wins:** It is a hidden-mechanism hook, the pattern the mastermind flags as strongest for this audience, and it is specific enough to be understood and to sting in under one second.

## Caption

Every fast loan app promises the same thing: speed, simplicity, no paperwork. That is also exactly how the real cost stays out of view.

Before 2022, India's digital lending space had a genuine trust problem. Some apps pooled borrower repayments through their own accounts, buried the true cost inside a flat fee, and leaned on aggressive recovery tactics when people fell behind. RBI's digital lending rules exist because of that history, not as a formality.

The rules are already on your side. The Key Fact Statement is supposed to show your actual annualised cost. A cooling-off window is supposed to let you exit without penalty. Your repayment is supposed to move bank to bank, never through the app's own account.

Most borrowers never check for any of this. The next time a loan is one tap away, that is the tap worth slowing down for.

Which of these three checks would you have skipped before today?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, RGB PNG, one file per slide.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background throughout. Gold #C9A84C for eyebrows, dividers, accents and the follow line. Off-white #F6F1E7 for primary headlines and body text. Slate #AEB7C2 for secondary and fine-print text. Red #D94B45 reserved for the single APR warning figure on Slide 5. Green #4B8B72 used only for the checkmark icons on Slide 7's rule card.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold and Regular for headlines, pull-quotes and the receipt's APR figure. DM Sans Regular, Medium and Bold for body copy, labels, chrome and checklist text. Two typefaces only, as required.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. Every visual is code-rendered: a phone-outline motif, a push-notification card, a receipt card, a Key Fact Statement rule card, a numbered checklist, and a three-point repeat-loan timeline. No real app screenshots, no stock imagery.
- Chart and data guidance: One data moment only, the receipt card on Slide 5, highlighting a single number (~73% APR) rather than a table of figures. Clearly labelled as an illustrative example, not a real product's fee schedule.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headlines run 68 to 96px, body copy 26 to 40px, a 96px safe margin is enforced on all four sides on every slide, and no slide uses more than three visual clusters.
- Accessibility notes: All text uses high-contrast off-white or gold on navy. The checklist and rule card pair colour with numerals or checkmark icons rather than relying on colour alone.
- Animation notes: Static by default across all nine slides, consistent with the brand's default. No slide currently requires motion for comprehension; Slide 5's APR reveal is the only candidate if a motion version is ever produced.
