# You Never Missed a Bill. The Interest Came Back Anyway.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: The credit card "Convert to EMI" button and how it quietly reintroduces interest.
- Central idea: Converting a credit card purchase into EMI feels like discipline, but it quietly brings back the interest, fees and GST that paying your bill in full and on time was supposed to avoid.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals, 21 to 40, who use credit cards and pay their bill in full most months.
- Audience tension: They believe "I always pay on time, so I never pay interest." That belief breaks the moment they tap "Convert to EMI" without checking the cost.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (the pre-tap checklist) and comments (readers sharing their own EMI-conversion experience).
- Why people may save, share or comment: The checklist on Slide 8 is genuinely useful before their next EMI conversion. The core line "on-time does not mean interest-free" is a sharp, shareable correction to a common belief. The topic invites people to compare notes on what they were charged.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: You never missed a bill. The interest came back anyway.
2. Contrarian hook: Paying your credit card in full does not always mean interest-free.
3. Relatable-pain hook: One EMI tap undid months of on-time payments.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: This is how your bank earns from your "discipline."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: The button that quietly turns your bill into a loan.

**Selected hook:** You never missed a bill. The interest came back anyway.

**Why it wins:** It creates an immediate contradiction for anyone who prides themselves on paying on time, so the only way to resolve it is to swipe.

## Caption

Kevin here. Every month, thousands of card users do everything right. They check the due date. They pay the full amount before it is due. They feel responsible about it.

Then one purchase gets converted into EMI, maybe from a bank SMS, maybe from a checkout screen offer, and the math quietly changes. Interest returns. A processing fee shows up. GST sits on top of both. None of it looks like a penalty. It just looks like a slightly bigger bill that still gets paid on time.

Your credit score will not catch this. Your on-time-payment streak will not catch this either. Only checking the interest rate and the processing fee before you tap "convert" will.

This is not a case against EMI. Spreading a large expense sometimes makes sense. It is a case for reading the fine print before your thumb decides for you.

Have you ever converted a credit card bill to EMI and later wondered where the extra money went?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background, Gold #C9A84C accents and hero numbers, Off-white #F6F1E7 primary text, Slate #AEB7C2 secondary text and source notes, Signal red #D94B45 for the GST/cost escalation only, Muted green #4B8B72 for the "paid in full" stamp only.
- Typography: Editorial serif for headlines (Playfair Display, or Liberation Serif as fallback), clean sans serif for body, labels, checklist rows, source notes and slide numbers (DM Sans, or DejaVu Sans as fallback). No more than two typefaces used anywhere.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real bank screenshots. All UI elements (phone frame, "Convert to EMI" button, statement card) are simple code-rendered outlines, not fake app recreations.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5's "12%-24%" is the only large data callout; no charts required for this topic. Source note stays small, in slate, non-competing with the headline.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay in the 80-120px range on cover and big-claim slides, body copy 30-40px, safe margins of at least 80px on all sides so nothing is lost in the profile-grid crop.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast (off-white or gold text on navy background throughout). Avoid placing gold text on gold elements. Keep line lengths short for readability at small size.
- Animation notes: None required. Every slide works as a static image; the statement-card motif provides visual movement across the sequence without needing actual animation.
