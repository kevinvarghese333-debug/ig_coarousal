# The Minimum Due Trap

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Why paying only the "minimum amount due" on a credit card is not the safe middle path it feels like.
- Central idea: Paying the minimum due keeps your card out of default, but it quietly switches off the free credit that made the card worth having in the first place.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals, 21 to 40, who carry at least one credit card and have, at some point, paid less than the full statement amount.
- Audience tension: Paying the minimum due feels responsible ("I didn't miss my payment") when the mechanism working against them is invisible.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares, this is a decision-rule carousel people forward to someone who "always pays the minimum."
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes an everyday, non-alarming habit as expensive, gives a concrete rule to check, and is the kind of warning people send to a sibling or partner rather than keep to themselves.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: The credit card button that quietly changes how your money works.
2. Contrarian hook: Paying your credit card bill is not the same as paying it off.
3. Relatable-pain hook: You paid the minimum due. The interest did not care.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: The "minimum due" button was never built to protect you.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: One habit quietly switches off your credit card's free credit.

**Selected hook:** Paying your credit card bill is not the same as paying it off.

**Why it wins:** It works alone in the feed with zero context, creates instant tension for anyone who has ever paid less than the full amount, and it is specific enough to promise a real mechanism rather than generic advice.

## Caption

You did not miss a payment. So it does not feel like a mistake.

That is exactly why the minimum due is easy to underestimate. It is not built to flag danger. It is built to keep an account technically current while interest keeps compounding quietly in the background, and while the free credit period on your new spending disappears without an alert.

None of this means credit cards are bad. It means "not overdue" and "not expensive" are two different things, and the gap between them is where a lot of quiet, avoidable cost lives.

If you already pay your statement in full every month, this probably will not change anything for you. If you sometimes do not, it might be worth fifteen minutes with your card's terms and conditions this week.

This is general information, not personalised financial advice. Your card's actual terms may vary, check them directly.

If you had to explain the minimum due to a friend in one honest sentence, what would you tell them?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, RGB, one PNG per slide, portrait 4:5.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 (interest-free OFF state only), muted green #4B8B72 (interest-free ON state only).
- Typography: Editorial serif for headlines and big ₹ figures, clean sans serif for body copy, labels, source notes and slide numbers. Intended fonts are Playfair Display (headlines) and DM Sans (body/labels); if those font files are not present in the environment, Liberation Serif and DejaVu Sans are used as the closest available fallback, preserving the serif/sans contrast and mobile readability.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No screenshots, logos or external images are used, all visuals are code-rendered (receipt/statement outline, stacked bar, money-flow arrows, status pill, rule box).
- Chart and data guidance: One stacked bar (Slide 4) and one three-step money-flow diagram (Slide 5), each highlighting a single figure rather than a dense dataset. No axes beyond simple labels, both readable in under three seconds.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80 to 110 px, body copy 32 to 40 px, fine print (source note on Slide 4 and Slide 9 only) kept small but never below comfortable legibility, all key text kept inside a safe margin away from edges and away from the profile-grid crop zone.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast (off-white or gold text on navy, or dark text on the off-white card in Slide 7), avoid conveying the interest-free ON/OFF state through colour alone, pair it with the text label "ON" or "OFF" so it does not rely on colour perception.
- Animation notes: All 9 slides are static. Motion was considered for the status-pill flip on Slide 6 but a static reveal is clear enough on its own and keeps the production simple and dependable.
