# On Time Is Not the Same as Paid Off

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Paying only the "minimum due" on a credit card, even before the due date, quietly cancels the interest-free period.
- Central idea: Being on time is not the same as being paid off. The minimum due button feels responsible but starts a 30-48% annual interest clock on the entire bill, from the date you spent the money.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21-40 with at least one credit card, especially first-time cardholders and anyone who has ever tapped "pay minimum due" without reading past that screen.
- Audience tension: Feeling financially responsible ("I paid on time") while quietly accumulating high-interest debt without realising the mechanism.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares, since this is a decision-rule carousel people will want to reference before their next bill and forward to someone who "always pays the minimum."
- Why people may save, share or comment: It corrects a specific, common false belief with a concrete rupee example; it is the kind of post people send to a sibling or partner who carries a balance; it invites an honest comment about their own payment habit.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: The number on your bill is not the real cost.
2. Contrarian hook: On time is not the same as paid off.
3. Relatable-pain hook: You paid on time. You are still paying 40% interest.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Minimum due" is the most profitable button on your bill.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Paying the minimum is how a small bill becomes a long one.

**Selected hook:** On time is not the same as paid off.

**Why it wins:** It directly corrects a belief nearly every cardholder holds, creates instant personal tension without needing an explanation, and sets up a swipe to find out why.

## Caption

You paid the bill before the due date. The app did not flag anything. Nothing felt wrong.

But "on time" only describes when you paid. It says nothing about how much.

The minimum due button exists because someone has to make the small number look safe. It is structured so you can tap it, feel responsible, and move on, while the interest-free period quietly ends and the bank starts charging on the full amount from the date you actually spent it, not from the due date.

This is not about being careless. Most people who fall into this never miss a due date. They just do not realise that paying something is not the same as paying it off.

If you carry a balance right now, the fastest thing you can do is stop treating it like a manageable side note and start treating it like the most expensive loan in your life, because on most cards, that is exactly what it is.

Full payment or minimum due, most months? Answer honestly in the comments.

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 used only on Slides 4, 5 and 6 for the interest/risk elements, muted green #4B8B72 not used in this carousel since there is no positive financial signal to highlight.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headlines and hero numbers, Playfair Display Regular for select subheads, DM Sans Regular/Medium/Bold for body copy, labels, source notes and the slide-count marker.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No bank app, statement, or UI screenshot is used anywhere in this carousel, by design, since no specific issuer's interface was verified for reproduction.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5 uses a code-drawn receipt-card with four labelled rows, not a chart. Slide 6 uses a simple small downward line to represent credit score pressure, with no axis numbers implied as precise data.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay between 80 and 120 px, body between 30 and 40 px, all key text kept at least 90 px from every edge to survive the profile-grid crop.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy maintains strong contrast throughout; red is used only for warning emphasis, never as the sole carrier of meaning (always paired with words like "interest" or a minus sign context).
- Animation notes: None. This carousel is static by default per the design system; no slide required motion to be understood.
