# Your Credit Score Is Fine. Your Money Is Not.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: What actually happens when you pay only the credit card minimum due, every month, on time.
- Central idea: Paying the minimum due keeps your credit score safe. It does not keep your money safe.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian credit card holders, 21 to 40, who pay on time and assume that means they are fine.
- Audience tension: "I am not in trouble. I always pay something before the due date." The carousel shows why that belief can be wrong.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares. This is a decision-framework carousel people forward to a sibling, partner or friend who "always pays the minimum."
- Why people may save, share or comment: It corrects a specific, common belief with a mechanism, not a warning. It gives a one-line question to ask before the next bill. It is easy to send to someone with "this is you" energy.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Your credit score is fine. Your money is not."
2. Contrarian hook: "Paying on time does not mean you are debt-free."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You paid the minimum due again. That was the trap."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Minimum due exists because it works better for the bank than for you."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "That green 'payment successful' is hiding a 40 percent problem."

**Selected hook:** Your credit score is fine. Your money is not.

**Why it wins:** It states the exact tension in seven words, is understandable in under a second, and creates a gap (fine vs. not fine) the reader needs slide 2 to close.

## Caption

You pay the minimum due. On time, every time. The app says "payment successful." Your score stays healthy. It feels like proof that you are managing the card well.

Here is the part that does not show up on the home screen. Paying only the minimum does not clear the bill, it mostly covers that cycle's interest and fees. Once you miss paying the full amount, the interest-free period ends, and interest is charged on what you owe from the date you spent it. At the kind of rates Indian card issuers commonly charge on a carried balance, that adds up faster than most minimum payments shrink it.

None of this shows up as a problem on your credit report. Your score only checks whether you paid on time, not how much you are still carrying. Two people can have the same score and completely different amounts of debt.

This is not a reason to panic about your card. It is a reason to ask one question before your next bill: are you clearing it, or are you about to carry it?

What is the smallest financial habit that quietly cost you the most?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 reserved for the crossed-out interest-free period detail on Slide 4 only, muted green #4B8B72 for the steady "score" dot motif.
- Typography: Editorial serif for headlines and the ₹50,000 hero figure (target: Playfair Display Bold, fallback in this build: DejaVu Serif Bold). Clean sans serif for body copy, labels, source notes and slide numbers (target: DM Sans, fallback in this build: DejaVu Sans Regular/Bold). No more than two typefaces used anywhere.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real bank UI, no real screenshots. All visuals are code-rendered abstractions (statement card, phone-frame outline, comparison block) so nothing resembles an actual bank's interface.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 6 uses a simple two-column comparison, not a chart. Slide 5's ₹50,000 figure and interest range are the only numbers presented as "big" data, kept to one figure per slide per the one-figure rule.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headlines sized 80 to 120px, subheads 38 to 52px, body 30 to 40px, all text kept inside a safe margin of at least 90px from every edge so nothing is cropped in the profile grid.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background maintains strong contrast throughout. Gold and green are used for accent and motif only, never as the sole carrier of meaning (labels accompany every colour-coded element).
- Animation notes: None required. Every slide's first frame is designed to work as a static image, consistent with the mastermind's default of static-first carousels.
