# No-Cost EMI Is Not Actually Free

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No-Cost EMI" on phones, appliances and electronics.
- Central idea: No-cost EMI removes the interest you can see. It does not
  remove the interest. It just moves it into the price, the discount you
  do not get, or a fee, so "no cost" really means "no cost you can see."
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals, 21 to 40, shopping the
  festive sale season, comfortable swiping "No Cost EMI" at checkout
  without reading the fine print.
- Audience tension: They believe "no cost" is a fact about the product.
  It is a fact about how the bill is presented.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (it is a checklist they will want before
  their next purchase) and comments (a specific, judgement-based question).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes something they do
  often (festive EMI shopping) and gives them one concrete question to ask
  next time, which is exactly the kind of "send this to a friend before
  they buy" content that gets shared in family and friend chats.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "That 'No Cost EMI' button has a cost. You are just not shown it."
2. Contrarian hook: "'No Cost EMI' is not a discount. It is a payment plan wearing a discount's clothes."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You picked No Cost EMI to save money. The bank did not agree to lose any."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Someone always pays the interest on 'No Cost EMI.' It is usually you, quietly."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "RBI has a name for 'zero percent interest.' It is not zero percent."

**Selected hook:** "That 'No Cost EMI' button has a cost. You are just not shown it."

**Why it wins:** It names the exact object the viewer sees at checkout (the button), creates immediate tension between the label and reality, and promises a concrete reveal rather than a vague warning.

## Caption

Every festive season, the checkout screen gets a new favourite button:
No Cost EMI. It feels like a discount. It is not one. It is a way of
paying, and the bank still gets paid for lending you the money, just not
in a line item labelled "interest."

RBI flagged this back in 2013, when it said the idea of truly zero
percent interest does not hold up. The cost has to land somewhere. Most
of the time it lands in a cash discount you quietly do not get, a higher
listed price for the EMI plan, or a processing fee with GST on top.

None of this makes No Cost EMI a trap. It makes it a feature you should
read like one, not a discount you should trust like one. The real
question is not whether the EMI is free. It is whether you would still
buy the thing at its full price, today, in cash.

What is something you bought on EMI that you would not have bought in
cash?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, all 9 slides, RGB, sRGB colour.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18. Gold accent #C9A84C for emphasis, labels and the recurring receipt/checklist rules. Off-white #F6F1E7 for primary text. Slate #AEB7C2 for secondary text and fine print. Signal red #D94B45 used once, on Slide 6's closing line only. Muted green #4B8B72 not used in this carousel (no positive-signal moment).
- Typography: Editorial serif for headline moments (Playfair Display where available); clean sans serif for body, labels, source notes and slide numbers (DM Sans where available). See font-availability note below.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real bank or app screenshots. All visuals are code-drawn (phone-frame outline, receipt-style cards, checklist card). No fake UI, no fake statements.
- Chart and data guidance: No chart needed for this topic; the receipt-growth motif carries the "cost accumulating" idea instead of a bar or line chart, which keeps the carousel free of unverified precise figures.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay large (roughly 76 to 108 px depending on line length); body copy stays at 34 to 40 px; fine print (source note, "illustrative example" tag) kept legible at a minimum of 26 px, never smaller.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background maintains strong contrast throughout; gold-on-navy used only for emphasis words and rules, not full paragraphs, to avoid low-contrast reading strain; red used sparingly and never as the only signal (always paired with text).
- Animation notes: None used. Every slide is designed to read fully as a static image; no slide depends on motion for comprehension.
