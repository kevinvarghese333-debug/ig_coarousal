# "No-Cost" EMI Is Not Always a Free Decision

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost / no-cost EMI on phones and appliances
- Central idea: A no-cost EMI still carries interest. A seller discount cancels it, and fees and GST may still show up.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian viewers aged 21 to 40 buying phones and gadgets on EMI
- Audience tension: It says zero, so it feels free. Is it?
- Primary engagement goal: Saves, then comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A decision they face before every big purchase, with a three-question checklist and a prompt to check their own statement.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Find the line on your EMI bill that says zero, and the one that does not.
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost" EMI is not always a free decision.
3. Relatable-pain hook: New phone. Six EMIs. "No-cost." Then the statement arrives.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Who pays the interest on a no-cost EMI?
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Read the GST line before you tap "No-cost EMI".

**Selected hook:** "No-cost" EMI is not always a free decision.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a belief the viewer holds at checkout and promises a specific thing to look for.
