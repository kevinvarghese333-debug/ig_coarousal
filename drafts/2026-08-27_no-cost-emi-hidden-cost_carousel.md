# No Cost EMI Is Not the Same as Free

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No Cost EMI" offers at checkout, and what the label actually means.
- Central idea: No Cost EMI is not interest-free. The interest cost does not disappear, it gets moved into the price, a forgone discount, or a processing fee, and RBI itself said "zero percent interest" is not a real concept.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals and first-time buyers, 21 to 40, who have picked a "No Cost EMI" option on a phone, appliance or gadget purchase.
- Audience tension: The label feels like a free upgrade. Most people have never asked who is actually paying the interest, or where that cost went.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares. This is a decision-framework carousel people will want to keep for their next big purchase, and send to a sibling or friend who is about to buy on EMI.
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes a purchase decision they make often, gives them a two-question checklist to use next time, and cites a regulator instead of a guru opinion, which makes it easy to forward without sounding preachy.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Zero percent interest does not exist. RBI said so, in writing."
2. Contrarian hook: "'No Cost EMI' has a cost. It is just never written on the button."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You picked 'No Cost EMI' because it felt free. It was not."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The bank never lends for nothing. Someone else is paying your interest."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "That 'No Cost EMI' button is hiding where your interest went."

**Selected hook:** "Zero percent interest does not exist. RBI said so, in writing."

**Why it wins:** It borrows instant authority from a regulator instead of an opinion, which stops the scroll and earns trust in one line, and it directly contradicts what the checkout button implies, creating immediate tension.

## Caption

You picked "No Cost EMI" at checkout because it felt like a free upgrade. It usually is not.

In 2013, RBI made a simple point to banks: zero percent interest is not a real thing. If a lender is not charging interest, someone else is paying it, and that cost has to turn up somewhere.

Most of the time it turns up in one of three places. The listed price goes up slightly. The cash discount you would have gotten for paying upfront quietly disappears. Or there is a processing fee, with GST added on top.

None of this makes No Cost EMI a bad option. Spreading a big purchase over a few months can be a reasonable choice. The problem is choosing it without checking what the cash price actually was.

Before your next big purchase, compare two numbers: the full cash price with any discount, and the total EMI outlay, fees included. The gap between them is your real cost, just wearing a different name.

Have you ever compared the two and found a bigger gap than you expected?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all nine slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2 for labels and fine print, muted red-adjacent #D94B45 used sparingly on Slide 6 for the two new cost line items, muted green #4B8B72 reserved and unused in this carousel (no positive financial signal to highlight).
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headlines and hero numbers, Playfair Display Regular for secondary serif moments, DM Sans Regular/Medium/Bold for body copy, labels, receipt line items, source notes and slide numbers. Two typefaces only.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. Every visual is code-rendered (receipt card, stamp, flow diagram, split panel). No real app screenshots, no fake bank interfaces, no third-party logos.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 4 uses a simple three-box money-flow diagram, not a chart with axes. No bar or line charts in this carousel, the story does not need one.
- Mobile-readability notes: Minimum body text size roughly 34 to 40 px, headline text 80 to 110 px depending on line length, all critical text kept inside a safe margin of at least 90 px from every edge so nothing is clipped in the profile-grid crop.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background gives strong contrast throughout. Gold accent used only for emphasis and highlight boxes, never as body text colour on navy, since gold-on-navy body text would reduce legibility at small sizes.
- Animation notes: Static carousel. No motion used, since every slide's point lands clearly as a still image and the receipt motif already carries continuity without needing movement.
