# Someone Is Paying the Interest on Your "Zero-Cost" EMI

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost / no-cost EMI at checkout
- Central idea: "No-cost" EMI is not interest-free. The interest is still charged, a seller discount offsets it, and GST, fees and the habit of thinking in EMIs can still cost you.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried viewers aged 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances on card EMI during festive sales
- Audience tension: It feels free, but who is paying for it?
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (decision rule) and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: Festive-sale timing, a 3-point checklist worth saving, and a friend who always buys on EMI to send it to.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Where does the interest go on a "no-cost" EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost" EMI is not always a free decision.
3. Relatable-pain hook: ₹5,221 a month feels small. That is the trick.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Someone is paying the interest on your "zero-cost" EMI.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Check these lines before you tap "No-cost EMI".

**Selected hook:** Someone is paying the interest on your "zero-cost" EMI.

**Why it wins:** It is specific, a little sceptical, and makes the viewer want to know who that someone is.
