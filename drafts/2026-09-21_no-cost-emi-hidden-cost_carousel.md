# Zero-Cost EMI Is Not Free EMI

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost ("no cost") EMI on retail purchases such as phones and appliances.
- Central idea: Zero-cost EMI does not remove the interest. It renames it and moves it into the price, a forgone discount, or a fee.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals aged 21-40 who buy phones, appliances or gadgets on EMI, often for the first time.
- Audience tension: The checkout screen makes "no cost" sound like a settled fact, so most buyers never check whether it actually is one.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (so people can re-check it before their next EMI purchase) and shares (so people send it to a friend about to buy something on EMI).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes something they have personally clicked on without reading, gives them a concrete decision rule, and invites them to admit whether they checked or not.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Zero-cost EMI has a cost. You just never see the bill for it."
2. Contrarian hook: "‘Zero-cost EMI’ is not free EMI."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You checked the EMI amount. You never checked the price."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Banks cannot lend money at zero percent. So where does the interest go?"
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "That ‘No Cost EMI’ badge is doing more work than you think."

**Selected hook:** "‘Zero-cost EMI’ is not free EMI."

**Why it wins:** It is a direct contrarian correction of a phrase the reader has personally clicked on, which creates instant tension without needing an image or a number to land in under one second.

## Caption

You tap "No Cost EMI" because the checkout screen makes it sound settled. It rarely is. Banks are not set up to lend money for nothing, so the interest a zero-cost plan appears to erase usually shows up somewhere else on the receipt: a discount that quietly disappears once you choose EMI, a processing fee for the conversion, or GST charged on the interest the bank still works out internally.

None of this makes no-cost EMI a scam. Sometimes the math genuinely works in your favour, especially when a brand is absorbing the cost to move stock. The problem is that most buyers never check, because the label does the deciding for them.

The fix is boring and effective: before you tap that button, check the cash price on its own, ask if a discount applies without EMI, and add any processing fee to the total. Compare that number to the EMI total, not to the monthly figure.

Have you ever checked the cash price after seeing "No Cost EMI" at checkout, or did the label decide for you?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2. Warning red #D94B45 and muted green #4B8B72 are not needed for this topic and are unused.
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display, or the nearest available serif fallback) for headlines and the Slide 7 pull-quote. Clean sans serif (DM Sans, or the nearest available sans fallback) for body copy, labels, the receipt motif, source notes and the 0X/09 slide counter.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No real app screenshots or bank interfaces are used, all visuals are code-drawn typographic and diagram elements.
- Chart and data guidance: Only Slide 4's two-box money-flow diagram and Slide 5's comparison card carry any data-adjacent visual, both are illustrative and carry no invented statistic.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay large (80-120 px range for the biggest claims), body copy stays within the 30-40 px range, nothing sits closer than the safe margin to any edge, all copy is legible at typical phone-screen size without zooming.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background keeps contrast high throughout, gold is used only as an accent, never as body-text colour, so no low-contrast text exists on the carousel.
- Animation notes: Fully static carousel. No motion is used because the mechanism explanation (Slide 4) and the comparison (Slide 5) are both clear as static images, and a static carousel keeps production reliable for this routine.
