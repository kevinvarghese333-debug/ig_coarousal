# The Rule Changed. The App Didn't.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How instant loan apps make small, short-tenure borrowing feel effortless, even after RBI forced full cost disclosure into the process.
- Central idea: RBI now forces every lending app to publish the real cost of a loan in one document, the Key Fact Statement. The app's own design still makes sure the borrower never really stops to read it.
- Primary audience: Salaried professionals and first-time borrowers, 21 to 40, who have seen or used an instant loan app for a small, short-tenure loan.
- Audience tension: "This felt too easy, but the rules say I was protected. Was I?"
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (practical decision rule) and shares (a hidden mechanism worth sending to a friend who uses these apps).
- Why people may save, share or comment: The Key Fact Statement / APR rule and the lender-behind-the-app structure are genuinely useful, specific facts most borrowers do not know. The closing question invites people to name their own experience.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: RBI changed the rules on loan apps in 2025. Almost nobody felt it.
2. Contrarian hook: The loan app followed every RBI rule. You still didn't feel the risk.
3. Relatable-pain hook: ₹8,000 in 90 seconds. That is exactly how it was designed.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: The app is not your lender. It never was.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: The paperwork changed. The 90 seconds didn't.

**Selected hook:** The rule changed. The app didn't.

**Why it wins:** It compresses the entire central idea into six words, is contrarian enough to stop a scroll, and sets up direct curiosity about what rule and what didn't change.

## Caption

Loan apps did not get sneakier. Some of them got more regulated. Since May 2025, RBI requires every digital lender to hand you a Key Fact Statement, one document with the real annualised cost of your loan, before you accept. Most borrowers have never opened one.

Here is the part fewer people know: the app on your phone is often not the actual lender. It is a Lending Service Provider working for a bank or NBFC that RBI actually regulates. The rule was built to make that relationship, and the real cost, impossible to miss.

None of that changes the interface. One button. One pre-filled amount. A cooling-off window you are never reminded exists. The paperwork got honest. The design stayed exactly as easy as before.

This is not a reason to panic about every lending app. It is a reason to spend the same 90 seconds reading the number that you spent accepting the loan.

Which loan app screen made borrowing feel too easy for you?
