# You Paid On Time. You Are Still Paying Interest.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Why paying only the minimum amount due, on time, still costs a
  cardholder the interest-free period.
- Central idea: Paying only the minimum due, on time, does not protect
  the interest-free period. It ends it, for the unpaid balance and for
  every new purchase made that cycle, because interest-free credit is a
  reward for paying in full, not for meeting the due date.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals, 21 to 40, who hold at
  least one credit card and sometimes pay only the minimum due, believing
  "on time" is the only thing that matters.
- Audience tension: Most cardholders track the due date closely and the
  amount paid loosely. This carousel breaks that habit by showing the
  amount paid is what actually protects them.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares (people forward this to a
  parent, sibling or friend who "always just pays the minimum") plus
  comments from people surprised by their own past bills.
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes a habit many people
  think is safe and low-effort ("I never miss a due date") as the exact
  habit quietly costing them money. That gap between belief and mechanism
  is what makes people send it to someone else.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "You paid your credit card bill on time. You are still
   paying interest."
2. Contrarian hook: "Paying the minimum due on time is not the same as
   paying on time."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "Your bill said ₹2,100. Next month's interest said
   otherwise."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The interest-free period is a reward.
   Most cardholders don't know how easily they lose it."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "One habit turns a credit card bill into a loan
   you never applied for."

**Selected hook:** "You paid your credit card bill on time. You are still
paying interest."

**Why it wins:** It creates an immediate contradiction the viewer must
swipe to resolve, and it is phrased entirely in second person, so it reads
as a claim about the viewer's own bill, not a general finance fact.

## Caption

Most people track one number on their credit card bill: the due date.
Miss it, and you expect trouble. Meet it, and you assume you're safe.

But the due date only tells you when to pay. It says nothing about how
much. Pay only the minimum, right on time, and the interest-free period on
that bill doesn't pause. It ends, for the balance you didn't clear and for
every new purchase you make until you pay the full amount again.

That's the part that catches people off guard. Not a missed payment. A
"safe" one that was never actually full.

This isn't about panicking over every minimum-due payment. Sometimes it's
the only option, and that's fine, if you treat what's left like the loan
it has become, not a bill you already handled.

Before your next statement lands, check one thing: are you paying the
total amount due, or just the minimum? What's stopped you from clearing
the full bill when you could?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all nine slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white
  #F6F1E7 for primary text and light cards, secondary slate #AEB7C2 for
  supporting text, warning red #D94B45 used only on Slide 5's partial-
  payment card, muted green not needed for this topic.
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display, or the nearest available
  serif) for headline moments; clean sans serif (DM Sans, or the nearest
  available sans) for body copy, labels, source notes and the slide-
  number marker. No more than these two typefaces anywhere in the set.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. No bank interface, app screen
  or statement screenshot is used or fabricated; all visuals are code-
  rendered (receipt card, timeline bars, phone-frame outline).
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5's timeline bars and Slide 6's
  oversized percentage are the only data visuals; each carries exactly
  one highlighted idea, no combined or multi-metric charts.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay large (70 to 110 px
  depending on line length), body copy stays at 34 to 42 px, nothing
  smaller than 26 px carries required meaning, all text sits inside a
  minimum 90 px safe margin from every edge.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast (off-white or gold text on
  navy background throughout), avoid colour as the only signal (Slide 5's
  red card is also labelled "Partial payment" in text, not colour alone).
- Animation notes: None used. The carousel is fully static; motion was
  not required to make any slide's point clear.
