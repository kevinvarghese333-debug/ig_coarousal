# You Paid On Time. The Bank Still Won.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Paying the credit-card minimum due on time and still losing money to interest.
- Central idea: Paying the minimum due by the due date keeps your credit score clean, but it does not stop interest. Interest is charged on your entire outstanding balance from the date you spent, not just on the part you did not pay.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals, 21 to 40, who carry at least one credit card and have paid "minimum due" at some point without knowing what it actually protects.
- Audience tension: "I paid on time, my score is fine, so I assumed I was fine" versus the reality that interest is already running.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares (this is a decision-rule carousel people send to a friend who "always pays the minimum").
- Why people may save, share or comment: It corrects a specific, common belief in a way that feels personally useful; it names a real cost people didn't know they were paying; it ends with a concrete rule, which is inherently save-worthy.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "You paid your credit card bill on time. The bank still won."
2. Contrarian hook: "On-time payment does not mean interest-free."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You paid something. Next month's bill is somehow bigger."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Minimum due does not exist to help you."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "Paying ₹2,000 on a ₹40,000 bill feels responsible. It is not enough."

**Selected hook:** "You paid on time. The bank still won."

**Why it wins:** It is short, personal, uses "you," and creates immediate tension between an action the reader has taken (paying on time) and an unexpected outcome (the bank winning), without giving away the mechanism.

## Caption

You paid the minimum due before the deadline. No late fee, no red flag on your score. So it feels handled.

Here is the part most people never check: once you do not clear the full statement amount, the interest-free period disappears. Interest gets charged on your entire outstanding balance, counted from the day you made each purchase, not from the day you missed the full payment. Your score stays clean. Your money does not.

This is not a reason to panic about every card swipe. It is a reason to know what "minimum due" actually buys you: a clean score, not a clean bill. The two are not the same thing, and banks are not in a hurry to explain the difference.

If you can clear the full amount within a short window after the due date, the interest bill shrinks fast. If you cannot, it is worth knowing that going in, not finding out from next month's statement.

- Image or screenshot requirements: None. This carousel uses only code-rendered typography and diagrams; no external screenshots or logos were needed or used.
- Chart and data guidance: One comparison bar chart on Slide 6 only (credit card vs typical personal loan, approximate ranges, clearly labelled and sourced). One simple timeline diagram on Slide 4. No pie charts. Highlight exactly one figure per data slide.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80 to 120px, subheads 38 to 52px, body 30 to 40px, source notes kept small but never below legible size on a phone screen. All key text stays inside a safe margin away from the outer edge and away from the profile-grid crop zone.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain strong contrast (off-white or gold text on navy background throughout). Do not rely on colour alone to convey meaning; the red/green accents on Slides 2 and 6 are paired with explicit words (checkmark plus "on time," bar plus label).
- Animation notes: None used. Carousel is fully static; the mastermind file allows this and nothing here required motion to be understood.
