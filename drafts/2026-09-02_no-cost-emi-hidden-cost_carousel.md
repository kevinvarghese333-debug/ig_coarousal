# No-Cost EMI Has A Cost. It Just Moves.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No-cost EMI" / "zero-cost EMI" checkout offers on phones, electronics and other consumer purchases.
- Central idea: The cost of a "no-cost EMI" purchase does not disappear. It moves, usually out of the cash discount you would have gotten, and sometimes into a fee.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals aged 21 to 40 who buy phones, appliances and furniture on EMI, often through e-commerce checkout screens.
- Audience tension: They feel smart for using "no cost" EMI, but have never actually compared it to the cash price.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (it is a decision checklist people will want before their next purchase) and comments (inviting a real judgement call).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It reframes a checkout moment they hit often, gives them a concrete comparison to make next time, and includes a regulator's own scepticism about the term, which makes it forwardable to a friend mid-purchase.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "No-cost EMI has a cost. It just moves."
2. Contrarian hook: "Zero percent interest does not exist. RBI said so in 2013."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You paid every EMI on time. You still paid more than the price tag."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The store is not losing money on your 'no cost' EMI."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "That 'no cost' badge at checkout is doing quiet math on you."

**Selected hook:** "No-cost EMI has a cost. It just moves."

**Why it wins:** It states the whole central idea in six words, needs no context to land, and opens a loop (where does it move to?) that the next eight slides exist to close.

## Caption

That "no cost EMI" badge at checkout is not a discount. It is a different way of collecting the same cost.

Most no-cost EMI offers work by quietly withdrawing the cash discount you would have gotten for paying upfront, and sometimes adding a processing fee on top. RBI flagged this back in 2013, saying the very idea of zero percent interest does not exist in these schemes. The offer is not illegal. It is just named better than it works.

The fix is not avoiding EMI. It is comparing the right number. Not the monthly instalment. The total amount you actually pay, cash price versus EMI price, before you tap confirm.

Save this before your next big purchase and run the two totals side by side.

Would you still choose EMI after checking the total, or does the discount change your decision?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 used sparingly if at all (this topic is a mistake, not a scam, so red is optional), muted green #4B8B72 not needed here.
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display, or Liberation Serif as the local fallback) for headlines and hero numerals; clean sans serif (DM Sans, or DejaVu Sans / Liberation Sans as the local fallback) for body copy, labels, checklist items and the slide-number marker. Maximum two typefaces, as specified.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None required. This carousel uses only code-rendered typography and simple geometric motifs (price-tag cards, receipt lines), no screenshots or external images needed.
- Chart and data guidance: No chart needed. Slide 5 and Slide 7 use simple two-value comparison blocks (cash total vs EMI total, monthly figure vs total figure) rather than a chart, since a single highlighted contrast is clearer at this data scale than a bar or line chart would be.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80 to 120px, subhead 38 to 52px, body 30 to 40px, per the mastermind defaults. Keep all text at least 80px from every edge (safe margin) so nothing is lost in the profile-grid crop.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast (off-white or gold text on navy background throughout). Do not rely on colour alone to distinguish the two comparison cards on Slides 2 and 5, use position and labels as well.
- Animation notes: Entire carousel is static by design. Motion was not used because every slide's point lands clearly as a still frame and the topic does not need a demonstrated interaction (unlike, for example, a phone-screen tap sequence).
