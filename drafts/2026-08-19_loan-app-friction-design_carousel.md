# A ₹5,000 Loan Is Not Small. That Feeling Is the Product.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: How instant loan apps engineer borrowing to feel small and harmless
- Central idea: Loan apps are designed to remove every moment of friction that would normally make a borrower pause, and that engineered ease is exactly what pulls people into repeat borrowing.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals aged 21 to 40 who have seen a "pre-approved loan" notification on their phone, and first-time borrowers who use instant credit apps for small purchases or cash-flow gaps.
- Audience tension: A small loan amount feels harmless in the moment, but the design choices that make it feel harmless (pre-filled amounts, one-tap disbursal, buried APR) are the same choices that make re-borrowing easy.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments. The checklist slide (8) is built to be saved before someone accepts a loan offer, and the closing question is built to draw real experiences into the comments.
- Why people may save, share or comment: The RBI directory and Key Fact Statement checklist is genuinely useful before accepting any loan app offer. The "600 unauthorised apps" fact is shareable because it reframes a common, dismissed habit (tapping accept on a loan app) as a real risk. The closing question invites people to admit a habit many are quietly embarrassed by.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "The loan app was never designed to feel like debt."
2. Contrarian hook: "A ₹5,000 loan is not small. That feeling is the product."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You didn't apply for a loan. Your phone offered you one."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Why the loan app makes borrowing feel like nothing."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "RBI found 600 illegal loan apps. Yours might not be one of the safe ones."

**Selected hook:** A ₹5,000 loan is not small. That feeling is the product.

**Why it wins:** It is contrarian without being alarmist, states the entire carousel's argument in one line, and creates immediate tension by turning a familiar, dismissed feeling into something worth questioning.

## Caption

Every festive season, loan-app downloads spike. Not because more people suddenly need money, but because borrowing has been made to feel like nothing.

In 2021, RBI's own working group counted close to 1,100 lending apps live on Indian app stores. A large share of them could not be clearly traced to any registered bank or NBFC. That gap is not a small technical detail. It is the reason RBI has spent the years since building rules: a mandatory Key Fact Statement before you borrow, a cooling-off period to exit without penalty, and a public directory to check who is actually behind an app.

None of that protects you if you never open it. The apps are fast because slow used to be the thing that made people ask better questions.

This is not a call to avoid credit. It is a call to notice when convenience has quietly replaced consent.

Have you ever taken a loan from an app and later struggled to name the actual lender behind it? Tell me what happened.

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2, warning red #D94B45 used only on Slide 5's "unauthorised" numeral, muted green not used in this carousel.
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display, fallback Lora) for headlines and big numerals. Clean sans serif (DM Sans, fallback Work Sans) for body copy, labels, source notes and slide-count markers. No more than two typefaces used.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real app screenshots or bank interfaces are used or fabricated. All notification cards and lists are original code-rendered shapes, not copies of any real product UI.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5 uses two large sourced numerals instead of a chart, since the comparison is a single before/after count, not a trend. Slide 6 uses a simple three-card accumulation instead of a bar chart, since the point is escalation, not precise magnitude.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes range 76 to 118px, body copy 32 to 40px, fine print 26 to 28px, tested against safe margins of at least 90px on every edge.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white text on navy background maintains strong contrast throughout. Red is used only once, on Slide 5, and never as the sole carrier of meaning (it pairs with the label "unauthorised").
- Animation notes: Carousel is static by default per the design mastermind. No slide requires motion to be understood.
