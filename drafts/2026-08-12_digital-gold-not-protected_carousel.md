# The ₹100 Digital Gold Button Is Not What You Think

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Digital gold bought in small amounts through UPI apps (Paytm,
  PhonePe, Google Pay and similar).
- Central idea: The ₹100 digital-gold button feels like a safe, regulated
  way to save. SEBI has now said, on record, that it is neither SEBI-
  regulated nor RBI-regulated, and carries risks most buyers do not know
  they are taking.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals aged 21-40 who buy small
  amounts of digital gold on festivals, salary day or as a savings habit,
  inside an app they already trust for payments.
- Audience tension: The interface looks identical to a UPI payment or a
  bank balance. The protections are not.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments (a question people want to
  answer about their own gold-buying habit, and a fact worth revisiting
  before the next purchase).
- Why people may save, share or comment: Many readers already hold digital
  gold and have never checked what actually protects it. This is a
  genuine "wait, is that true" moment with a fresh, recent regulatory
  hook (SEBI's November 2025 warning), which makes it feel current rather
  than recycled advice.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "Your digital gold has no regulator. SEBI just said so."
2. Contrarian hook: "That gold button in your UPI app is not an investment. It's a private IOU."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You've bought digital gold on payday. Nobody checked what protects it."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "Digital gold looks like a bank product. It isn't built like one."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "SEBI just warned about the ₹100 gold button in your UPI app."

**Selected hook:** "Your digital gold has no regulator. SEBI just said so."

**Why it wins:** It uses a very recent, verifiable regulatory event to
create instant, specific tension, rather than a vague warning, and it
directly targets an app-based habit a large share of the audience already
has.

## Caption

The ₹100 "buy digital gold" button sits right inside the UPI app you use
for rent, groceries and paying friends back. It looks exactly as safe and
regulated as everything else in that app. It isn't. SEBI said in November
2025 that digital gold offerings on platforms like these are not covered
by SEBI or RBI, and can carry real counterparty and operational risk. That
means no mandated disclosure rules, no statutory grievance redressal, and
none of the capital safeguards that apply to a mutual fund, a stock or a
gold ETF. The gold itself is usually held by a private vault operator who
says it is insured and audited, but there is no law requiring anyone to
verify that claim.

None of this means digital gold is a scam or that you should panic-sell
what you already hold. It means the ₹100 tap deserves the same basic
questions you'd ask before any other investment: who holds this, what
protects it, and what happens if something goes wrong.

Do you know who actually holds your digital gold right now?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, all nine slides, portrait 4:5, PNG, RGB.
- Palette: Navy #080C18 background throughout. Gold #C9A84C for the primary
  accent, headline emphasis words, card borders and the recurring
  UPI-card motif. Off-white #F6F1E7 for primary body text. Slate #AEB7C2
  for source notes, slide labels and secondary lines. Warning red #D94B45
  used on the cross-mark icons on slides 4 and 5, signalling the missing
  protections. Muted green is not used in this carousel; the topic is a
  caution, not a resolved-positive story, so the palette stays gold, navy
  and red throughout for tonal consistency.
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display, or DejaVu Serif as the
  local fallback) for headlines and the main claim on every slide. Clean
  sans serif (DM Sans, or DejaVu Sans as the local fallback) for
  supporting lines, checklist items, source notes and the 0X/09 slide
  marker.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No real app screenshots and no
  fabricated SEBI letterhead or logos. The UPI-style card and vault icon
  are simple code-rendered shapes using the palette above, not a copy of
  any specific app's interface.
- Chart and data guidance: No chart used. The UPI digital-gold growth
  figures found during research varied by source and base month, so no
  single precise number is shown on a slide; the carousel relies on the
  regulatory mechanism, which is well corroborated, rather than a
  transaction-volume statistic.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes stay at or above 70px on claim
  slides, body copy at or above 30px, no line exceeds roughly 30
  characters on the largest headline slides.
- Accessibility notes: High contrast maintained throughout (off-white or
  gold text on navy). Cross and check icons carry meaning through shape,
  not colour alone, so the warning slides remain legible without relying
  on red/green distinction.
- Animation notes: Static carousel by default, consistent with the
  mastermind guidance that static performs well and motion should only be
  added when it improves comprehension. No motion is required for this
  topic.
