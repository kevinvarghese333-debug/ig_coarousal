# Zero-cost EMI is not a free decision

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost or no-cost EMI on consumer purchases
- Central idea: A zero-cost EMI is not free; the interest is relabelled as a lost discount or built into the price.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried viewers aged 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances on EMI during sales
- Audience tension: "It says zero interest, so what is the catch?"
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (checklist) and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A three-question check before any EMI purchase, a line to send to a friend, and a personal question about the last EMI they took.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Where does the interest go in a zero-interest EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: Zero-cost EMI is not a free decision.
3. Relatable-pain hook: ₹10,000 a month feels small. Until you add it up.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Someone always pays the interest.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: The "zero" in zero-cost EMI is a label, not a number.

**Selected hook:** Zero-cost EMI is not a free decision.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a belief the viewer holds today, in a phrase they have seen on every sale screen.

## Caption

Zero-cost EMI sounds like a free loan. It is not, because a lender still has to earn something for waiting six months to get paid.

In most no-cost EMI offers, that earning is either taken out of the price you would have paid in cash, or it is already sitting inside the sticker price. Sometimes the bank charges interest and the seller gives a discount of a similar size, so the totals look the same. The numbers on the slides are made up to show the mechanism, not any real product.

What changes is how it feels. A small monthly instalment makes a big purchase easy to say yes to.

This does not mean no-cost EMI is always a bad call. It means the label is not the number to look at. Terms differ by bank, card and seller, so check the current terms before you pay.

When did you last choose an EMI because the monthly number felt small? Tell me what you bought and whether you would do it again.
