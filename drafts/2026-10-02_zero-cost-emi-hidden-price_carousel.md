# Somebody Always Pays for "No-Cost" EMI

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost EMI
- Central idea: "No-cost" EMI is not automatically free; the real test is total paid versus the cash price.
- Primary audience: Indian professionals aged 21 to 40 who buy phones and appliances on EMI
- Audience tension: It feels free, so nobody checks.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves, with comments on the closing question
- Why people may save, share or comment: A three-question checklist, a sendable warning for the EMI-loving friend, and a personal judgement question.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: What does "no-cost" EMI actually cost?
2. Contrarian hook: Zero-cost EMI is rarely zero-cost.
3. Relatable-pain hook: You compared phones. Did you compare prices?
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Somebody always pays for "no-cost" EMI.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: The EMI looked free. The bill was not.

**Selected hook:** Somebody always pays for "no-cost" EMI.

**Why it wins:** It makes a specific, slightly sceptical claim about something the viewer does, and the swipe pays it off.
