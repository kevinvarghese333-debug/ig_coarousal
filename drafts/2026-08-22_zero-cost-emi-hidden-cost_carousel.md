# Zero Cost EMI Is Not Zero Cost

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No Cost EMI" / "Zero Cost EMI" checkout offers on big-ticket purchases.
- Central idea: "No Cost EMI" is not interest-free. The cost usually reappears as a forfeited cash discount or a processing fee, and RBI told banks in 2013 that true zero-percent interest does not exist.
- Primary audience: Indian salaried professionals and first-time borrowers, 21 to 40, mid-purchase on a phone, appliance or gadget.
- Audience tension: they want the convenience of spreading a payment, but suspect the "free" label is hiding something they can't quite name.
- Primary engagement goal: saves (as a pre-checkout checklist) and shares (to whoever in the family repeats "no cost EMI, no harm").
- Why people may save, share or comment: it reframes a phrase they see weekly, gives them a concrete two-number test to run at checkout, and invites them to admit where they got caught by it.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: The zero in "zero cost EMI" has to hide somewhere.
2. Contrarian hook: Zero cost EMI is not zero cost.
3. Relatable-pain hook: That "No Cost EMI" button skipped one thing: the discount.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Someone is paying your EMI's interest. It is you.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: RBI said this in 2013. Checkout screens still hide it.

**Selected hook:** Zero cost EMI is not zero cost.

**Why it wins:** It directly contradicts the exact phrase printed on the checkout button, so it reads as a correction rather than a headline, which stops the scroll and is quotable on its own.

## Caption

Every sale season, checkout offers the same choice: pay in full, or pay in "No Cost EMI". The second option feels like a free upgrade. It usually isn't.

Interest doesn't vanish just because a screen doesn't show it. It gets folded back into the price, usually by removing the cash discount that exists for people who pay upfront. RBI made this point to banks back in 2013: true zero percent interest doesn't really exist, because the cost has to sit somewhere.

This isn't an argument against EMI. Spreading a large, planned purchase over a few months is often a reasonable call. The problem is agreeing to a cost without knowing you're paying one.

Next time "No Cost EMI" shows up at checkout, ask for the upfront price first. Compare both totals before you confirm.

Where has "No Cost EMI" quietly cost you more than paying upfront, and did you catch it before or after you paid?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, RGB/sRGB.
- Palette: Navy `#080C18` background, Gold `#C9A84C` primary accent and numerals, Off-white `#F6F1E7` primary text, Slate `#AEB7C2` secondary/support text, Signal red `#D94B45` used only for the "cost" outcome marker on Slide 8, Muted green `#4B8B72` used only for the "free" outcome marker on Slide 8.
- Typography: Playfair Display Bold for headlines and pull-quotes, Playfair Display Regular for secondary display moments, DM Sans Regular/Medium/Bold for body copy, labels, source notes and slide numbers. Two typefaces only.
- Image or screenshot requirements: None. Every visual is code-rendered (price tag, twin price cards, flow diagram, receipt/checklist card). No real bank interface, no fabricated app screen, no logos.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5's receipt card is the only slide carrying numbers; keep it to three line items plus one highlighted gap figure. Slide 4's flow diagram stays to one source and two destinations, no more.
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80-120px, subhead 38-52px, body 30-40px; keep all text at least 90px from any canvas edge; test at actual phone width before export.
- Accessibility notes: Off-white on navy gives high contrast for body copy; reserve gold for accents and numerals, not full paragraphs, since gold-on-navy is lower contrast for long text; keep line lengths short.
- Animation notes: Static carousel by default per the routine's rule. If a future video cut is made, Slide 4 (flow arrow) and Slide 5 (counting receipt total) are the only two slides where motion would add comprehension.
