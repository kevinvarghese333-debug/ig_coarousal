# No Cost EMI Is Not Free Money

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No cost EMI" checkout buttons and what actually happens behind them.
- Central idea: "No cost EMI" can be priced fairly today because regulation forced transparency, but fair pricing does not mean free money, and the real cost is the spending decision the label makes easier to say yes to.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21-40 who have used no-cost EMI on phones, appliances, flights or big-ticket shopping.
- Audience tension: The label feels like a discount with no downside, but people rarely check what changed on their statement or in the price to make that "zero" possible.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (it is a checklist people will want before their next checkout) and shares (it reframes something everyone has clicked but never questioned).
- Why people may save, share or comment: It names a regulation most people have never heard of, gives a concrete 3-step check before their next EMI checkout, and pokes at a habit (easy EMI clicking) that almost everyone recognises in themselves.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "RBI wrote a rule because of this button."
2. Contrarian hook: "'No cost EMI' still has a cost. It just moved."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You've clicked 'No Cost EMI' without reading past the word 'no'."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The 'zero' in no-cost EMI comes from a discount you gave up."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "Zero interest used to be a disguise. Here's what changed, and what didn't."

**Selected hook:** "RBI wrote a rule because of this button."

**Why it wins:** It is specific, creates instant curiosity about a familiar object (the checkout button), and signals credibility (a regulator responded) without giving away the mechanism yet.

## Caption

That "No Cost EMI" button at checkout feels like a discount with no catch. It usually isn't one.

In most cases, the "0% interest" is built from a discount you would have gotten anyway for paying cash. The lender folds that same amount into the EMI structure and calls it zero cost. RBI noticed this too. Since its 2022 Master Direction on credit and debit cards, issuers can no longer camouflage interest as "zero cost" EMI. They now have to show the principal, interest and discount separately, right on your statement.

That is real progress. It also does not change the other cost: no cost EMI removes the one moment that used to make you pause before a big purchase, the pinch of paying the full price today.

Before your next EMI checkout, look at your last statement. Compare the cash price with the EMI price. Then be honest with yourself.

Would you have bought it if there was no EMI option at all?
