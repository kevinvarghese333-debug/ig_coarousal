# "Zero-cost" EMI can still cost you ₹3,000

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost EMI
- Central idea: "Zero-cost" EMI is not always free, because you may lose the cash discount you would get for paying in full.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances on EMI
- Audience tension: The EMI looks free, so the decision feels risk-free
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A checkout rule to reuse, a number that surprises, and an A/B question

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity: "Where does the ₹3,000 go in a zero-cost EMI?"
2. Contrarian: "Zero-cost EMI is not always a free decision."
3. Relatable-pain: "That easy ₹10,000 a month is not the full price."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism: "Some sellers quietly keep two prices."
5. Warning-or-risk: "Check this before you tap Convert to EMI."

**Selected hook:** "Zero-cost" EMI can still cost you ₹3,000.

**Why it wins:** A familiar phrase plus a specific number creates instant tension.
