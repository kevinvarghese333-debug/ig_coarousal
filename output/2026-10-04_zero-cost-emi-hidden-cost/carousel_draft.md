# Zero-cost EMI: Who Pays for the Zero?

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost EMI
- Central idea: A "no-cost" EMI is still a loan, and the cost usually sits in a lost discount or a fee.
- Primary audience: Indian shoppers aged 21 to 40 using EMI at checkout
- Audience tension: It looks free, so why think about it?
- Primary engagement goal: Saves, with comments on the cash-price versus EMI gap
- Why people may save, share or comment: A simple three-step checkout rule, and a chance to share their own gap.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Who pays for the zero in zero-cost EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: Zero-cost EMI is not always a free decision.
3. Relatable-pain hook: Your EMI looks small. Check the total.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: The discount you lose is the interest you pay.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "No-cost" EMI can still cost you.

**Selected hook:** Someone pays for "zero-cost" EMI. Check if it is you.

**Why it wins:** It names a familiar checkout label and hints that the viewer may be the one paying.

## Caption

"No-cost EMI" is a pricing label, not a law of nature.

A loan always has a cost. When the EMI looks free, that cost usually sits somewhere else: a cash discount you give up by choosing EMI, a processing fee, or other charges that the bank or merchant applies.

The simple check is to compare two numbers: the price if you pay in full today, and the total you will pay across all the instalments. If the second number is bigger, you are borrowing, whatever the banner says.

The example in this carousel uses illustrative numbers to show the arithmetic. Real offers differ by merchant, card and lender, so check the current terms on your own checkout screen and card statement.

There is also a quieter cost. A small monthly amount makes a big purchase feel affordable. That is useful for a merchant. It is not always useful for you.

What is the biggest gap you have seen between the cash price and the EMI total? Tell me in the comments.
