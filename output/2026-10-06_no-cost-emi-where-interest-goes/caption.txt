Festive sale season means one banner will follow you everywhere: No-cost EMI.

Here is the part worth pausing on. A lender is still being paid for waiting six months. That money has to come from somewhere. Sometimes the seller or brand absorbs it. Sometimes the cash discount you would have got by paying in full quietly goes away. Either way, "no-cost" describes the EMI line, not the whole purchase.

The numbers in this carousel are hypothetical, built only to show the mechanism. Your actual offer will differ, and terms vary by lender, card and seller. So the useful habit is simple: ask for the cash price first, compare it with the total on the EMI, and read the processing fee and GST lines before you confirm.

None of this means EMIs are bad. A planned EMI can be a perfectly reasonable tool. The problem starts when a free-sounding label stops you from asking the question.

When you last bought something on EMI, did you check the cash price first?
