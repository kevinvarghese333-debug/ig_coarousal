You do not need to miss a due date to lose money on a credit card. Most people never miss one. What actually decides whether you pay interest is a single line most people scroll past: Total Amount Due versus Minimum Amount Due.

Pay only the minimum, on time, every month, and your bank stops giving you the interest-free period on the entire bill. Interest starts running from the date you spent the money, not from the date you missed a payment, because you never missed one.

This is not about being careless. It is about a bill designed with two numbers, only one of which actually protects you. The gap between them is where the balance quietly grows even while your credit report looks clean.

Check your last statement. Find the line that says Total Amount Due. If what you paid was less than that number, the difference is already earning interest.

Did you know the difference between these two numbers before today, or did you find out the hard way?
