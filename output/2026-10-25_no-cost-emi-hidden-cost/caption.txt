"No-cost EMI" sounds like the lender is doing you a favour. Lenders do not work like that.

The interest still exists. What changes is where it sits. Sometimes the seller absorbs it and recovers it through pricing or margin. Sometimes the cash discount you would have got disappears the moment you choose EMI. And depending on the lender, a processing fee or tax on it can show up separately. Terms differ, so the only reliable check is the full amount you will pay.

There is a quieter cost too. A small monthly number makes a big purchase feel easy, and we stop asking whether we would buy it at all if the EMI option did not exist.

None of this means EMI is bad. It means "zero" is a pricing label, not a guarantee of zero cost. The numbers in this carousel are illustrative, so check the offer in front of you.

Question for you: when you last used a no-cost EMI, did you compare it with the pay-in-full price first?
