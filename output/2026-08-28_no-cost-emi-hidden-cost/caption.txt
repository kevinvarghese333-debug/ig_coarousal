"No Cost EMI" sounds like the bank did you a favour. In 2013, RBI told banks plainly that zero percent interest does not exist, and that the "waived" interest on these schemes was usually recovered from the customer another way, often through the processing fee instead of a visible interest line.

The label has since changed from "Zero Percent EMI" to "No Cost EMI," but the underlying arithmetic has not moved. Most of the time, choosing EMI over cash means giving up a discount you would have received for paying upfront. The store's price does not change. Your price does.

This is not a case against EMI. Sometimes spreading a payment is the right call for your cash flow. It is a case against believing "no cost" means no cost. Before your next big-ticket purchase, ask for the cash price and compare it yourself.

Have you ever checked the cash price against the EMI price on the same purchase, and were the numbers actually different?
