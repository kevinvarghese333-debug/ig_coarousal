Every checkout screen has a version of this button now: pay a little more later, but pay "no cost" for the privilege of splitting it up. It sounds like a workaround. Mostly, it is a rename.

RBI told banks back in 2013 that zero percent interest is not a real thing; the cost shows up somewhere, usually the price you would have paid anyway or a discount that quietly disappears when you switch from "pay now" to "pay later." The lender is not doing you a favour. It is just choosing where to put the number.

None of this means EMI is always wrong. Spreading a genuine, necessary expense can be a sensible cash-flow decision. The problem is choosing it because the label says "free," not because you checked the total.

Next time you see that button, do the two-line maths before you tap it.

Have you ever compared the upfront price against the total no-cost EMI payout, and were they actually the same?
