"No-cost" EMI sounds like the lender is working for free. It is not.

In most no-cost EMI offers, interest is still calculated by the lender. The seller or brand then gives an upfront discount of about the same size, so the interest line shows as zero. That is why the phone price on the EMI can look the same as the price on the box.

What often remains is everything around the interest: GST on the interest component, a processing fee on the conversion, and in many cases no reward points on the EMI purchase. The ₹470 on the bill slide is an illustration with assumed numbers, not a quote from any bank. Your terms will differ, so check the current terms before you convert.

The useful habit is simple. Look for the cash price, read the EMI breakdown screen, and check what the statement actually shows after the purchase.
