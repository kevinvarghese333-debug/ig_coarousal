Kevin here. Every month, thousands of card users do everything right. They check the due date. They pay the full amount before it is due. They feel responsible about it.

Then one purchase gets converted into EMI, maybe from a bank SMS, maybe from a checkout screen offer, and the math quietly changes. Interest returns. A processing fee shows up. GST sits on top of both. None of it looks like a penalty. It just looks like a slightly bigger bill that still gets paid on time.

Your credit score will not catch this. Your on-time-payment streak will not catch this either. Only checking the interest rate and the processing fee before you tap "convert" will.

This is not a case against EMI. Spreading a large expense sometimes makes sense. It is a case for reading the fine print before your thumb decides for you.

Have you ever converted a credit card bill to EMI and later wondered where the extra money went?
