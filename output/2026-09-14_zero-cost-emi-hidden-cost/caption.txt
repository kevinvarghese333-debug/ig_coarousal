Every festive sale season, checkout asks the same question: pay full price today, or split it with "No Cost EMI," interest showing zero. The second option feels like a favour. It usually is not.

Zero percent interest is not really a lending product in India. What looks free is typically a seller's discount, moved upfront and repackaged as "no" interest, while a processing fee can still sit on the invoice, and GST can apply to that fee and the interest portion.

The expensive moment is rarely day one. It tends to be the day you return the product, close the EMI early, or miss a due date, because that is usually when the "free" part quietly disappears from your refund or your bill.

None of this makes EMI a bad tool. It means "No Cost" is doing a lot of marketing work for a normal pricing decision.

Before your next "No Cost EMI" tap: have you ever actually checked what the cash price was?
