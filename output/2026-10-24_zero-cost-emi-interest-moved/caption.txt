That "No-cost EMI" tag at checkout is easy to misread.

The bank still lends you the money, and a lender does not work for free. What usually happens is that the seller gives an upfront discount that offsets the interest, so the total looks the same as the sticker price.

In the example on the slides, the discount cancels ₹1,326 of interest on a ₹30,000 phone. But GST on that interest and any processing fee can still reach you. Terms vary by bank, card and seller, so check the EMI screen before you confirm.

The cost I worry about more is behavioural. ₹5,221 a month feels small, so a ₹30,000 purchase gets approved faster than ₹30,000 in one go would.

None of this means EMI is bad. It means "no-cost" is a label, not a calculation. Read the lines on the screen.
