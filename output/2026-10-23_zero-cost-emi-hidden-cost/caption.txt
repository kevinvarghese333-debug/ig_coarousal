Zero-cost EMI sounds like a free loan. Often it is a pricing decision made before you reach the payment screen.

The example in this carousel is illustrative, not a real quote. Say a phone is ₹60,000 on a six-month "no-cost" EMI, but a seller might offer it for ₹57,000 if you pay in full. The ₹3,000 gap is the cost, even though no line says "interest". On the money you actually used, that works out to roughly 18% a year.

Terms differ by seller, bank and card, and fees or taxes may also apply. So the useful habit is simple: before converting a purchase to EMI, ask for the pay-in-full price and look for the interest and discount lines in the current terms.

The bigger effect is behavioural. A ₹60,000 phone starts to feel like a ₹10,000 phone, and that makes yes easier.

This is education, not advice for your situation.
