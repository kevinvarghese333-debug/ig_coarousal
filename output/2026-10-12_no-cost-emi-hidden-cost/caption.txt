"No-cost EMI" sounds like the shop is being generous. Usually, it is a pricing structure.

A lender or card issuer has to be paid for waiting six months. That cost can show up as interest, as a processing fee, or as a cash discount you no longer get. The label changes. The arithmetic does not.

In the example on these slides, the same phone is ₹29,000 in cash and ₹30,000 over six EMIs. The ₹1,000 gap looks small. Spread over six months on a falling balance, it works out to roughly 12 percent a year. These are illustrative numbers, not a real offer. Real prices, fees and terms differ by seller and lender, so read the checkout breakup before you tap yes.

None of this means EMIs are bad. A spread-out payment can be sensible when you know what it costs. The risk is when "can I afford it?" quietly becomes "can I afford this month?"
