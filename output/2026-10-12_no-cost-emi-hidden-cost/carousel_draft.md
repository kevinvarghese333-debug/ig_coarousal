# "No-cost" EMI Is Not Free. Someone Is Still Paid.

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost EMI
- Central idea: A "no-cost" EMI still has a cost, paid as interest, fees or a lost cash discount, and it changes the question you ask.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21 to 40 buying phones and gadgets on EMI
- Audience tension: It says no-cost, so it feels safe to say yes.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A three-question checkout checklist, a number that surprises, and a real either/or choice.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: What "no-cost" EMI actually costs.
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost" EMI is not free.
3. Relatable-pain hook: It says no-cost. You tap yes.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Someone is paid for waiting six months.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: The EMI is small. The question you skipped is not.

**Selected hook:** "No-cost" EMI is not free. Someone is still paid.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a belief the viewer holds at every checkout, with an open loop on who gets paid.
