## Claims used on-slide and their support

| Slide | Claim | Support | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | RBI requires the minimum due to be structured so it covers interest and fees before principal, avoiding negative amortisation | RBI Master Direction, Credit Card and Debit Card Issuance and Conduct Directions, 2022 (dated 2022-04-21, effective 2022-07-01), amended 2024-03-07 | Corroborated by two independent legal/regulatory summaries; recommend a direct rbi.org.in check before publishing, see [VERIFY] below |
| 6 | Carrying a balance forward causes most Indian card issuers to withdraw the interest-free/grace period on new purchases | Consistent across multiple independent Indian bank and fintech explainers (BankBazaar, Axis Bank, InvestingPro) | Industry-wide pattern, not attributed to a single named issuer |
| 5 | Illustrative example (₹40,000 statement, ₹2,000 minimum due, ₹38,000 carried forward and accruing interest) | Hypothetical, explicitly labelled on-slide as "an illustrative example, not a specific card" | Not a real card's data; used only to demonstrate the mechanism |

## No specific numbers stated as universal fact

- No annualised interest rate or APR is stated on any slide (rates vary by issuer and change over time).
- No specific number of interest-free/grace-period days is stated on any slide (commonly cited range is roughly 20 to 50 days, kept in research notes only).
- No bank, NBFC, or card product is named.
