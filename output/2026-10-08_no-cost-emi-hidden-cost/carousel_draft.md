# "No-cost" EMI is not a free decision

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: "No-cost" or "zero-cost" EMI offers at festive-sale checkout
- Central idea: A "no-cost" EMI usually moves the interest somewhere else, so the real question is whether the pay-in-full price is lower.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances during festive sales
- Audience tension: The monthly number feels easy, so the total price stops being examined.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (checkout checklist) and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: It is a checkout-time rule, it names a friend's favourite line ("it is only ₹10,000 a month"), and the closing question asks for a real judgement.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Who pays for "no-cost" EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost" EMI is not a free decision.
3. Relatable-pain hook: It is only ₹10,000 a month. Until it is not.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Where the interest goes when the screen says zero.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Before you tap "No-cost EMI", ask one question.

**Selected hook:** "No-cost" EMI is not a free decision.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a belief the viewer holds at the exact moment of a festive-sale purchase, in seven words.
