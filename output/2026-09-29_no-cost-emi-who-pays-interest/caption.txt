"No-cost EMI" sounds like a rare thing: a loan with no interest. In practice, lenders still need to be paid for the money they advance.

So the cost usually goes somewhere else. It can sit inside the product price, in a cash discount you stop getting, or in charges like a processing fee and GST on the interest portion. Not every lender or seller does all of this, and terms change, so the only reliable check is the actual checkout and card statement.

The slides use a made-up ₹60,000 phone to show the mechanism. Your numbers will differ.

The part I find more interesting is behaviour. A small monthly number shortens the pause before we buy. That pause is often the cheapest financial protection we have.

None of this means EMI is always wrong. It means the "zero" on the screen is not the full answer.

Before you tap EMI, ask what the price is if you pay in full today.
