# Sources and Fact Check: Credit Card Minimum Due Trap

Full research trail is in `research_notes/2026-08-20_credit-card-minimum-due-trap_research.md`. This file is the condensed, on-slide fact check.

## Claims used on slides, and their grounding

| Slide | Claim | Grounding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Paying only Minimum Due keeps the account "current" but loses the interest-free period; interest can apply from the transaction date | RBI Master Direction, Credit Card and Debit Card, Issuance and Conduct (2022, amended March 2024), corroborated via multiple 2025 to 2026 secondary sources | [VERIFY] against primary RBI text, not fetchable in this session |
| 4, 5 | Minimum Amount Due is usually around 5% of the outstanding balance | RBI-aligned MAD formula reported effective December 1, 2022 (higher of 100% interest/fees/taxes or 5% of TAD, plus past-due/over-limit/EMI components), simplified for on-slide clarity | [VERIFY] against primary RBI text or current issuer T&Cs |
| 5 | Card interest is often 3% to 3.75% a month, depending on the card | Converges across 2026 secondary sources (Forbes Advisor India, Bajaj Finserv Markets, Fincash, Axis Bank, Airtel), issuer-specific and variable | [VERIFY] against 2 to 3 current issuer rate cards near publish date |
| 7 | Minimum due payments keep the account marked current; the durable risk is rising credit utilisation, not an immediate score drop | Consistent across secondary consumer-finance sources on CIBIL impact | [VERIFY] against CIBIL or another bureau's own consumer page |

## Claims deliberately excluded from on-slide copy

- No specific figure for how much revenue card issuers earn from revolving interest. Slide 6 uses qualitative language ("one of the more reliable ways card issuers earn revenue") instead of a number, since no current, verified, general figure was found.
- No mention of the RBI's separate 3-day grace period before late payment is reported to bureaus. That is a real but distinct protection (about lateness, not partial payment) and was left out to keep the carousel to one idea.
- No claim more specific than "usually around 5%" for the minimum due percentage, since the real formula has several components and varies by cardholder circumstance.

## Network access note

Direct fetch of `rbi.org.in` (both the Master Direction page and the consumer FAQ PDF) returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` in this sandboxed session. All regulatory claims above are therefore grounded in independent secondary reporting that describes the same Master Direction, not a direct read of the primary text. This is flagged so Kevin can do a fast primary-source check before publishing rather than treating these as independently confirmed from RBI directly.

## Numbers on Slide 5 and 6

The ₹40,000 / ₹2,000 / ₹38,000 example is explicitly labelled "Illustrative example" on-slide. It is a round-number illustration of the mechanism (5% minimum on a ₹40,000 bill), not a specific card's real statement, and is not sourced to any single issuer or transaction.
