# No-Cost EMI Is Not Free

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Zero-cost / no-cost EMI at checkout
- Central idea: No-cost EMI usually still has interest; the seller's discount often pays it, so the real cost is the cash discount you give up.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians aged 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances on EMI
- Audience tension: "No cost" feels like a free loan, so people stop comparing prices.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves (checkout checklist) and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A three-number rule to use at the next checkout; a real either/or question.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: So who is paying for your no-cost EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: No-cost EMI is not free.
3. Relatable-pain hook: You tapped "No cost EMI" on a phone. Did you check the cash price?
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: Your no-cost EMI is paid for by a discount you never saw.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: The EMI banner is not the price. Check this first.

**Selected hook:** No-cost EMI is not free. So who is paying for it?

**Why it wins:** It states a clear claim a viewer has personally seen, then opens a question that earns the swipe.
