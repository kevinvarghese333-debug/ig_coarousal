Zero-cost EMI sounds like a free loan. It rarely is.

Here is the practical bit. When a seller offers no-cost EMI, the lender usually still charges interest. The seller's discount often absorbs it, which means the cash discount you could have had is quietly gone. Depending on the lender, GST on the interest part, a processing fee or early-closure charges can also appear. Terms differ, so read the EMI breakdown screen, not the banner.

The numbers on the slides are illustrative, not a real offer. RBI flagged a version of this back in 2013, when it told banks that interest in zero-percent schemes was often recovered through processing fees.

EMI is not bad. Sometimes spreading a payment is the right call for your cash flow. The mistake is choosing it because the label said "no cost" instead of comparing totals.
