11 pm. One packet of chips. Delivered in nine minutes. It feels like a small miracle, not a business decision. It is both.

Quick commerce did not build a ten-minute delivery network because speed is where the money is. A dark store costs roughly the same to run whether your order is worth 100 rupees or 500. Rent, staff and inventory do not shrink for a small basket. The number that actually decides whether a platform makes money is average order value, not delivery time.

That is why the more persistent prompt on these apps is not "arriving in 9 minutes." It is "add more to avoid a fee." Speed gets you to open the app. Basket size is what keeps the business open.

None of this makes the convenience fake. It is real, and often worth paying for. But the next time an app nudges you to add one more item, it is worth a two-second pause: would you have bought this anyway?

What is the smallest thing you have added to a cart just to avoid a fee?
