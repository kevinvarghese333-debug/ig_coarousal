# Sources and Fact-Check: The Credit Score Timing Trap

## Claims used on slides

| Slide | Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2, 9 | Paying the full bill by the due date avoids interest charges | Verified | RBI FAQ, Credit Card and Debit Card Issuance and Conduct: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/FAQMDCreditCardandDebitCard.pdf (amendment dated 7 Mar 2024) |
| 4, 5, 8 | The bank reports the outstanding balance as of the statement date to the credit bureau, regardless of later payment | Verified via industry explainers, consistent with billing-cycle reporting structure | Paisabazaar: https://www.paisabazaar.com/credit-score/how-credit-utilization-ratio-works-and-what-percentage-maintain-aio/ ; CardTrail: https://cardtrail.in/basics/credit-utilization-ratio-india-guide/ |

| 7, 8 | RBI has moved bank reporting to credit bureaus from monthly to multiple times a month, effective around mid-2026 | Verified directionally; exact reference-date grid marked [VERIFY] | RBI Master Directions index: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12764 ; Business Standard: https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/rbi-extends-credit-information-reporting-norms-july-1-2026-125120401312_1.html |

## [VERIFY] items not resolved before this production run

- Exact RBI reference-date grid for the new reporting cycle. Sources disagree: one describes 7th/14th/21st/28th/last day, another describes 9th/16th/23rd/last day for NBFCs specifically. On-slide copy avoids stating a specific date list for this reason.
- Whether the 1 July 2026 effective date is uniform across commercial banks, NBFCs and local area banks, or staggered by category.
- Any bureau-published exact factor-weight percentage for utilization vs payment history. Not found from an Indian bureau primary source; excluded from slide copy.

## Claims deliberately excluded from slide copy

- Specific CIBIL/Experian/Equifax scoring-weight percentages (commonly cited "30% utilization, 35% payment history" figures trace to US FICO methodology, not a published Indian-bureau formula).
- Specific bank-by-bank credit card interest rates (vary 30-45% annualized across issuers and change over time).
- Any specific numeric score-point drop from a given utilization level (no reliable public source ties utilization X% to a specific point drop).
