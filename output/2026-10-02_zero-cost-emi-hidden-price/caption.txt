Zero-cost EMI sounds like the one deal where nobody wins and nobody loses. Usually, somebody is paying. It is just not always shown as "interest".

Here is the useful bit. The EMI amount is not the thing to check. The comparison is. What would you have paid in cash today? What fees come with the EMI? What is the total across all instalments?

In the example here, the gap between cash price and EMI total works out to roughly 18% a year. The numbers are illustrative, not a real offer, and real offers vary by seller, lender and card. Some may genuinely cost nothing extra. The only way to know is to compare.

RBI flagged this pattern for card EMI schemes back in 2013, saying interest was often passed on as a processing fee. That is context, not a reason to panic. It is a reason to ask one more question before you tap Pay.

What is the biggest thing you bought on "no-cost" EMI, and did you compare it with the cash price?
