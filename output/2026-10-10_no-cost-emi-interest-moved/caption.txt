"No-cost EMI" sounds like a free pass. Usually it is a pricing structure.

The bank still earns interest on the loan. In many offers, the seller or brand absorbs that interest by giving up a discount, or by building it into the price. Either way, the number on the checkout screen says zero, but the money has moved somewhere else.

Two things are worth checking before you tap. First, whether the cash or upfront price is lower than the EMI price. Second, whether the EMI schedule shows GST on the interest or a processing fee, because those can still land on your card statement. Terms differ by bank, card and seller, so check the current terms for the exact offer in front of you.

The slides use an illustration: a ₹60,000 phone, 6 EMIs of ₹10,000 and an assumed 15% a year. Real offers will differ.
