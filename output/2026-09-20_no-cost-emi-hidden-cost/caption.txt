"No Cost EMI" sits right there at checkout, next to a green tick, telling you this purchase costs nothing extra. Most of the time, that is not quite true.

The mechanism is simple once you see it. Sellers often give a cash discount to people who pay the full price upfront. Choose no-cost EMI instead, and that discount is usually the first thing to go. Its value is what ends up covering the interest the lender still needs to earn. RBI has said for years that "zero percent interest" is not really a product, the cost just moves somewhere else.

None of this means EMI is bad. It means the label is doing more marketing than the maths. Before you tap that button again, check the cash price, check the discount you are giving up, and check any processing fee.

If you had to pay the full amount today, in cash, would you still buy it?

That question is worth more than the "0%" badge.
