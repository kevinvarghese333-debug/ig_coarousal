"No-cost EMI" sounds like the lender is doing you a favour. It is not. Lending money has a cost, and someone always covers it.

In many offers the lender charges interest, then the seller gives a discount that roughly cancels it out. The EMI line shows no interest, so it looks free. The cost has moved into the price you agreed to pay.

The way to see it is simple. Ask for the pay-in-full price, with any cash, UPI or card offer, and compare it with the total you will pay across all EMIs. The gap is what you are paying to spread the bill.

Also check the small print. Fees and GST can sit on top, and the full purchase amount may block your card limit. Terms differ by lender and seller, so read the current terms before you tap.

Sometimes spreading the payment is worth it. Just know the price of that choice.
