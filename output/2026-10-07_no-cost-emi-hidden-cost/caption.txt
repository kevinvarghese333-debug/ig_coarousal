"No-cost EMI" sounds like the shop is doing you a favour. Usually, someone is still being paid for the time you take to pay.

The money can sit in different places. A cash discount you no longer get. A processing fee. GST on a charge. Sometimes the interest is simply folded into the price. Terms differ by card, bank and seller, so the label alone tells you very little.

The example in this carousel is made up to show the mechanism: a ₹57,000 cash price against six payments of ₹10,000. That ₹3,000 gap works out to roughly 18% a year, before any fee or GST. Your own purchase will look different, which is exactly why the check matters.

The simple habit is to ask for the pay-in-full price first, then compare. A smaller monthly number is easy to say yes to. A total is harder.

Next time you see "no-cost EMI" at checkout, do you take the discount or the EMI, and why?
