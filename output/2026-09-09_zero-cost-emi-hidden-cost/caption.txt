You have probably used no-cost EMI. Most people have. It feels like a free upgrade: same product, smaller monthly number, zero interest on the screen.

Here is the part that rarely gets explained. In a lot of these schemes, the "zero" is not free money appearing from nowhere. It is usually the cash discount a full-payment buyer would have received, quietly left out of the EMI price. The RBI has flagged this exact camouflaging since 2013: interest that is not called interest is still interest.

None of this means no-cost EMI is a trap to avoid completely. Sometimes spreading a payment is the right call for your situation. The point is smaller and more useful: check the cash price against the EMI total before you tap confirm. If they match, you have genuinely lost nothing. If they do not, now you know exactly what you paid for the convenience.

Have you ever actually compared the two numbers before choosing EMI, or did you just take the lower monthly figure at face value?
