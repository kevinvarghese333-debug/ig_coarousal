Zero-cost EMI sounds like the bank is doing you a favour. Usually, the cost has just changed address.

A lender still has to earn something for lending the money. That return can sit in a higher price, a cash or card discount you no longer get, or fees on the plan. Which one applies depends on the seller and the lender, so the checkout page matters more than the banner.

The slides use assumed numbers to show the logic. They are not a quote from any store or bank.

There is also a quieter cost. When a large price becomes a small monthly number, the bigger purchase starts to feel reasonable. That is a decision worth making deliberately, not by default.

"Zero" is a claim to check, like any other. Compare the price you would pay today with the total you would pay on the plan, and read the current terms on your own card or loan.

Do you pick the lower monthly number or the lower total price, and what made you choose it?
