# Sources and Fact Check: No-Cost EMI Is Not Free

## Claims used on-slide and their support

1. **"No-cost EMI is not literally free; the cost is reallocated rather than removed."**
   Supported by RBI's stated regulatory position (see #4) and by consistent, independent industry explanations of the subvention mechanism (see #2).

2. **"The retailer or brand pays the bank the interest upfront. It is called a subvention."** (Slide 4)
   Corroborated by multiple independent industry/payments sources: Innoviti ("No Cost EMI for Merchants: A Complete Guide to Boosting Retail Sales"), Paytm Business documentation ("EMI Subvention / No Cost EMI"), Moneyview ("No Cost EMI Means, What is No Cost EMI, How It Works"), and Zwitch ("What Is Meant By No Cost EMI"). These sources are independent of one another and describe the same mechanism, which supports treating it as reliable industry-standard information.

3. **"Say a store offers 3% off for paying in full. Choose 0% EMI instead, and that discount often quietly disappears."** (Slide 5)
   This is an explicitly hypothetical, illustrative worked example, not a claim about a real retailer or product. It is used to demonstrate the mechanism industry sources describe (a cash/instant discount being withheld or reduced when a buyer selects EMI instead of full payment), not to assert a specific, universal percentage.

4. **"RBI has said, since 2013, that true zero percent interest does not really exist in retail lending."** (Slide 7)
   Corroborated by multiple independent secondary sources describing an RBI circular from September 2013 addressing "zero percent interest" schemes on retail/consumer-durable purchases, including Business Standard's contemporaneous coverage and Moneylife's reporting, plus legal/compliance commentary from Vinod Kothari Consultants. Direct retrieval of the primary RBI document was attempted but blocked by this session's network egress policy (rbi.org.in and rbidocs.rbi.org.in were both inaccessible). The exact circular number is therefore marked [VERIFY] and has been kept out of the on-slide copy; only the general, well-corroborated year and claim are used on-slide.

5. **"Add a processing fee. Add GST on top of that."** (Slide 6)
   Supported by finance-explainer sources (ClearTax, BankBazaar, myPaisaa) describing GST applying to processing fees and/or the interest component embedded in no-cost EMI pricing. On-slide copy uses "often" and "can apply" rather than asserting this is universal, since GST applicability and processing-fee amounts vary by issuer and were not verified against a primary tax-authority source.

## Claims deliberately excluded from the carousel

- Any specific bank's named processing-fee amount — fee amounts vary by issuer and change over time; slide 6 instead shows a rounded illustrative range ("often ₹199–499") rather than naming a specific bank.
- Any specific retailer's real discount percentage on a real product — no primary retailer pricing data was consulted; slide 5 uses a clearly labelled hypothetical example instead.
- A claim that all no-cost EMI schemes charge a visible, separately itemised GST or processing fee line — some issuers structure this differently. Phrased with "often"/"can" rather than "always."
