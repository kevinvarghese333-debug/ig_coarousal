Most people think "I pay my credit card bill on time" settles the matter. It does not. Card issuers treat "on time" and "in full" as two different things, and the gap between them is where the interest-free period quietly disappears.

Pay only the minimum due, even by the due date, and the outstanding balance starts earning interest from the date of each purchase, not from today. Some cards apply that same loss of the free period to new spending too. It is not a punishment for being careless. It is how a revolving balance is built to work.

None of this means minimum due is always wrong to use in a genuine emergency. It means treating it as a normal monthly habit is a different decision than most people realise they are making.

If you carry a balance most months, the actual rule is simple: whatever is left is a loan, not a balance, and it deserves to be paid down first.
