"No-cost EMI" sounds like the bank has agreed to lend you money for free. It has not.

In most of these offers, the lender still charges interest. The seller then gives a discount of about the same size, so the checkout total looks unchanged. On paper, you pay the phone's price. In practice, three things are worth a second look.

First, GST can apply to the interest portion, and the discount may not cover it. Second, a processing fee may sit on top. Third, the cash price on another platform or at a local shop may be lower than the price attached to the EMI offer.

The example on the slides is illustrative: Rs 30,000, 15% a year, 6 months. Real terms vary by lender and card, so check the current offer.

RBI has also said that zero percent interest does not really exist in card EMI schemes. The cost is priced in somewhere.

Question: if you could pay for your next phone today, would you still choose no-cost EMI, and why?
