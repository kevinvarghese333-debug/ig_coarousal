# "No-cost" EMI still has a cost

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: No-cost EMI on consumer purchases
- Central idea: A "no-cost" EMI usually does not remove the interest. It cancels it with a seller discount, and some cost can remain.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indians 21 to 40 buying phones and appliances on EMI in sale season
- Audience tension: It feels free, so people stop comparing prices
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and comments
- Why people may save, share or comment: A three-step checklist to use at checkout, and a judgement question about buying on EMI when you could pay upfront.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: Who actually pays for your "free" EMI?
2. Contrarian hook: "No-cost" EMI still has a cost.
3. Relatable-pain hook: Your phone EMI looked free. Check the bill.
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: The discount that hides your EMI interest.
5. Warning-or-risk hook: Before you tap "no-cost EMI", read this.

**Selected hook:** "No-cost" EMI still has a cost. The cost just moved.

**Why it wins:** It corrects a belief the viewer holds right now, in seven words.
