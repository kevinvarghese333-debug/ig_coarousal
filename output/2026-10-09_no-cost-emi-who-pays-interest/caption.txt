Zero-cost EMI sounds like a loan with the interest deleted. It is not. In most offers the lender still charges interest, and the seller or card issuer gives an upfront discount of about the same size, so the net looks like zero.

That is why the checkout screen feels clean and the bill can still surprise you. GST on the interest, a processing fee or a blocked credit limit can sit outside the discount. Offers differ by lender and seller, so check the current terms before you tap.

The slides use round, illustrative numbers on a ₹60,000 phone. They are not a quote from any bank.

The simplest test is to ask the shop for its price if you pay in full today. If that price is lower than the EMI price, the gap is what the EMI really costs you. RBI's card rules also expect the principal, interest and upfront discount to be shown before conversion, so ask to see them.
