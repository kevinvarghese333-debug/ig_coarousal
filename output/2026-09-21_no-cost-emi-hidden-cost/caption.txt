You tap "No Cost EMI" because the checkout screen makes it sound settled. It rarely is. Banks are not set up to lend money for nothing, so the interest a zero-cost plan appears to erase usually shows up somewhere else on the receipt: a discount that quietly disappears once you choose EMI, a processing fee for the conversion, or GST charged on the interest the bank still works out internally.

None of this makes no-cost EMI a scam. Sometimes the math genuinely works in your favour, especially when a brand is absorbing the cost to move stock. The problem is that most buyers never check, because the label does the deciding for them.

The fix is boring and effective: before you tap that button, check the cash price on its own, ask if a discount applies without EMI, and add any processing fee to the total. Compare that number to the EMI total, not to the monthly figure.
