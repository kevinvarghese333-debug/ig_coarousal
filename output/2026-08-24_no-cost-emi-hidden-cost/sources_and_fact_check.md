## Claims used on-slide, and their support

| Slide | Claim | Support | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4, 5, 6 | Most "No Cost EMI" offers work by folding the cash discount you would have received for paying upfront into the interest that is then labelled "zero." | Corroborated by multiple independent explainers (Poonawalla Fincorp, CRIF High Mark, CAclubindia). Stated in hedged language ("most", "usually", "often") since exact structure varies by seller and lender. | Reasonably confident, hedged |
| 7 | Regulators have, in the past, formally objected to "zero percent interest" labelling because the interest does not disappear, it moves. | RBI notification "Zero percent Interest Finance Schemes for Consumer Durables," issued September 2013 (rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/31069.pdf), corroborated by Business Standard and Moneylife reporting from the same period. | Confident on substance and year; exact circular number and exact day not independently re-verified in this session (rbi.org.in was unreachable from this environment) |

## Claims deliberately kept off-slide

- Any specific rupee or percentage worked example (for example, a ₹-value showing "how much extra you pay") — no single verified figure applies across sellers, banks and offers.
- A specific processing-fee range for converting a purchase to EMI — fee amounts vary by issuer and were not corroborated against an official, current fee schedule.
- A specific GST percentage tied to EMI interest or processing fees — GST treatment is nuanced and lender-dependent; no single figure could be confirmed as universally current.
- The exact RBI circular number and exact day of the 2013 notification — kept general on-slide ("RBI notification ... 2013") rather than stating an unverified reference number.
