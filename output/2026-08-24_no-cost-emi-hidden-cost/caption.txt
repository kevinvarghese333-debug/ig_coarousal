You have seen this at checkout. Pay now, or choose No Cost EMI, same total either way, interest showing as zero. It looks like a free upgrade. Most of the time, it is a trade you made without noticing.

On many no-cost EMI offers, the interest gets funded by the discount you would have received for paying upfront. Say the discount instead of interest. The number on your bill does not move. Where it comes from does.

This has been flagged before. Regulators have objected to "zero percent interest" labelling in the past, for the same reason we are talking about today: the interest does not disappear, it just changes its name.

None of this makes No Cost EMI a bad choice. Spreading a big purchase can be sensible. The point is to choose it on purpose, knowing what you gave up, instead of tapping it because the number said zero.

Next time you are at checkout, what would you have paid for the same thing today?
