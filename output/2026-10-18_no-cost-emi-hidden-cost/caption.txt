Zero-cost EMI is one of the most familiar buttons in Indian checkout screens, and one of the least examined.

A lender still has to earn something on the money it advances. So the interest does not disappear. In many no-cost EMI offers, it gets matched by a seller discount, and the invoice shows a tidy zero. The number in the example slide is illustrative, but the mechanism is the one to look for.

RBI's card rules also say that an EMI with an interest component should not be camouflaged as zero-interest or no-cost. Terms differ across cards, banks and sellers, so check the current terms before you tap.

The bigger shift is behavioural. A ₹30,000 phone stops being a ₹30,000 decision and becomes a small monthly one.

This is general education, not advice for your situation.
