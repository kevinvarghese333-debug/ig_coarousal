# The Minimum Due Trap

## Status

Production complete. Review before Instagram publishing.

## Strategic Premise

- Topic: Why paying only the credit card "minimum amount due" on time still lets interest pile up.
- Central idea: Paying the minimum due by the due date keeps your credit score safe, but it does not protect the interest-free period. Interest starts running on the whole outstanding amount, and on new purchases, from the transaction date.
- Primary audience: Salaried Indian professionals aged 21 to 40 who use a credit card regularly and have, at least once, paid only the minimum due instead of the full bill.
- Audience tension: They believe "I paid on time, so I am fine." The carousel shows the specific point where that belief breaks.
- Primary engagement goal: Saves and shares. This is a decision rule people forward to a sibling, partner or friend who carries a card balance.
- Why people may save, share or comment: It rewrites a habit many people think is safe. It gives a precise mechanism, not a vague warning, so it feels credible enough to send to someone. The final slide invites a specific, answerable question about behaviour, not opinion.

## Five Cover Hooks

1. Curiosity hook: "You paid your credit card bill on time. Why is it still growing."
2. Contrarian hook: "Paying the minimum due is not the safe option you think it is."
3. Relatable-pain hook: "You paid something. The app said thank you. The bill grew anyway."
4. Hidden-business-mechanism hook: "The minimum due is designed to keep you paying, not to clear your debt."
5. Warning-or-risk hook: "One missed rupee on your credit card bill can cost you the whole interest-free period."

**Selected hook:** "You paid your credit card bill on time. Why is it still growing."

**Why it wins:** It states a contradiction the reader has likely lived through, forces a beat of confusion before the mechanism resolves it, and does not require any prior finance knowledge to feel the tension.

## Caption

You paid the bill. The app said thank you. So why did next month's statement still carry interest?

Most people believe "on time" means "safe." With a credit card, that is only true if you pay the full Total Amount Due, not the Minimum Amount Due.

Miss that distinction and the card issuer treats the bill as unpaid in full. The interest-free period disappears, not just on the old balance, but on every new purchase you make that cycle. Interest starts counting from the date of each transaction, not from the due date you actually paid by.

The minimum due itself is not designed to shrink your debt. It is built to cover interest, fees and a small piece of principal, so the account stays "current" while the real balance barely moves.

None of this shows up as a missed payment. It shows up quietly, as a bill that grows even though you kept paying.

Check your own card's Total Amount Due against what you actually paid last month. Were you paying it off, or just keeping it current?

## Design Handoff

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5, identical across all 9 slides.
- Palette: Navy background #080C18, gold accent #C9A84C, off-white text #F6F1E7, secondary slate #AEB7C2 for labels and source notes, signal red #D94B45 used only for the interest bracket on Slide 5, muted green #4B8B72 not used in this carousel (no positive financial signal to highlight).
- Typography: Editorial serif (Playfair Display, or DejaVu Serif as fallback) for headline moments on Slides 1, 3, 6 and 9. Clean sans serif (DM Sans, or DejaVu Sans as fallback) for body copy, labels, slide numbers and source notes on all slides.
- Image or screenshot requirements: No screenshots used. All visual elements (phone frame, receipt card, timeline, stacked bar, checklist) are code-drawn generic shapes, not copies of any real bank interface or statement.
- Chart and data guidance: Slide 5 timeline and Slide 7 stacked bar are illustrative diagrams only, not sourced data, and are labelled or designed so they cannot be mistaken for real statistics. Slide 6 numbers are explicitly labelled "Illustrative example."
- Mobile-readability notes: Headline sizes 80 to 120px, subhead 38 to 52px, body 30 to 40px, all rendered with generous line spacing. Safe margin of at least 90px on all sides so nothing sits at the edge or in the profile-grid crop zone.
- Accessibility notes: Maintain high contrast (off-white or gold text on navy background throughout). Avoid conveying meaning through colour alone on the comparison card and stacked bar; labels are always present in text.
- Animation notes: Entire carousel is static. No slide requires motion to be understood, so no animation was produced for this run.
