# Research Notes: The Credit Score Timing Trap

## Topic Selected

Why paying your credit card bill on time and in full no longer guarantees a healthy-looking credit profile, because of when your balance gets reported to the credit bureau versus when you pay it off, and why this now matters faster because of RBI's move to more frequent bureau reporting.

## Why This Topic Was Selected

- Priority match: timely and verifiable Indian regulatory development (RBI credit information reporting frequency change, effective 1 July 2026) combined with a widespread audience confusion (on-time payment protects your score).
- The topic bank listed "why people who pay their credit card bill on time still get into money trouble" as a priority topic. This angle sharpens it into one verifiable mechanism (statement-date reporting) rather than a repeat of the well-worn "minimum due trap" content already saturating Indian finance content.
- No topic in `published_log.csv` yet (empty log, first run). No conflict with the 30-day repeat rule.
- Strong save/share potential: gives a concrete, checkable behaviour change (pay down big purchases before the statement date, not just before the due date).

## Primary Sources Consulted

1. RBI FAQ and Master Direction page for Credit Card and Debit Card - Issuance and Conduct
   - https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/FAQMDCreditCardandDebitCard.pdf
   - https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/92MDCREDITDEBITCARDC423AFFB5E7945149C95CDD2F71E9158.PDF
   - Confirms: if the total amount due is not paid by the due date, the interest-free period is lost and interest is charged from the transaction date on the outstanding balance, not just the unpaid minimum. Confirms mandatory warning language banks must print on statements about minimum-payment consequences. Amendments dated 7 March 2024.

2. RBI Master Directions index page (Credit Information Reporting amendments)
   - https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12764
   - Confirms RBI issued amendment directions in 2025 to shorten the reporting cycle for credit information reporting by commercial banks, NBFCs and local area banks to Credit Information Companies (CICs).

3. Secondary reporting corroborating the RBI amendment timeline and mechanics (used because the specific reference-date grid was not independently confirmed on a primary RBI PDF in this session):
   - https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/rbi-extends-credit-information-reporting-norms-july-1-2026-125120401312_1.html (deferral of effective date to 1 July 2026, dated report)
   - https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/rbi-shortens-frequency-of-bank-reports-to-credit-information-companies-124080800668_1.html (original 2024 move from monthly to fortnightly)
   - https://www.nbfccompliance.com/post/rbi-credit-information-companies-credit-bureau-amendment-directions-2025 (describes structured reporting on fixed reference dates: 9th, 16th, 23rd and last day of the month for NBFCs, plus a full-file monthly submission)
   - Note: one source describes reference dates as 7th/14th/21st/28th/last day, another describes 9th/16th/23rd/last day (likely differing by regulated-entity category or draft vs final direction). Exact reference-date grid is marked [VERIFY] below; on-slide copy avoids the specific date list and instead says reporting has moved from monthly to multiple times a month.

4. Credit utilization ratio mechanics and its role in Indian credit scoring
   - https://www.paisabazaar.com/credit-score/how-credit-utilization-ratio-works-and-what-percentage-maintain-aio/
   - https://cardtrail.in/basics/credit-utilization-ratio-india-guide/
   - Confirms: card issuers report the outstanding balance as of the statement generation date to the bureau, not the post-payment balance. A large purchase sitting on the statement can show as high utilization even if the cardholder pays the full bill on time. Common guidance across Indian financial sites: keep utilization under roughly 30 percent of the limit for a healthy-looking profile.
   - The often-quoted "utilization carries about 30 percent weight in score calculation" figure traces to publicly explained FICO-style scoring factor weights (US model) and is repeated by Indian aggregator sites, but TransUnion CIBIL does not publish its own exact factor weightings. Marked [VERIFY] and excluded from on-slide copy as a hard percentage; slides instead say utilization is "one of the biggest factors," which is well supported without citing an unverifiable exact weight.

## Claims Included in the Carousel

- Paying the full bill by the due date avoids interest charges and protects the cardholder from the "interest from transaction date" penalty. (Verified: RBI Master Direction / FAQ.)
- The bank still reports the cardholder's outstanding balance as it stood on the statement date to the credit bureau, regardless of whether the bill is later paid in full. (Verified: multiple industry explainer sources; consistent with how billing-cycle reporting works structurally.)
- A high balance on the statement date can show as high credit utilization even when the cardholder never misses a payment. (Verified: consistent explanation across sources.)
- RBI has moved lenders from monthly to a more frequent reporting cycle to credit bureaus, taking effect 1 July 2026. (Verified: RBI Master Directions page plus corroborating dated reporting; specific reference-date grid marked [VERIFY].)
- More frequent reporting means a high statement-date balance reflects in the bureau record sooner and more often than under the old monthly cycle. (Reasonable inference from the verified reporting-frequency change; framed on-slide as directional, not a guaranteed score-movement claim.)
- Practical rule: paying down a large purchase before the statement generation date (not just before the due date) keeps reported utilization lower. (Verified: consistent guidance across financial explainer sources.)

## Claims Removed Because They Were Not Adequately Verified

- Exact CIBIL/Experian/Equifax factor weightings (e.g., "utilization is 30 percent of your score, payment history is 35 percent"). These are FICO-model figures commonly repeated online; Indian bureaus do not publish their own exact weights. Removed from slide copy.
- The exact reference-date grid for the new RBI reporting cycle (7/14/21/28 vs 9/16/23/last day across sources). Removed the specific date list from slide copy; kept the directional claim only (monthly to multiple-times-a-month, effective 1 July 2026).
- Any specific bank-by-bank interest rate figures (rates vary 30-45 percent annualized across issuers and change over time). Not used on slides; general language only ("high annualised interest," no specific number attributed carousel-wide).
- Any suggestion that a specific numeric score drop will occur. Score-impact language kept qualitative ("can look worse," "can affect approval") rather than quantified.
