# Research Notes: No Cost EMI, the hidden cost

## Topic selected

Why "No Cost EMI" is not the same as an interest-free purchase, and where the interest actually goes.

## Why it was selected

- It is the top priority item in `carousel_topic_bank.md` and has not been drafted or published before (this is the first carousel in the repository's history, confirmed by an empty `published_log.csv` and an empty Published section in the topic bank).
- It hits a moment nearly every audience member has faced: choosing "No Cost EMI" at checkout on a phone, appliance or gadget purchase.
- It has a genuine, checkable regulatory anchor (an RBI stance from 2013) rather than a vague behavioural claim, which supports the brand's "explained without guru nonsense" position.
- It fits the Trap or Warning carousel type: appealing surface, missing question, risk, safer way to think.

2. General mechanics of "No Cost EMI" pricing (cash discount forgone, processing fee, GST on fees): ClearTax ("GST on No Cost EMI: Rates, Applicability and Credit Card Impact"), TaxGuru ("The Big Picture behind No Cost EMI"), and two consumer finance blogs (myMudra, myPaisaa) consistently describe the same three-part mechanism:
   - The seller/brand often pays the bank the interest amount upfront (a subvention) to advertise "0% EMI."
   - That subvention is commonly recovered by removing or reducing the cash/instant discount a full-upfront buyer would otherwise get, or by pricing the EMI option higher than the cash price.
   - Banks still charge a processing fee on the EMI, and financial service fees of this kind typically attract GST.
   - **[VERIFY]**: These domains (cleartax.in, taxguru.in, mymudra.com, mypaisaa blog) were blocked by this environment's egress policy for direct WebFetch, so only search-result snippets could be reviewed, not the full articles. The general three-part mechanism is consistent across all four independent snippets, which supports using it descriptively. Exact processing-fee amounts and the precise GST rate applied to a given fee were NOT verified to a primary source (CBIC/GST law or a specific bank's current tariff) and are therefore excluded from on-slide copy.

## Claims included in the carousel

- RBI's 2013 position that "zero percent interest" is not a real, cost-free concept, stated in plain language without citing the exact circular number on-slide (kept in this research file instead).
- The general mechanism: on a "No Cost EMI" purchase, the interest cost does not vanish, it is typically recovered through the listed price, a forgone cash discount, or a processing fee.
- The general fact that a processing fee is a real, separate charge, and that fees of this kind usually carry GST (no specific rate stated on-slide).
