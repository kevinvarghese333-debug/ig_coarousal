# Research Notes: Zero-Cost EMI Hidden Cost

## Topic selected

Why "No Cost EMI" / "zero-cost EMI" is not actually a free decision, and what the missing interest is usually replaced by (a foregone cash discount, and sometimes a processing fee).

## Research notes

- Multiple independent secondary sources (Business Standard, Moneylife, Onmanorama) consistently report that the RBI acted in September 2013 to stop banks from marketing retail loan schemes as "zero percent interest," on the basis that a truly interest-free loan is not a real product and that the cost is otherwise built into the price or recovered as processing charges. This is consistent across sources but the exact circular number and precise operative wording have not been independently confirmed against the primary RBI PDF in this session.
- The commonly described mechanism for "No Cost EMI" / "zero-cost EMI" today (per Moneyview, Innoviti, Paytm docs): the bank or NBFC still charges interest on the loan. That interest cost is funded by the merchant/platform, usually by removing an equivalent upfront cash discount rather than passing a visible interest line to the buyer. This is a widely and consistently described industry mechanism across the sources above, not a single official regulatory figure, so it is presented on-slide as a general mechanism rather than as a cited statistic.
- No specific current interest rate, processing-fee percentage, or GST rate is used on any slide, because these vary by bank, NBFC, merchant and product and were not independently verified for a single current figure. The carousel uses general language ("often," "usually," "check the terms") rather than a fixed number, per the financial-safety rules.
- The worked numeric example on Slide 5 (a ₹24,000 phone, a 5% cash discount, six EMIs of ₹4,000) is an **illustrative hypothetical**, not a reported real transaction or statistic. It exists purely to make the mechanism concrete, the same way the design mastermind's own copy examples use illustrative figures. It is not a claim that needs source verification because it is not presented as fact about any real product or lender.

## Claims included in the carousel

1. "No Cost EMI" schemes still involve interest being charged by the bank/NBFC; that cost is usually funded by the merchant rather than eliminated. (Supported by sources 4, 5, 6.)
2. RBI has taken the position that a genuinely interest-free retail lending scheme is not a real product, and pushed back on "zero percent" marketing. (Supported by sources 1, 2, 3 — secondary reporting, primary circular not independently confirmed this session.)
3. The interest cost is commonly recovered by removing a cash discount the buyer would otherwise have received for paying the full price upfront. (Supported by sources 4, 5, 6.)
4. General practical rule: compare the full upfront price to the total of all EMI instalments before assuming a scheme is free. (Derived directly from claims 1 and 3, not a separate factual claim requiring its own citation.)

## Claims removed because they were not adequately verified

- A specific current interest rate charged on "No Cost EMI" schemes by any named bank or NBFC. Removed because rates vary by lender and product and change over time.
- A specific processing-fee percentage or GST treatment of that fee. Removed for the same reason; slide copy instead says "check the current terms."
- Any claim naming a specific bank, NBFC, retailer, or platform. Removed to avoid implying a product recommendation or singling out one company without a verified, current citation.
- Any statistic about how many Indian consumers use No Cost EMI, or behavioural-economics statistics about instalment spending. No verified current Indian-market figure was found in this session, so the carousel describes the behavioural effect in general terms only, without a number attached.
