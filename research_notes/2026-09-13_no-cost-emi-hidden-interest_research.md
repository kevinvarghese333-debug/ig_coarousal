# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Hidden Interest

## Topic Selected

How "No Cost EMI" actually works, and why the zero percent framing is not the same as zero cost.

## Why It Was Selected

- Priority topic bank item 1 ("Why zero-cost EMI is not always a free decision") has not been drafted or published before; `published_log.csv` and `carousel_topic_bank.md`'s Published section were both empty at the start of this run.
- It is a common and expensive money mistake (priority tier 3) and a common audience confusion (priority tier 2): almost every reader has tapped "No Cost EMI" at a checkout without knowing the mechanism behind it.
- It is evergreen and does not depend on volatile market data, so the accuracy risk is lower than a markets or company-earnings topic.
- It fits the Trap-or-Warning carousel type in the design mastermind (appealing surface → missing question → risk → safer way to think) without requiring fear-mongering.

- Business Standard, "RBI diktat on 0% EMI will impact banks, consumer durable firms" (business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/rbi-diktat-on-0-emi-will-impact-banks-consumer-durable-firms-113092600851_1.html) — corroborates the same 2013 RBI action and its expected market impact.

- Multiple 2026-dated finance blogs (Finnable, CardCheck, myPaisaa, Economics Shala, business.paytm.com documentation on EMI subvention) — consistent, non-contradictory descriptions of the interest-subvention mechanism: the lender still books and charges interest on the loan, the merchant pays that interest to the lender, and the merchant typically recovers the cost by not offering the cash discount it would otherwise give for full payment. No numeric claim from these sources was used on-slide.

## Research Notes

- The consistent story across every source, primary-adjacent and secondary, is the same three-step mechanism: (1) the bank still charges interest on the underlying loan, (2) the merchant, not the customer, pays that interest to the bank as "subvention," (3) the merchant typically recovers this cost by withdrawing the cash discount it would otherwise offer for outright payment. This mechanism (not any specific number) is the safest, best-supported claim to build the carousel around, and it is what Slides 3 to 5 explain.
- Multiple sources mention that GST is charged on the interest/processing component of an EMI conversion, and that per-instalment processing fees are commonly charged by banks (cited ranges as wide as ₹99 to ₹999 per month across different blogs). These are tax and fee details that vary by bank, card and time period, and no primary GST or bank source was found in this pass. Per the production rules ("do not use fragile or variable fee, tax or product numbers unless clearly verified and current" and "if a claim cannot be adequately verified, remove it from on-slide copy"), these numbers were excluded from the carousel and caption entirely rather than blocking the run.
- The rupee example used on Slide 5 (₹60,000 phone, illustrative discount) is not sourced to any real bank, retailer or product. It is explicitly labelled "an illustrative example" on-slide and again in the Design Handoff and Quick Pre-Post Checks sections of the draft, so it is not presented as a verified statistic.

## Claims Included in the Carousel

- The interest-subvention mechanism (bank still charges interest → merchant pays it → merchant recovers it by withdrawing the cash discount). Supported consistently across all sources consulted; not dependent on a single fragile figure.
- The historical claim that the RBI, around 2013, found that "0% interest" EMI schemes were not genuinely free and that interest was sometimes camouflaged as a processing fee. Used on Slide 9 and in the caption. Flagged [VERIFY] for Kevin to confirm the exact circular reference against RBI's own site before publishing, since only secondary reporting was available in this research pass.
- The behavioural claim that removing a visible interest cost also removes the moment a buyer would otherwise ask "can I afford this today." This is presented as an interpretive, editorial observation (consistent with the brand's operator voice), not as a cited statistic, so it does not require a source.

## Claims Removed Because They Were Not Adequately Verified

- Any specific GST rate or rupee amount applied to the interest/processing component of a no-cost EMI. Removed from all on-slide copy and the caption.
- Any specific per-instalment processing fee amount or range charged by banks (e.g., the ₹99 to ₹999 figures seen in secondary blog sources). Removed from all on-slide copy and the caption; fee amounts vary by bank, card and time and were judged too fragile to state as fact.
- No specific bank, NBFC or retailer is named anywhere in the carousel, to avoid an implied claim about any single institution's current terms.
