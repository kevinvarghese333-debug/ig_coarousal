# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Hidden Cost

## Topic selected

Why "zero-cost EMI" is not always a free decision — the mechanism behind
no-cost EMI schemes on phones, appliances and electronics in India.

## Why it was selected

- Priority topic #1 in `carousel_topic_bank.md`, not previously drafted or
  published (published_log.csv is empty — this is the first run).
- Matches a "common and expensive money mistake" (priority tier 3 in the
  routine's topic-selection order) and a "common audience confusion"
  (tier 2): most buyers assume "no cost" literally means zero cost.
- Evergreen, high-frequency purchase moment (phones, appliances, festive
  sales) for the 21-40 salaried/first-time-borrower audience — strong
  save/share potential since it is genuinely useful before a purchase.
- Does not require fragile, fast-changing statistics (bank-specific rates,
  live market data), so it holds up well over time.

## Primary sources consulted

1. **Reserve Bank of India — 2013 circular on retail lending schemes of
   banks (zero percent interest EMI schemes on consumer goods).**
   RBI directed banks that the concept of "zero percent interest" is not
   real economically, since the interest cost is built into the product
   price or recovered through the processing fee, and that this practice
   distorts transparent pricing. RBI told banks not to promote schemes
   labelled "zero percent interest" for consumer durables/consumption
   loans.
   Source reporting on the circular: Business Standard, "RBI asks banks
   to charge uniform interest rate on retail EMI schemes,"
   https://www.business-standard.com/article/reuters/rbi-asks-banks-to-charge-uniform-interest-rate-on-retail-emi-schemes-113092500642_1.html
   (dated September 2013); Moneylife, "RBI says no zero percent interest
   scheme for buying consumer goods,"
   https://www.moneylife.in/article/rbi-says-no-zero-percent-interest-scheme-for-buying-consumer-goods/34611.html
   (dated 2013).
   [VERIFY] — I have not read the original RBI circular PDF directly
   (RBI's website archive was not accessed in this session); the summary
   above is drawn from contemporaneous financial-press reporting of the
   circular. Kevin should confirm wording against RBI's official
   notification before the carousel implies a direct RBI quote.

2. **General mechanism of no-cost EMI (interest subvention).**
   Multiple current explainer sources (bank and fintech blogs, 2025-2026)
   describe the same mechanism consistently: the retailer/brand pays the
   lender an upfront amount (interest subvention) roughly equal to the
   interest the lender would otherwise charge, and in exchange withholds
   the upfront cash discount it would have offered a paying-in-full
   customer. The customer's EMI instalments add up to close to the sticker
   price, so no separate "interest" line appears on the statement.
   Representative sources:
   - moneyview.in, "No Cost EMI Means, What is No Cost EMI, How It Works,"
     https://moneyview.in/insights/what-is-the-meaning-of-no-cost-emi-and-how-does-it-works
   - Innoviti (payments infrastructure provider), "Disadvantages of No
     Cost EMI: What Businesses and Merchants Should Know,"
     https://innoviti.com/blogs/disadvantages-of-no-cost-emi/
   - ICICI Bank, "No Cost EMI Credit Card: Meaning, Eligibility & Key
     Benefits," https://www.icici.bank.in/personal-banking/blogs/card/credit-card/no-cost-emi-on-credit-card-and-how-does-it-work
   All accessed August 2026; treat as corroborating explainer sources
   rather than primary regulatory sources.

3. **GST on processing fee / interest component.**
   Standard GST rate on financial services (including loan processing
   fees) in India is 18%. This is general, well-established tax
   treatment, not a scheme-specific number.
   [VERIFY] — Whether GST is charged on the "waived" interest itself
   varies by lender and product, and some no-cost EMI schemes structure
   pricing to avoid a visible GST line. The carousel avoids stating a
   specific rupee GST figure for this reason and keeps the language to
   "GST may apply on the processing component."

## Claims included in the carousel

- No-cost EMI is not literally free; the cost is usually recovered by the
  lender through the merchant, not charged as a separate visible interest
  line. (Supported by source 2, consistent across multiple independent
  explainers.)
- RBI has, in the past, taken the position that "zero percent interest"
  is not a real pricing structure for consumer loans and pushed for
  transparent, uniform pricing regardless of scheme labelling. (Supported
  by source 1, marked as historical regulatory context, not framed as an
  ongoing live enforcement action or a direct quote.)
- The upfront cash-discount price and the no-cost EMI total price are
  often different for the same product, and the gap is the disguised
  interest. (Supported by source 2.)
- Practical decision rule: ask for the discounted upfront price before
  choosing no-cost EMI, and compare total outflow, not the monthly
  number. (This is a general financial-literacy heuristic, not a
  product-specific or numeric claim, so it does not require a citation.)
