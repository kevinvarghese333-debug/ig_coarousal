# Research Notes: The Credit Card Minimum-Due Trap

## Topic selected

Why people who pay their credit card bill "on time" every month can still end up paying heavy interest, because paying on time and paying in full are treated completely differently by the card issuer.

## Why this topic was selected

- It is the flagship example central idea named directly in `whenkevintalks_carousel_design_mastermind.md`, confirming strong fit with brand voice and audience.
- It sits in Priority topic #2 from `carousel_topic_bank.md`.
- `published_log.csv` and `carousel_topic_bank.md` show no prior published or drafted carousels (this is the first run), so there is no 30-day repeat conflict.
- It is a near-universal Indian salaried-professional experience (credit card bill, minimum due button in the banking app), giving strong recognition and share potential.
- The mechanism (loss of interest-free period) is grounded in a clear, checkable primary regulatory source rather than a fragile statistic, which keeps the financial-accuracy risk low.
- It naturally supports a save-worthy decision rule (treat any carried balance as a loan, not a balance) without giving personalised credit advice.

## Claims included in the carousel (on-slide)

- Paying only the "minimum due" amount, even on time, is legally and mechanically different from paying the bill "in full."
- If the full statement balance is not cleared, the interest-free period is lost and interest is charged from the date of each transaction on the outstanding balance, not from the due date, and often on new transactions too, based on the RBI Master Direction, 2022 mechanism described above.
- The minimum due is commonly around 5% of the outstanding balance (framed as "commonly," not universal, since there is no single fixed RBI formula).
- Illustrative worked example (₹40,000 bill / ₹2,000 minimum due / ₹38,000 carried forward) is explicitly labelled as an illustrative example with invented round numbers, not a real bank statement or real customer data.
- General statement that revolving balances generate ongoing interest revenue for the card issuer, framed as a description of how revolving credit works economically, not an accusation against any named bank.

## Claims removed because they were not adequately verified

- The specific "3-day mandatory grace period on late payment charges" and other "RBI 2026 new rules" claims circulating on secondary finance blogs. Excluded because the primary RBI source could not be directly fetched and confirmed in this session, and the claim is not central to the carousel's one idea.
- Any single, fixed interest rate percentage (e.g. "42% per year") presented as a universal or current figure. Replaced with a range framed as "often" and flagged for verification, since it varies by card and issuer and can change.
- Any single, fixed minimum-due percentage presented as a universal rule. Framed as "commonly around 5%, set by the issuer" instead.
