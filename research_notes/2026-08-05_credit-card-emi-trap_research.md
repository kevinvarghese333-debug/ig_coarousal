# Research Notes: Credit Card EMI Conversion Trap

## Topic Selected

Why paying a credit card bill in full and on time does not always mean interest-free, because converting a purchase into EMI quietly reintroduces interest, fees and GST.

## Why It Was Selected

- Fits priority topic #2 in `carousel_topic_bank.md`: "Why people who pay their credit-card bill on time still get into money trouble."
- No topic has been published in the last 30 days (`published_log.csv` is empty), so no repeat-topic conflict.
- Meets the "common and expensive money mistake" priority tier: EMI conversion offers are pushed proactively by banks and apps at checkout and via SMS, and the interest/fee/GST stack is easy to miss because the amount still shows up as a normal, on-time EMI payment.
- The mechanism is narrow enough to state as one sharp sentence, satisfying the One-Idea Rule, and does not require fragile, single-bank-specific numbers to make the point (ranges and general mechanics are enough).

## Primary Sources Consulted

1. **RBI Master Direction, Credit Card and Debit Card - Issuance and Conduct Directions, 2022** (effective 1 July 2022, subsequently amended). Index page: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12300 ; notification page: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12342&Mode=0
   - Secondary summaries (LinkedIn explainers by industry commentators) indicate the Direction requires card issuers to show principal, interest and any upfront merchant/issuer discount transparently when a transaction is converted to EMI, in the statement, and prohibits presenting an interest-bearing EMI conversion as "zero-interest" or "no-cost" EMI.
   - I was not able to open and quote the exact clause text directly in this session (web fetch to the RBI PDF was not attempted beyond search-result summaries). This claim is marked [VERIFY] and flagged for Kevin to confirm the exact clause before publishing, or to drop the RBI reference if it cannot be quickly confirmed.

2. **Standard Chartered Bank, "Balance on EMI" FAQ**: https://www.sc.com/in/credit-cards/balance-on-emi-faq/
   - States a one-time processing fee of up to 1% (plus applicable taxes) became effective 19 February 2024 for their Balance-on-EMI conversion product. This is one bank's disclosed fee, used here only as an example of how processing fees are structured, not as a universal figure.

3. **Paisabazaar, "Credit Card EMI Conversion – How It Works, Charges & Tips"**: https://www.paisabazaar.com/credit-card/emi-credit-card/ (accessed via search, dated 27 June 2026)
   - Reports EMI conversion interest rates generally starting around 12.5% per annum and varying by bank and card, with different issuers quoting different bands.

4. **CardCheck, bank-by-bank EMI conversion breakdown**: https://cardcheck.in/blogs/best-credit-card-emi-conversion-options-india-bank-by-bank-2026
   - Corroborates that rates vary meaningfully bank to bank (examples cited in search summary: roughly 12%-18% for some issuers, higher for others).

5. **righttoinformation.wiki, "Credit Card Charges in India: Every Fee Explained (2026)"**: https://righttoinformation.wiki/credit-card-charges-india
   - Cited in search summary as a source for processing-fee ranges (roughly ₹99-₹250, or a percentage of transaction value, plus GST) across providers. Treated as a secondary aggregator, not a primary source; used only to sanity-check the order of magnitude.

6. **ClearTax, "GST on Credit Card and Credit Card EMI"**: https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-credit-card-emi
   **BankBazaar, "GST on Credit Card EMI, Impact, Charges and Tax Details"**: https://www.bankbazaar.com/credit-card/gst-on-credit-card-emi.html
   - Both describe the standard 18% GST rate applying to the interest component and to processing fees on credit card EMI conversions, not to the principal amount. For genuine "no-cost EMI" (merchant-subsidised, not a post-purchase Balance-on-EMI conversion), GST on interest does not apply because there is no interest component to tax, though GST can still apply to any processing fee.

## Research Notes

- The carousel deliberately narrows to **post-purchase EMI conversion of an existing credit card transaction/bill** (sometimes called "Balance on EMI," "EMI on Call," "Flexi EMI" depending on the bank), not merchant-subsidised "no-cost EMI" offered at the point of sale. This keeps the One-Idea Rule intact: one specific mechanism, not a general survey of all EMI types.
- The core mechanism does not depend on any single bank's exact rate: multiple banks converge on the general pattern of (a) an annualised interest rate roughly in the low-double-digits to mid-twenties percent range, (b) a one-time processing fee, and (c) 18% GST charged on the interest and fee, not on principal. This is stable enough to state as a range with a "rates vary by bank, check current terms" hedge, per the brand's financial-safety rules.
- Deliberately avoided using cash-advance interest-free-period mechanics as a second example, to keep the carousel to one idea rather than turning it into a general "credit card gotchas" listicle.

## Claims Included in the Carousel

1. Converting a credit card purchase to EMI can add interest, roughly in a 12%-24% per annum range depending on the bank and card (Slide 5). Hedged with "rates vary by bank" source note on-slide.
2. A one-time processing fee applies to EMI conversion (Slide 4/5, not quantified on-slide beyond "processing fee" to avoid overstating a specific figure).
3. GST of 18% applies on top of the interest and the processing fee, not on the principal (Slide 6).
4. The EMI still being paid on time does not erase the interest/fee/GST cost already added; it also does not show up as a credit-record problem (Slide 7). This is a logical consequence of claims 1-3, not a separate factual claim requiring its own citation.
