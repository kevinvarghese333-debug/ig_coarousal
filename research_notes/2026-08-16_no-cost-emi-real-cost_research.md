# Research Notes: 'No Cost EMI' Was Never Actually Free

## Topic Selected

Why "No Cost EMI" schemes are not actually free, and where the hidden cost goes (merchant subvention, foregone cash discount, processing fee and GST on that fee).

## Claims Included in the Carousel

- EMI is a loan and loans carry interest (general financial fact, not disputed).
- "No Cost EMI" schemes typically work through merchant subvention: the seller pays the interest amount to the bank/lender upfront, and in exchange the cash discount that would otherwise be offered is often not extended to the EMI buyer. (Corroborated across multiple secondary sources and consistent with RBI's description.)
- A processing fee is typically charged on EMI transactions. (Widely corroborated, kept as a general statement without a specific rupee figure.)
- In 2013, the RBI described the interest in zero percent EMI schemes as "camouflaged" and passed on to customers as processing fee, and moved to require uniform interest and processing fee treatment. Presented as historical regulatory context, with the year stated on-slide.
- ₹60,000 phone worked example on Slide 5: this is an illustrative calculation built for the carousel, not a claim about any real bank, retailer or product. Labelled "illustrative" both on-slide and in the design handoff.
