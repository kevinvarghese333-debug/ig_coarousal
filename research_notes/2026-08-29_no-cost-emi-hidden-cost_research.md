## Research Notes

The mechanism is consistently described the same way across every bank and aggregator source found: a "no-cost EMI" is a normal interest-bearing loan in the background. What removes the interest line from the customer's view is that the merchant or bank funds it, usually by not passing on a cash/instant discount that would otherwise apply, so the total the customer pays converges on the original, undiscounted sticker price. Two named mechanisms recur: (1) discount-removal (foregone cash discount subsidizes the EMI interest) and (2) interest subvention (merchant pays the bank the interest upfront out of its own margin). Both produce the same customer-facing result.

RBI's clearest historical intervention is from September 2013, when it told banks to charge uniform interest rates and processing fees on retail EMI schemes and stated that "the very concept of zero percent interest is non-existent." This is well corroborated across multiple outlets (Reuters via Business Standard, PTI via Business Standard, Moneylife) with consistent quoted language, though the exact circular/notification number could not be located in available search results. A 2023 Onmanorama retrospective piece independently confirms the same underlying 2013 action, which increases confidence in the substance even without the primary document in hand.

Behavioral evidence for "cost feels smaller, so people spend more" is well-supported academically, but the strongest paper (Maesen & Ang 2025) studied Buy Now Pay Later in a US retail context, not Indian card-issuer no-cost EMI specifically. The carousel applies this as a general behavioral mechanism, not as India-specific data, and slide 6 describes it as "a 2025 Journal of Marketing study on instalment payments" without overclaiming it was conducted on Indian no-cost EMI users specifically.

Processing fee figures vary meaningfully across sources (flat ₹99-500, or 1-3% of transaction value depending on lender/card), so no specific fee number was put on any slide; the carousel only states qualitatively that "processing fees can still apply" and that GST applies on top of any fee charged (the 18% GST-on-fees point was one of the most consistently repeated facts across ClearTax, Bajaj Finserv Markets and Upstox, so it was considered safe to state as a general rule rather than a claim about all lenders' base prices).

## Claims Included in the Carousel

- No-cost EMI does not eliminate a cost; it relocates it (usually via a foregone discount or merchant-funded interest subvention). — High confidence, converges across ICICI, Bajaj Finserv, Moneyview, BankBazaar, Paisabazaar.
- In 2013, RBI directed banks to stop advertising "zero percent interest" schemes and stated that zero percent interest, as a concept, does not exist. — High confidence via multiple converging press reports; exact circular number not confirmed, so not cited on-slide.
- A 2025 Journal of Marketing study found instalment payments are associated with increased purchase incidence and amount, with the effect linked to reduced perceived financial constraint. — Confirmed via AMA summary and preprint; described generally, not as India-specific data.
- RBI's Digital Lending Directions, 2025 (effective 8 May 2025) require lenders to provide a Key Fact Statement disclosing the full cost of credit before disbursal. — Confirmed via Mondaq/Lawrbit legal summaries; described on-slide as applying to loan-app/digital lending, not claimed to cover card-issuer no-cost EMI specifically.
- GST at 18% applies to interest/service components and to processing fees (not to the base product price). — Confirmed via ClearTax, Bajaj Finserv Markets, Upstox; stated as a general rule, not tied to a specific lender.
- Processing fees on no-cost EMI "can still apply" and "are rarely waived" (slide 6). — Stated qualitatively only; no specific number used on-slide given the range across sources.
