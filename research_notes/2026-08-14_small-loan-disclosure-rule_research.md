## Research Notes

- The Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 were notified 8 May 2025, reference RBI/2025-26/36 DOR.STR.REC.19/21.07.001/2025-26. They consolidate and are reported to supersede the September 2022 Guidelines on Digital Lending and the June 2023 Default Loss Guarantee guidelines.
- Multiple sources report staggered implementation: most provisions effective immediately on 8 May 2025, multi-lender arrangement provisions effective 1 November 2025, and Digital Lending App reporting requirements effective 15 June 2025. These implementation dates are secondary-sourced and were not used on-slide, they are background only.
- Key Fact Statement (KFS) requirement: regulated entities must provide a KFS containing the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), the recovery mechanism, details of a grievance redressal officer designated for digital lending matters, and the cooling-off or look-up period. This four-part description is consistent across sources and was used directly on slides 4 and 5.
- A separate RBI circular made KFS mandatory for all new retail and MSME term loans (digital or physical) sanctioned on or after 1 October 2024. That circular explicitly excludes credit card receivables, physical-mode lending to non-MSME corporates, and non-term-loan MSME lending. This exclusion detail was not needed on-slide but confirms the carousel's framing is about term loans and small-ticket digital loans, not credit cards.
- One secondary source claimed digital lending disbursals must flow directly into the borrower's bank account, bypassing the lending service provider entirely. This detail originates from the 2022 guidelines and is plausible, but it could not be checked against primary RBI text in this session, so it was deliberately left out of the on-slide copy.

## Claims Included In The Carousel

- RBI's 2025 digital lending rules require a Key Fact Statement before a borrower accepts a loan (slide 4).
- The Key Fact Statement must show the APR, the recovery process, the cooling-off period, and a grievance contact (slides 4 and 5).
- The practical framing that disclosure exists but app design still favours fast acceptance over careful reading (slides 6 and 7) is an editorial interpretation built on the verified facts above, not itself a claim requiring a source.

## Claims Removed Because They Were Not Adequately Verified

- The specific claim that loan disbursement must bypass the lending service provider and land directly in the borrower's account. Plausible and reported by secondary sources, but not confirmed against primary RBI text in this session. Removed from on-slide copy.
- No specific interest rate, APR percentage, or fee amount for any real lender or app was used anywhere in the carousel. The ₹15,000 example on slide 2 is an illustrative, hypothetical scenario for reader relatability, not a claim about any real product, rate, or company.
