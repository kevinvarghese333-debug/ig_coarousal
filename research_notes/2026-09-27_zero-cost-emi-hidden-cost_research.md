# Research Notes: Why "Zero-Cost" EMI Is Not a Free Decision

## Topic selected

Why zero-cost (no-cost) EMI on phones and appliances is not actually free, and what the buyer usually gives up in exchange.

## Why it was selected

- Tops the priority list in `carousel_topic_bank.md` and has not been drafted or published before (this is the first run; `published_log.csv` is empty).
- Matches priority order in the brief: it is a common and expensive money mistake, tied to a real, verifiable RBI position and a live shopping moment (festive-season phone and appliance sales), which is timely in India during September to November.
- High save/share potential: buyers actively compare "no cost EMI" checkout screens right before a purchase, so the insight is actionable at the exact decision moment.
- One-sentence central idea: "No-cost EMI is not a free decision because the cost simply moves from the interest line to a discount you quietly give up."

On-slide copy uses "tax on that fee" language instead of a fixed percentage.

3. Mechanism of "no-cost" EMI (cash discount forgone / interest subvention):
   - Forbes India, "How No-Cost-EMIs have changed the way Indians shop." https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/deep-dive/how-no-cost-emis-have-changed-the-way-indians-shop/2998406/1

   - Zerodha Varsity (Substack), "The actual cost of no-cost EMI." https://zerodhavarsity.substack.com/p/the-actual-cost-of-no-cost-emi
   - This mechanism (the lender still books interest; a merchant or brand pays that interest to the lender upfront as "interest subvention"; the buyer's price is structured so the cash-discount they would have received is withheld instead) is consistent across sources with no material contradiction, so it is used directly on-slide.

## Claims included in the carousel (on-slide)

- "No-cost EMI" schemes generally work by the lender still charging interest internally, with a merchant or brand paying that interest upfront (interest subvention) instead of the interest being waived outright. — Supported by Forbes India, Moneyview, Zerodha Varsity.
- The most common way the cost reappears for the buyer is a forgone cash discount: the buyer who chooses EMI typically does not get the same discount a full-cash buyer would get on the same product. — Supported by Forbes India, Moneyview.
- Regulators have previously pushed back on "0%"-branded EMI schemes because the interest cost does not disappear, it is repositioned elsewhere in the pricing. — Supported by three 2013 press reports on the RBI position (see [VERIFY] note above on the primary circular).
- A processing or convenience fee, where charged, can itself carry tax. — Supported by ClearTax and Paisabazaar; no specific rate is stated on-slide.
