# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Is Not Free

## Topic selected

"No cost EMI" checkout buttons (e-commerce, credit cards, consumer durables) and the real mechanism behind them.

## Why it was selected

- Priority 1 topic in `carousel_topic_bank.md`, never previously drafted or published (first run of the routine, `published_log.csv` and `carousel_topic_bank.md` Published section are both empty).
- It is a "common and expensive money mistake" (priority tier 3) that almost every audience member in the 21-40 salaried/first-time-borrower bracket has personally encountered at a checkout screen.
- It has a genuine, verifiable regulatory anchor (RBI Master Direction, 2022), which supports the "hidden mechanism" story type without needing invented statistics.
- It is evergreen, not tied to a single company or a fast-moving market number, so it stays accurate well past the publish date.

## Central idea (one sentence)

"No cost EMI" can be priced fairly today because regulation forced transparency, but fair pricing does not mean free money, and the real cost is the spending decision the label makes easier to say yes to.

## Research notes

- The mechanism: when a seller offers a cash discount, "no cost EMI" financing structures are often built by the seller/lender folding that same discount into what would otherwise be the interest cost, so the EMI schedule nets out to roughly the cash price. This is the industry-standard explanation across every source checked, but the exact amount forgone varies by seller, lender and product, so it is not stated as a fixed number on-slide.
- Card issuers can also charge a processing/conversion fee for EMI conversion, and fees (unlike loan interest, which is GST-exempt under GST law when charged by a regulated lender) generally attract GST. Because the fee amount and whether GST applies to the fee versus the interest component varies by issuer and product, **no specific fee or GST percentage is used on any slide**. The carousel says "interest, GST or a processing fee" without a number.
- The regulatory reveal used on Slide 6 is a paraphrase, not a verbatim quote, of the requirement that EMI conversion "shall not be camouflaged as zero-interest/no-cost EMI" and that principal, interest and upfront discount must be shown separately, both before conversion and on the statement. This paraphrase is corroborated by three independent secondary summaries (TaxGuru, LiveLaw, Mondaq) but the primary PDF was not directly readable in this session. **[VERIFY] before publishing: confirm exact clause wording against the official RBI PDF linked above.**
- Slide 5's rupee example (₹40,000 laptop, ₹2,000 forgone discount) is explicitly illustrative, not a claim about any real product or real seller. It is labelled "illustrative numbers" on the slide itself.
