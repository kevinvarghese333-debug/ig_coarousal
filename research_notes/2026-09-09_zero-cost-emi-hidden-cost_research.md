## Claims Included in the Carousel

1. **RBI has, since 2013, disallowed banks from advertising "zero percent interest" EMI schemes on credit card purchases**, stating that true zero-percent interest does not exist and that in such schemes the interest cost is often folded into a processing fee instead of being shown as interest.
   - Cited on-slide only in general terms ("RBI has flagged this since 2013") without quoting exact notification number or exact date, to avoid publishing an unverified primary citation.
   - Source: RBI notification, widely reported as RBI/2013-14/292, DBS.CO.PPD.No.3578/11.01.005/2013-14, dated September 2013 (secondary sources differ on exact day: 17 vs 20 September 2013). [VERIFY exact date and notification number against rbi.org.in before publishing]
2. **RBI also required that any discount or subvention offered by a manufacturer/dealer be reflected as a reduction in the loan/sanctioned amount, rather than being used to dress up a fake 0% interest rate.** This directly supports the carousel's mechanism explanation (discount forgone = hidden cost).
   - Source: same notification, as summarised by Business Standard / Moneylife reporting.
3. **The general mechanism of modern "no-cost EMI" schemes**: a retailer or platform typically prices the no-cost EMI option without the cash/upfront-payment discount that a full-payment buyer would receive, and/or a processing fee is charged separately. This is a widely and consistently described mechanism across multiple independent consumer-finance explainers, not a single-source claim, so it is treated as a safe general description rather than a specific statistic.
