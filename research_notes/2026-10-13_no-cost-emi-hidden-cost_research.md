# Research: No-Cost EMI Still Has a Cost

## Topic selected
Why zero-cost EMI is not always a free decision (topic bank priority 1; nothing published in the last 30 days).

## Why it was selected
Common audience confusion, expensive and frequent mistake, and relevant to festive-season buying. Evergreen with a clear mechanism.

## Research notes
- Per search summaries, RBI circular of 17 Sep 2013 told banks to stop zero-percent EMI schemes on card purchases, saying interest was hidden in fees and discounts should be reflected in the loan amount rather than a lower rate.
- Secondary sources agree no-cost EMI usually works as interest charged by the lender with a seller discount of similar size; GST on interest or fees, processing fees and a forgone cash discount can leave a net cost; the full amount may block card limit.
- Effective rate on the illustration computed by us: 28,500 = 5,000 x annuity(6 months); monthly rate 1.4855%, effective annual 19.36%.
