# Research Notes: The Credit-Card Minimum-Due Trap

## Topic selected

Why cardholders who pay their credit-card bill "on time" (by only clearing
the minimum amount due) can still end up paying interest, and why the
interest-free period disappears the moment less than the full bill is paid.

## Why it was selected

- Matches topic bank priority item #2: "Why people who pay their
  credit-card bill on time still get into money trouble."
- No topic has been published or drafted before (published_log.csv and
  carousel_topic_bank.md "Published" section are both empty — this is the
  first carousel produced by the routine).
- Common and expensive money mistake (priority tier 3), and a widespread
  audience confusion (priority tier 2): most first-time cardholders equate
  "paid by the due date" with "paid safely," regardless of amount paid.
- Strong save/share potential: it is a mechanism explainer people forward
  to family members who "always just pay the minimum."

## Central idea

Paying only the minimum due, on time, does not protect the interest-free
period. It ends it — for the unpaid balance and for every new purchase
made that cycle — because interest-free credit is a reward for paying the
bill in full, not for meeting the due date.

## Research notes

### 1. Loss of interest-free period on partial payment

Search-result summaries of RBI's own FAQ consistently state: if a
cardholder pays less than the full "Total Amount Due" by the due date, the
interest-free (grace) period is lost, and interest is charged from the
date of each transaction (not from the due date), on the outstanding
amount, adjusted for payments/refunds as credited — not merely on the
minimum amount left unpaid. This applies to new transactions made in the
same cycle too, not only to the carried-forward balance.

This is the core mechanism used in the carousel. **[VERIFY]** exact
wording against the RBI FAQ PDF directly, since this session could not
open it.

### 2. What "minimum amount due" typically contains

Multiple bank and aggregator sources describe minimum due as commonly
built from: roughly 5% of the outstanding balance, plus the full amount of
any active EMIs, plus previous unpaid dues, plus fees and applicable GST.
The exact percentage and formula vary by card issuer and are not set by a
single regulation — RBI does not fix the minimum-due formula, individual
issuers do, within RBI's broader conduct rules. **[VERIFY]** the specific
formula on any card mentioned in the carousel; not naming a specific bank
in the copy for this reason.

### 3. Length of the interest-free / grace period

Commonly described across issuer terms as roughly 20 to 50 days,
depending on the card and the date of purchase within the billing cycle.
This range varies by issuer and by where in the cycle a purchase falls.
The carousel avoids stating an exact number of days and instead describes
the period as ending, not the exact length, to avoid an unverified
universal figure. **[VERIFY]** if an exact figure is wanted for a future
carousel.

### 4. Monthly interest rate on revolved balances

Aggregator and issuer-adjacent sources describe typical credit card
finance charges as roughly 3% to 3.75% a month, which compounds to a high
annualised rate. This is issuer- and card-specific and changes over time,
so it is a fragile number by the mastermind doc's own standard. The
carousel uses only the softened, non-numeric framing "often works out to
well over 30% a year" rather than citing a specific bank's rate.
**[VERIFY]** current rate schedules before quoting a specific percentage
in future carousels.

### 5. The "3-day grace period" late-fee/credit-bureau-reporting rule

Search results returned inconsistent information: some describe a 3-day
buffer before a late fee or CIBIL "past due" reporting can apply as
already-described RBI guidance; one aggregator page stated an effective
date of April 2027, which appears out of step with the other reporting and
could not be reconciled from search snippets alone within this session.

**Decision: this claim is excluded from the on-slide carousel copy
entirely** because it could not be adequately verified and the reported
effective dates conflict. It is logged here only as a [VERIFY] item for a
possible future, separately-researched carousel — not used in this one.

## Claims included in the carousel (all as non-numeric or softly-hedged
statements, per the above)

1. Paying only the minimum due, even on time, ends the interest-free
   period rather than preserving it. [supports RBI FAQ summary, item 1]
2. When that happens, interest is charged from the date of each
   transaction, not from the due date, and applies to new purchases too,
   not only the unpaid balance. [item 1]
3. The interest-free period returns only once the full outstanding is
   paid by a due date again. [item 1]
4. Minimum due is typically a small slice of the bill (a percentage of the
   outstanding plus EMIs and fees), so paying it does not meaningfully
   reduce principal. [item 2, described generically, no specific % or bank
   named on-slide]
5. Revolved balances carry a monthly finance charge that commonly
   annualises to well over 30%. [item 4, stated without a specific
   percentage on-slide]
