# Research Notes: Zero-Cost EMI

## Topic selected

Why "zero-cost EMI" is not the same as a free purchase: the interest does
not disappear, it is renamed and relocated into the price, a forgone
discount, or a processing fee.

## Research notes

- The RBI's core, long-standing position (2013, reaffirmed in later
  commentary) is that a zero percent interest scheme is "non-existent" in
  practice: banks cannot lend at 0%, so any interest they would otherwise
  charge is recovered another way, most often camouflaged inside a
  processing fee, or offset against a discount the merchant would
  otherwise have offered a cash buyer.
- Multiple 2026-dated secondary explainers (Kotak MF, CardCheck,
  Smart Investing India) independently describe the same three levers
  that quietly restore the "removed" interest on a modern no-cost EMI
  purchase: (1) the cash-price discount is withdrawn or reduced once EMI
  is chosen, (2) a processing fee is charged on the EMI conversion, and
  (3) GST is applied to the interest component the bank calculates
  internally, even when a merchant subsidy cancels that interest out on
  the customer's own statement.
- Exact rupee figures for processing fees (quoted variously as
  "₹99–199 + GST" or "₹3,000–5,000 including GST" depending on the
  source, product ticket size and issuing bank) are inconsistent across
  sources and vary by bank, card network and purchase amount. Per the
  routine's rule against using fragile, variable fee figures, these
  specific rupee ranges are excluded from the on-slide copy. The slides
  refer to "a processing fee" and "GST on the interest component"
  without a numeric figure.
- No company-specific, RBI-specific dated statistic, or current interest
  rate is used on any slide. The claims used are the general mechanism
  (banks cannot lend at 0%; the cost is recovered through fee, discount
  removal or GST), which is corroborated by every source above.
- The ₹60,000 phone figure used on Slides 2 and 5 is an illustrative,
  round hypothetical price for a familiar purchase scenario. It is
  explicitly labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE" on the slide and is not
  presented as a real product price, statistic or company data point.

## Claims included in the carousel

| Claim used on slides | Status |
|---|---|
| Banks are not able to lend money at a true zero percent rate | Verified by RBI's own historical position (2013 notification, corroborated by 3 independent secondary sources) |
| On many "no cost EMI" purchases, the cash-price discount a buyer could otherwise negotiate is reduced or withdrawn | Verified by 3+ independent 2026 secondary sources describing current bank/merchant practice |
| GST can apply to the interest component the bank calculates internally, even when a merchant discount offsets it on the visible bill | Verified by 2 independent 2026 secondary sources (Kotak MF, CardCheck) |
| A processing fee is commonly charged to convert a purchase into EMI | Verified by multiple sources; no specific rupee figure used on-slide because figures vary by bank and are not stable enough to state as fact |
