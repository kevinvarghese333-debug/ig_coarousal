# Research Notes: "Zero cost" EMI — where the cost actually goes

## Topic selected

Why "zero cost EMI" / "no cost EMI" is not the same as interest-free credit, and where the interest actually shows up.

## Research notes

- The consistent, cross-source claim: in or around September 2013, RBI told banks that "the very concept of zero per cent interest is non-existent" for card-linked EMI schemes, because the interest cost has to be recovered somewhere — banks were told to keep processing fee and interest treatment uniform regardless of the sourcing/discount channel, so the cost cannot be hidden by relabelling it.
- The commonly reported mechanism, corroborated across independent finance-explainer sources: when a shopper takes "no cost EMI" instead of paying upfront, they typically forfeit an upfront cash discount that would otherwise have applied, and/or a processing fee is charged on the EMI. Either way, the amount the shopper effectively pays over the tenure is not lower than paying full price upfront minus the standard discount.
- A separate, independently stable fact used only as general category context (not a specific transaction number): GST on financial services (including loan/EMI processing fees) in India has been at the 18% slab since the GST regime's rate schedule took effect in July 2017. This is a general tax-category rate, not a bank-specific or time-sensitive number, but it is still flagged [VERIFY] below since GST slabs are set by government notification and can change.
- No specific bank name, specific processing-fee amount in rupees, or specific merchant/product was used in the carousel. The worked example on Slide 5 is explicitly labelled as an illustrative, rounded scenario, not a claim about any real transaction, bank or retailer.
