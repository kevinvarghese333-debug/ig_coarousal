# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Is Not Free

## Topic selected

Why "zero-cost" / "no-cost" EMI is not always a free decision — the cost does not disappear, it moves into a forfeited cash discount, a processing fee, GST on that fee/interest, or looser spending behaviour.

## Why it was selected

- It is Priority Topic #1 in `carousel_topic_bank.md` and has not been published in the prior 30 days (published_log.csv is empty; this is the first run).
- It is a live, recurring decision point for the target audience (buying a phone, appliance or gadget on EMI is one of the most common Indian salaried-professional purchases).
- It has a genuine hidden mechanism (merchant subvention) that most viewers have never had explained to them, which supports saves and shares.
- It allows a practical, save-worthy checklist without giving personalised financial advice.

## Primary sources consulted

1. **RBI, September 2013 circular addressing "zero percent interest" schemes on retail/consumer-durable purchases.** RBI directed banks that the concept of zero percent interest is not real; where such schemes exist, the interest is built into the processing fee or the product price, and RBI asked for the cash-discount benefit to be passed on by reducing the loan/sanctioned amount rather than being presented as "free" financing.

   - Corroborated instead through independent secondary reporting that quotes and paraphrases the circular consistently: Business Standard ("RBI asks banks to charge uniform interest rate on retail EMI schemes", Sept 2013 coverage) and Moneylife ("RBI says no zero percent interest scheme for buying consumer goods"), plus legal/compliance commentary (Vinod Kothari Consultants, "Is half-truth a lie: Hidden Costs in zero-interest loans").
   - **[VERIFY]** Exact circular number and date. Multiple secondary sources cite a circular dated on or around 17 September 2013; the exact reference number should be confirmed against rbi.org.in before this date is used on-slide with precision. On-slide copy has been kept deliberately general ("since 2013") rather than citing a specific circular number, to avoid stating an unverified reference number as fact.

2. **How merchant/brand subvention works (mechanism).** Multiple independent fintech/payments industry explainers (Innoviti, Paytm Business documentation on "EMI Subvention / No Cost EMI", Moneyview, Zwitch) describe the same mechanism: the interest a bank would normally charge is instead paid to the bank by the merchant, brand, or platform, funded as a marketing/sales cost. This is consistent across sources not related to each other, which supports treating the mechanism itself as reliable, non-controversial industry-standard information rather than a single-source claim.

3. **GST treatment of EMI-related fees.** Secondary finance-explainer sources (ClearTax, BankBazaar, myPaisaa) describe 18% GST as applying to processing fees and to the interest component that is embedded in a "no-cost" EMI, in line with the standard 18% GST rate that applies broadly to financial services and lending-related fees in India.
   - **[VERIFY]** Whether GST is separately itemised on every issuer's statement, and whether it is always exactly 18% with no exemptions, was not verified against a primary tax-authority source in this session. On-slide copy uses "often" rather than asserting this is universal.

## Claims included in the carousel (on-slide)

- No-cost EMI is not literally free; the cost is reallocated rather than removed. (Supported by RBI's stated position + industry subvention mechanism, both independently corroborated.)
- The interest a bank would normally earn is instead paid to the bank by the merchant/brand/platform (subvention), which the merchant treats as a marketing cost. (Supported by multiple independent industry sources.)
- A cash/instant discount for paying in full is often withdrawn or reduced when a buyer chooses 0% EMI instead. (Supported by industry explainers and consistent with RBI's stated concern about the "discount vs. interest" swap.)
- A processing fee, and GST on that fee and/or the interest embedded in the price, can apply even on a "no cost" EMI. (Supported by finance-explainer sources; phrased with "often"/"can" rather than as universal.)
- RBI has stated, since 2013, that the concept of a genuine zero percent interest scheme does not really exist in retail lending. (Corroborated by multiple independent secondary sources; exact circular number marked [VERIFY].)

## Claims removed or not used because they could not be adequately verified

- Any specific bank's exact processing-fee amount (e.g., a specific rupee figure charged by a named bank) — fee amounts vary by issuer and change over time; using a single named bank's fee would risk stating an outdated or non-representative number as fact. Removed from on-slide copy.
- Any claim about a specific retailer's exact discount percentage on a specific product — no primary retailer pricing data was consulted. The carousel instead uses an explicitly hypothetical, clearly-labelled illustrative example ("say a store offers 3% off for paying in full") rather than a real transaction, to demonstrate the mechanism without inventing a fact.
- Any claim that all no-cost EMI schemes charge a visible processing fee or GST line — some issuers may absorb this differently. Phrased with "can"/"often" instead of "always."
