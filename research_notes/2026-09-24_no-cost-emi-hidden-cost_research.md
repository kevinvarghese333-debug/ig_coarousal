# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Hidden Cost

## Topic Selected

Why "no-cost EMI" is not actually a free decision, and what the label is really hiding.

## Why This Topic Was Selected

- Priority topic #1 in carousel_topic_bank.md, and a first-run topic (published_log.csv is empty, no 30-day repeat conflict).
- Matches priority order item 3 (common and expensive money mistake) and item 2 (common audience confusion).
- Extremely high relevance for the 21-40 Indian salaried/first-time-borrower audience: no-cost EMI is offered at checkout on nearly every major e-commerce and retail purchase (phones, appliances, electronics) in India, so almost every reader has faced this exact screen.
- Strong save/share potential: this is a "send to a family member before they buy on EMI" carousel, and a decision-rule format (Slide 8) is inherently saveable.
- Has genuine regulatory backing (RBI has explicitly and repeatedly said "zero percent interest" is not a valid concept), so the carousel can make a sharp claim without inventing anything.

1. **RBI Master Circular on Credit Card, Debit Card and Third Party Products** (rbi.org.in, "Scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1574") — referenced via WebSearch as the RBI rule requiring card issuers to disclose principal, interest and merchant/issuer discount before converting a purchase to EMI, and prohibiting marketing an interest-bearing installment plan as "zero-interest" or "no-cost." Also requires an opt-out right for cardholders. **[VERIFY: exact circular title, date, and clause number — page could not be directly fetched this run.]**

2. **Business Standard, "RBI cracks whip on retail finance schemes with hidden cost"** (2013, business-standard.com/article/finance/rbi-cracks-whip-on-retail-finance-schemes-with-hidden-cost-113092500706_1.html) — via WebSearch: RBI's 2013 stance that "the very concept of zero percent interest is non-existent," and that such schemes "serve the purpose of alluring and exploiting the vulnerable customers." RBI directed banks/NBFCs to keep processing charges uniform across sourcing channels and pass merchant discounts through to EMI customers. **[VERIFY: could not directly fetch article to confirm exact quote wording and byline; treat quoted language as close paraphrase, not a verbatim primary-source quote, until re-confirmed.]**

3. **Moneylife, "RBI says no zero percent interest scheme for buying consumer goods"** — corroborates item 2 independently (same 2013 RBI position). Used as a second source for the same claim.

4. **BusinessToday, "Is No Cost EMI really free? When should you opt for it? Pros, cons & hidden costs"** (dated 2025-03-06 per URL slug) — via WebSearch: describes the standard mechanism — the merchant/bank discounts the sticker price by the interest amount that would otherwise be charged, so the "no interest" line on the EMI plan is really a repriced product, not free credit. **[VERIFY: could not directly fetch to confirm article date and full detail.]**

5. **BankBazaar, CRIF High Mark, Sahi, Freed — consumer finance explainers** — consistently describe two concrete hidden-cost mechanisms:
   - The cash/instant discount a walk-in or full-payment buyer would get is often not available to a no-cost EMI buyer (forfeited discount).
   - A processing fee (reported in the ₹199–₹999 range across these sources) plus 18% GST charged on the processing fee and/or the embedded interest component, billed upfront in month one.
   **[VERIFY: the specific ₹199–₹999 processing-fee range varies by lender/card issuer and by year — this is exactly the kind of "fragile fee number" the production rules say not to publish without current confirmation. Excluded from on-slide copy; kept here only as directional context.]**

## Claims Included in the Carousel (on-slide)

- No-cost EMI is not free; it means the interest cost has been moved somewhere else in the transaction, not eliminated. (Supported by sources 1, 2, 3, 4 — mechanism is consistent across every source found.)
- RBI itself has said the "zero percent interest" framing is not a valid description of these schemes. (Supported by sources 1, 2, 3 — general regulatory position, stated without quoting an exact clause or date on-slide.)
- A buyer paying the full price upfront in cash can sometimes get a discount that a no-cost EMI buyer does not receive, because the retailer prices the EMI plan off the higher, undiscounted sticker price. (Supported by sources 4, 5 — mechanism, not a specific number.)
- A no-cost EMI plan can still carry a processing fee and GST on that fee, charged in the first billing cycle. (Supported by source 5, kept as a generic "can still carry a fee" statement — no rupee amount used on-slide, per fragile-number rule.)
- RBI rules require the interest, principal and discount to be shown to the customer before the EMI conversion is confirmed, and require an opt-out. (Supported by source 1 — stated generally on-slide, without citing a specific circular number, since the exact circular could not be re-verified this run.)
