# Research Notes: "No-Cost EMI" Hidden Cost Carousel

## Topic selected

Why "No-Cost EMI" is not actually free: the interest does not disappear, it
changes shape (folded into the price, the forgone cash discount, or fees).

## Why it was selected

- Topic bank priority #1 ("Why zero-cost EMI is not always a free decision")
  and no carousel has been published or drafted yet, so no 30-day repeat
  conflict exists (`published_log.csv` and `carousel_topic_bank.md` are both
  empty of prior entries).
- Timely: mid-to-late September is the run-up to the Indian festive sale
  season (Diwali, Big Billion Days / Great Indian Festival style sales),
  when "No Cost EMI" badges are heavily pushed on phones, appliances and
  electronics.
- High save/share potential: it is a decision framework people can apply
  the next time they see the badge at checkout, and a myth many family
  members repeat ("EMI is free, no harm in taking it").
- Matches the Trap/Warning carousel structure in the mastermind doc:
  appealing surface -> missing question -> risk -> safer way to think.

## Primary sources consulted

1. **RBI circular, September 2013** - "Zero percent Interest Finance Schemes
   for consumer durables"
   - Circular reference (as reported by secondary sources; RBI's own PDF
     could not be fetched directly in this session - see note below):
     RBI/2013-14/292, DBS.CO.PPD No. 3578/11.01.005/2013-14.
   - Core finding used on-slide: RBI held that "the very concept of zero
     percent interest is non-existent" and that in zero-percent EMI schemes
     the interest element is "often camouflaged and passed on to the
     customer" (historically, via processing fee or price adjustment).
   - RBI required banks not to distort a product's interest-rate structure
     and to keep pricing transparent so customers can make an informed
     decision.
   - Source URL (RBI's own document repository, could not be fetched
     directly by the automated research tool in this session due to a
     network access restriction, but confirmed via search-indexed excerpts
     and a secondary financial-press report):
     https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/31069.pdf
   - Secondary corroborating report: Moneylife, "RBI says no zero percent
     interest scheme for buying consumer goods" (September 2013),
     https://www.moneylife.in/article/rbi-says-no-zero-percent-interest-scheme-for-buying-consumer-goods/34611.html
   - Secondary corroborating report: Business Standard, "RBI diktat on 0%
     EMI will impact banks, consumer durable firms" (September 2013),
     https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/rbi-diktat-on-0-emi-will-impact-banks-consumer-durable-firms-113092600851_1.html
   - Source date used on-slide: 2013 (year only, since the exact day could
     not be independently confirmed by direct primary-document fetch in
     this session). **[VERIFY]** exact circular date and number against the
     RBI document directly before publishing.

2. **How the cost is absorbed today** (general mechanism, not
   lender-specific figures)
   - Two common structures reported across financial-education sources:
     (a) the seller/brand funds a discount roughly equal to the bank's
     interest charge ("discount-based" no-cost EMI), or (b) the interest
     amount is built into a higher listed price for the EMI plan versus the
     upfront-cash price ("price-markup" no-cost EMI).
   - Aggregated from: Jupiter Money, "No-Cost EMI Explained: Is It Really
     Free?" (2026), https://jupiter.money/blog/no-cost-emi-explained/ ;
     Moneyview, "No Cost EMI Means, What is No Cost EMI, How It Works",
     https://moneyview.in/insights/what-is-the-meaning-of-no-cost-emi-and-how-does-it-works ;
     CRIF High Mark, "No Cost EMI: What the Discount Doesn't Tell You",
     https://www.crifhighmark.com/blog/no-cost-emi
   - Used on-slide only as a mechanism description ("the discount you would
     have got in cash" / "the price gets adjusted upward"), with no
     lender-specific numbers, since exact treatment varies by bank and
     merchant.

3. **GST on the interest/processing-fee component of card EMIs**
   - Multiple 2026 financial-content sources (Jupiter Money, myPaisaa,
     Freed) state that GST applies on the interest component that is
     booked internally on a no-cost EMI, and separately on any processing
     fee charged.
   - GST at 18% on financial/banking services has been the standard rate
     since GST's 2017 rollout and is not itself a fragile figure, but
     **whether a specific bank charges a separate visible processing fee,
     and the size of that fee, varies by lender and card** and is treated
     as **[VERIFY]** / lender-dependent. Per the production rules, this
     carousel does not put a specific rupee fee amount on any slide;
     wording used is "a processing fee, plus GST on it, is common."
   - **[VERIFY]** before publishing: confirm current GST treatment of
     credit-card EMI interest/fees directly against a CBIC/GST or bank
     product-document source rather than blog aggregators.

## Claims included in the carousel (on-slide)

1. "No-cost EMI" does not mean interest-free; RBI has held since 2013 that
   the "zero percent interest" concept does not exist and that the interest
   element is often folded elsewhere. (Slide 4) - sourced to RBI 2013
   circular via secondary reporting, flagged [VERIFY] for exact date/number.
2. The cost is commonly absorbed in one of two ways: a forgone cash
   discount, or a higher listed price for the EMI plan. (Slide 4/5) -
   general mechanism, multiple corroborating sources, no lender-specific
   numbers used.
3. A processing fee, and GST on the interest/fee, are common additions on
   card EMIs. (Slide 5) - kept qualitative ("common", "often") rather than
   a fixed rupee figure, per the no-fragile-numbers rule.
4. Smaller visible monthly amounts can change what people decide to buy,
   independent of whether they could pay the full price today. (Slide 6) -
   behavioural framing, not a statistical claim, so no external citation
   required.
