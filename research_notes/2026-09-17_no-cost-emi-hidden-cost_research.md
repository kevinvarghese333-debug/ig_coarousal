# Research Notes: No Cost EMI Is Not Actually Free

## Topic Selected

Why "No Cost EMI" / "Zero Cost EMI" offers on Indian retail and e-commerce purchases are not truly free, and what RBI has said about the practice since 2013.

## Why It Was Selected

- Ranked #1 in `carousel_topic_bank.md` priority topics, no conflicting entry in `published_log.csv` (log is currently empty, this is the first routine run).
- Matches Priority Order item 3 (a common and expensive money mistake) and item 2 (a common audience confusion): almost every salaried 21-40 audience member has used "No Cost EMI" at checkout without checking the cash price.
- Strong save/share potential: ends in a reusable three-point checklist, matches "Design for saves" guidance in the mastermind doc.
- One sharp central idea available: "No Cost EMI does not remove the interest, it moves it into a forfeited discount, a loaded price, or a fee, and RBI has said so since 2013."

## Research Notes

RBI's position that "zero percent interest" is not a real category dates to a September 2013 circular, prompted by banks/NBFCs and retailers advertising 0% EMI on credit-card-funded purchases while recovering the cost as a "processing fee" charged only through that channel. RBI's direction: interest rates and fees must stay uniform regardless of how the loan is sourced, so the cost cannot be hidden by rebranding it as a fee on one channel only. This is consistently and independently reported across three separate Business Standard articles plus Moneylife, all dated the same week (24-25 Sep 2013), and reaffirmed in a 2023 Onmanorama explainer, giving strong (though secondary-source-only) confidence in the substance of the claim.

The mechanical explanation of how "No Cost EMI" is priced today is consistent across every financial-explainer source checked (CRIF, BankBazaar, BusinessToday, Fi Money, Freed, getonecard, Score Kya Hua): the cost is covered either by forfeiting a cash discount the buyer would otherwise receive, or by loading the "interest" into the listed price before the EMI schedule is calculated. Both variants leave the EMI schedule showing "0%" while the buyer pays more than a genuine cash price.

The most current and strongest verifiable regulatory hook is RBI's Key Facts Statement (KFS) circular (15 April 2024, effective for loans sanctioned from 1 October 2024), which requires lenders to disclose the all-in Annual Percentage Rate (APR) upfront and bars charging the borrower anything beyond what the KFS discloses. This is confirmed by the primary circular reference and independently by Business Standard's April 2024 report. It is the natural resolution to the carousel's central tension: the discount/mechanism problem is old, but the disclosure fix is recent and real.

No specific 2020s enforcement action against a named bank, NBFC, or e-commerce platform for "No Cost EMI" branding could be found or verified, and none is claimed in the carousel. No NPCI-specific BNPL or EMI disclosure norm could be found; NPCI's only 2024-2025 relevant activity located was a product rollout (EMI conversion on UPI), which is unrelated to disclosure requirements and is not referenced in the carousel.
