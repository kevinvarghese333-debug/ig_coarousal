## Research notes

- The core mechanism behind "No Cost EMI" (also called zero-cost or zero-percent EMI) is interest subvention: the bank calculates its normal interest on the purchase amount, and the merchant (or brand) pays that amount to the bank upfront so the customer's EMI schedule shows no separate interest line.
- In exchange for absorbing that cost, merchants commonly remove or reduce any cash discount that would otherwise apply to a full upfront payment, and the "no cost" label typically does not disclose this trade-off at the point of sale.
- RBI communicated in September 2013 that it viewed the "zero percent EMI" framing as misleading, stating the interest was "camouflaged" and often recovered through processing fees, and directed banks to keep interest rates and processing fees uniform across products rather than presenting some as interest-free. This led issuers to rebrand "zero percent EMI" as "no cost EMI," though the underlying subvention mechanism is generally described as continuing.
- Multiple current (2026) secondary sources describe GST as applying to the interest component that banks record internally on subvention EMI, even when the customer-facing total is presented as "no cost." The exact treatment (rate, whether it is itemised separately, whether it varies by issuer) is not something this research pass could confirm against a primary CBIC or bank source, so it is marked [VERIFY] and kept soft ("can," not "does") on-slide.
- No current, verifiable, bank-specific fee schedule or interest rate was used in this carousel. The ₹60,000 phone / ₹1,800 (3%) cash-discount example on slides 2 and 5 is an invented, rounded illustration built to teach the mechanism, clearly labelled "illustrative example" and "not a specific brand or bank" on-slide, per the instruction to avoid fabricated real-world data.

## Claims included in the carousel

- The RBI 2013 statement that "zero percent" EMI interest is "camouflaged" and often recovered as a processing fee (slide 6), phrased with a specific year and framed as a historical regulatory statement, not a live rule.
- The general subvention mechanism: merchant pays the bank upfront, cash discount is usually removed in exchange (slides 4 to 5), phrased with "usually" per the brand's accuracy rules.
- GST can still apply to the interest a bank records internally on a "no cost" EMI (slide 6), phrased with "can" rather than as a universal claim.
- The illustrative ₹60,000 / ₹1,800 example (slides 2 and 5), explicitly labelled as illustrative and not real merchant data.

## Claims removed or softened because they could not be adequately verified

- Removed: any specific current GST rate (for example, "18% GST") on EMI interest. Multiple blogs cite 18% but this research pass did not confirm it against a primary CBIC source or verify it applies uniformly across issuers and product types, so no rate is stated on-slide.
- Removed: any specific bank or fintech name as an example of current "no cost EMI" practice, to avoid an unverified claim about a named institution's current terms.
- Softened: "the merchant removes the cash discount" was changed to "the merchant usually removes the cash discount" (slide 4) and "typical cash discount" with a footnote caveat (slide 5), since discount practices vary by retailer, category and season.
- Softened: GST claim uses "can still apply" rather than "always applies" (slide 6).
