# Research Notes: Why Loan Apps Are Built to Feel Effortless

## Topic selected

How instant loan apps engineer the borrowing experience to remove
friction, and what that missing friction is actually protecting you
from. Central idea: the ease of taking a small loan is a deliberate
business design choice, not a side effect, and the two RBI-mandated
disclosures that exist to counter it (the Key Facts Statement / APR,
and the cooling-off period) are the parts most borrowers never look at.

## Why it was selected

- Fits priority tier 4 (a business model with a hidden mechanism) and
  tier 3 (a common, expensive mistake: taking a "small" instant loan
  without checking the annualised cost).
- Grounded in two verifiable, current RBI regulatory actions (2022
  digital lending guidelines, 2024 Key Facts Statement circular), so
  the carousel can make a specific, defensible point rather than a
  vague warning.
- Not covered in `carousel_topic_bank.md`'s explicit list, but is a
  materially different angle on bank-item 8 ("How loan apps make
  small loans feel harmless") — this version anchors the argument to
  the KFS/APR disclosure mechanism rather than general fintech
  criticism, and to the cooling-off right rather than recovery
  practices. `published_log.csv` has no prior entries, so there is no
  30-day repeat conflict.
- Creator's NBFC-operator background gives real authority to a
  "business model" angle rather than a generic personal-finance take.

## Primary sources consulted

1. **RBI Guidelines on Digital Lending** (RBI/2022-23/111,
   DOR.CRE.REC.66/21.07.001/2022-23), effective September 2, 2022.
   Full text: https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/GUIDELINESDIGITALLENDINGD5C35A71D8124A0E92AEB940A7D25BB3.PDF
   (Source date: notification dated September 2, 2022)
   - Covers Regulated Entities (banks, co-operative banks, NBFCs
     including HFCs), Lending Service Providers (LSPs), and Digital
     Lending Apps (DLAs) of REs and LSPs.
   - Requires all loan servicing/repayment to flow directly between
     the borrower's bank account and the Regulated Entity's account —
     no pass-through or pooling account by a third-party app.
   - Mandates reporting of all digital loans to Credit Information
     Companies (CICs), regardless of tenor or amount.
   - Establishes a borrower right to a cooling-off / look-up period to
     exit a digital loan by paying principal plus proportionate
     APR, without penalty — minimum 3 days for loans with tenure of 7
     days or more.
   - Stated goals: address unregulated third parties, mis-selling,
     data-privacy breaches, unfair recovery practices and unfair
     interest rates in the digital lending ecosystem.

2. **RBI Circular: Key Facts Statement (KFS) for Loans and Advances**
   (RBI/2024-25/18, DOR.STR.REC.13/13.03.00/2024-25), dated April 15,
   2024, effective October 1, 2024.
   Full text: https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/CIRCULARKFS1504242AE2500BAF494C2A82442B0B642705C1.PDF
   (Source date: April 15, 2024)
   - Requires all Regulated Entities to give retail and MSME
     borrowers a standardised Key Facts Statement before loan
     disbursal.
   - The KFS must disclose the Annual Percentage Rate (APR): the
     all-inclusive annualised cost of the loan, including interest
     and all upfront/recurring charges (processing fee, insurance,
     documentation and any other third-party charge bundled into the
     loan).
   - Reconfirms the digital-loan cooling-off right: minimum 3 days for
     loans of 7 days' tenure or longer, minimum 1 day for loans under
     7 days, with exit allowed on payment of principal plus
     proportionate APR and no other penalty.

## Research notes

- The regulatory throughline across both documents is the same: a
  short-tenure, small-ticket loan can carry a very high *annualised*
  cost even when the flat fee looks small in rupee terms, because a
  short repayment window compresses a large percentage cost into a
  headline number that looks minor. The APR field in the KFS exists
  specifically to convert "small daily/flat fee" framing into a
  comparable annual figure.
- The cooling-off period is a real, RBI-mandated right, not a
  marketing feature — but it depends on the borrower knowing it
  exists and acting inside a narrow window (as little as 1–3 days).
- No RBI text characterises the *product design* of any specific loan
  app as intentionally friction-reducing — that framing (fewer steps,
  pre-filled KYC, one-tap disbursal) is standard, widely observed
  UX/fintech industry practice, not a regulatory finding. The
  carousel presents it as informed commentary/analysis, not as an
  RBI finding, and does not name or depict any specific app.
- No specific company, app name, market-size figure, default rate, or
  RBI enforcement action is used in this carousel. Where the topic
  bank or prior drafts might reference a named app, none is used here
  to avoid unverifiable claims about a specific product's practices.

   direct borrower-to-regulated-entity repayment, no pooling accounts,

   lenders to disclose the all-inclusive APR before disbursal. [Slides
