# Research Notes: "Zero-cost EMI has a cost. It is hidden, not removed."

## Topic selected

Why "no-cost EMI" or "zero-cost EMI" is not actually free, and what happens
to the interest that is technically not being charged.

## Why it was selected

- Falls under Priority 3 (a common and expensive money mistake) and
  Priority 4 (a business model with a hidden mechanism) in the production
  workflow's priority order.
- It is topic #1 in `carousel_topic_bank.md` and has not been drafted or
  published before (published_log.csv is empty, so no 30-day repeat
  conflict).
- The mechanism has a genuine primary-source anchor (an RBI notification),
  which keeps the carousel inside the financial-accuracy rules instead of
  relying on vague "some experts say" framing.
- High save/share potential: it hands the reader a checklist they can use
  the next time they see a "no-cost EMI" button, and a fact (RBI calling
  zero percent interest "non-existent") that is genuinely surprising to a
  non-finance audience.

## Primary sources consulted

1. **Reserve Bank of India, Notification: "Zero percent Interest Finance
   Schemes for Consumer Durables"**, dated September 25, 2013.
   PDF: https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/31069.pdf
   (RBI's own notifications index: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=4343&Mode=0)
   - Note on access: the RBI PDF domain (rbidocs.rbi.org.in) is blocked by
     this environment's network egress policy, so the document itself
     could not be fetched directly in this session. The notification's
     existence, date, and operative language below are corroborated
     independently by multiple contemporaneous news reports of the same
     RBI circular (see secondary sources). Kevin should open the RBI PDF
     link directly before publishing to confirm exact wording.
   - Reported operative language: RBI told banks that "the very concept of
     zero percent interest is non-existent" and that the interest element
     in these schemes is typically built into the processing fee or the
     product price rather than charged separately. RBI directed that
     processing charges and interest rates be kept uniform product-wise
     and sourcing-channel-wise, so the cost cannot be quietly shifted
     around to make a scheme look free.
   - Source date: September 25, 2013 (circular), reported same week by
     multiple outlets.

## Claims included in the carousel (and their basis)

1. **"RBI has said zero percent interest does not exist" / "RBI has been
   clear since 2013."** Basis: RBI's September 25, 2013 notification,
   corroborated by five independent secondary sources above. Used on Slide
   4 with a small on-slide source note ("Source: RBI, Sept 2013").
2. **"The interest does not disappear. It moves into the product price,
   a lost cash discount, or a processing fee."** Basis: same RBI
   notification's stated rationale (interest camouflaged as processing
   fee / built into pricing) plus general, well-documented industry
   practice of merchants withdrawing instant/cash discounts when a
   purchase is converted to a no-cost EMI. This is described as a general
   pattern using "often" and "can," not a universal rule, per the
   voice guidelines.
3. **Illustrative example on Slide 5 (₹40,000 phone, ₹1,500 discount).**
   This is explicitly framed as an illustrative example of the mechanism,
   not a real transaction, real retailer, or verified statistic. No
   specific brand, bank, or retailer is named.

## Claims researched but removed because they were not adequately verified

- **Exact processing-fee range (e.g. "₹99 to ₹299").** Found only in
  aggregator/finance-blog sources (e.g. CardCheck), not a primary source
  or top-tier reporting, and fees vary by issuer and change over time.
  Removed from on-slide copy. Marked [VERIFY] below.
- **"18% GST is charged on the interest/processing-fee component of
  no-cost EMI."** Found in multiple finance-content aggregator sites
  (ClearTax, Bajaj Finserv Markets, BobCard, DesiDime), not in a CBIC/GST
  Council primary source reachable this session. Directionally plausible
  (18% is the standard GST rate on financial services fees) but not
  confirmed against a primary tax source in this session. Removed from
  on-slide copy rather than risk stating a specific tax rate as fact.
  Marked [VERIFY] below.
- **Any claim naming a specific bank, NBFC, retailer, or e-commerce
  platform as engaging in this practice.** Not verified for any specific
  named entity, and the mastermind file explicitly prohibits universal
  claims about specific lenders or products. Kept the carousel general.
