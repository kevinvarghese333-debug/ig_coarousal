## Primary and high-quality sources consulted

1. **RBI Digital Lending Guidelines, released 2 September 2022.** Applicable to digital lending extended by banks and NBFCs ("Regulated Entities," REs), and covering Lending Service Providers (LSPs) and Digital Lending Apps (DLAs) that source or service loans on an RE's behalf. Core borrower-protection points corroborated across multiple legal/compliance summaries: the RE's name must be prominently disclosed to the borrower, loan amount, interest rate and method of calculation, all fees and charges, repayment schedule and penalties must be shown clearly before the borrower accepts, and outsourcing to an LSP/DLA does not reduce the RE's regulatory obligations.
   - Source: RBI notification PDF (rbidocs.rbi.org.in) identified via search; **[VERIFY]** direct text could not be fetched in this session because rbidocs.rbi.org.in is blocked by this environment's outbound network policy. Content corroborated via multiple secondary legal-compliance summaries (Nishith Desai Associates, Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas / India Corporate Law, Lawrbit, IncorpX), which independently agree on the core points above.
   - Source date: 2 September 2022.

2. **RBI Key Fact Statement (KFS) circular, dated 15 April 2024.** Requires regulated entities to give borrowers a standardised Key Fact Statement disclosing the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), all processing fees and penal charges, in a clear, consented format before a digital loan is finalised. APR is described as the "true cost of credit," bundling the headline interest rate with mandatory fees.
   - Source: corroborated across multiple legal/compliance summaries; **[VERIFY]** original RBI circular text not directly fetched this session (rbi.org.in domain blocked).
   - Source date: 15 April 2024.

3. **RBI digital lending rules include a minimum cooling-off period** (commonly cited as at least one day, extendable with board approval) during which a borrower can exit a loan by repaying principal and proportionate APR without a prepayment penalty; the lender may retain a disclosed one-time processing fee.
   - Source: corroborated across multiple legal/compliance summaries. **[VERIFY]** exact minimum period and current wording against the primary RBI Direction before quoting a specific number of days on-slide; this run does not state a specific number of days on any slide for that reason.

4. **Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025**, reported to consolidate the 2022 guidelines and subsequent circulars (including the 2023 Default Loss Guarantee clarification and the 2024 KFS circular) into a single framework, reported as issued in May 2025.
   - Source: corroborated across multiple legal/compliance summaries (Legal500, IndiaCorpLaw, Argus Partners). **[VERIFY]** exact issue date and current legal status before citing a specific document number publicly.

5. **RBI public advisory on unauthorised digital lending platforms and mobile apps.** RBI has publicly cautioned that some digital lending platforms and apps charge excessive interest, hidden charges, use unacceptable recovery methods, and misuse borrower data/agreements; RBI has recommended borrowers verify a lender is RBI-registered before using a loan app.
   - Source: Business Standard reporting (multiple articles from December 2020, describing an RBI press release), consistent with the RBI's own repeated public messaging on this topic in subsequent years.
   - Source date: original advisory reported December 2020; the underlying concern and RBI's general guidance to check registration before borrowing has been reaffirmed repeatedly since.
   - **[VERIFY]** exact current wording of RBI's most recent advisory before quoting it directly; this run uses only the general, repeatedly-corroborated point (verify lender registration, be alert to excessive charges and recovery harassment), not a specific quote.

## Claims included in the carousel (on-slide)

- Loan apps are optimised to make borrowing feel fast, small and frictionless; that is a deliberate design choice, not an accidental side effect.
- Many loan "apps" are not themselves the lender. Under RBI's framework they are often a Lending Service Provider (LSP) or Digital Lending App (DLA) acting for a bank or NBFC (the actual Regulated Entity), and that RE's name must be disclosed to the borrower.
- Because borrowing had become easy to accept without understanding its true cost, RBI now requires a standardised Key Fact Statement showing the Annual Percentage Rate (APR, the true cost of credit including fees) before a digital loan is finalised.
- RBI has repeatedly warned the public to check that a lending app is tied to an RBI-registered entity before borrowing, because unauthorised apps have been linked to excessive charges and unacceptable recovery practices.
- The practical rule: before accepting a loan-app loan, find the APR/Key Fact Statement and the name of the actual regulated lender, not just the headline "amount you get" or a small-sounding daily/weekly fee.

No specific interest rate, specific rupee loan amount, specific app name, specific number of cooling-off days, or specific circular reference number is stated as a precise, citable fact on any slide, for the verification reasons noted above.
