# Research Notes: Paying On Time Is Not The Same As Paying It Off

## Topic Selected

Why people who pay their credit card bill "on time" every month can still end up with a growing, revolving balance. The mechanism: "on time" often means paying only the Minimum Amount Due, which avoids a late fee but does not avoid interest, because paying less than the Total Amount Due cancels the card's interest-free period on the whole bill.

## Research Notes

1. **Loss of interest-free period.** Multiple independent secondary sources describing the RBI Master Direction consistently state: if a cardholder does not clear the Total Amount Due in full by the due date, the interest-free (grace) period is lost, and interest is charged from the date of each transaction, not from the due date or from a later "missed payment" date. This matches the carousel's central mechanism claim (Slide 4). Because this is a regulatory claim, it is marked [VERIFY] pending a direct read of the current primary Direction text.
2. **Minimum Amount Due (MAD) formula.** Search results describe MAD as generally calculated as the higher of (100% of interest, fees and taxes; a percentage of the total, commonly cited around 5%), plus any past-due or over-limit amount, plus any EMI instalments due. Crucially, one source noted a major bank (SBI) revised its MAD formula on July 15, 2025 to 100% of GST, EMI, fees and finance charges plus 2% of the remaining outstanding balance, confirming that the exact percentage is bank-specific and can change. The carousel therefore uses "often close to 5 percent" as a rounded, illustrative figure on Slide 5, not a universal rule, and this is flagged [VERIFY] with an instruction to check the specific bank's current MITC before publishing.
3. **Credit card interest rates in India.** Search results consistently place typical rates at roughly 3 to 4 percent per month, or about 36 to 48 percent per annum, with specific examples of ICICI Bank at 3.75 percent per month (45 percent per annum) and Axis Bank in the 35 to 40 percent per annum range. The carousel uses the range "36 to 48 percent a year" (Slide 6) and "roughly 3 to 4 percent a month" (Slide 5), both qualified with "roughly" / "can," consistent with the instruction to avoid presenting variable bank-specific fee and rate numbers as fixed universal facts. Flagged [VERIFY] to reconfirm against current bank rate cards at time of posting, since rates can change.
4. **Credit utilisation ratio and CIBIL score.** Also researched (utilisation ratio contributes roughly 30 percent of a CIBIL score; recommended to stay below 30 percent utilisation) but deliberately **not included** in this carousel. Including it would introduce a second central idea (credit score mechanics) alongside the interest-mechanism idea, which violates the one-carousel-one-idea rule in the design mastermind document. Kept out of scope for this run; flagged in `carousel_topic_bank.md` as a possible future angle ("What a credit score does not tell a lender" / utilisation-ratio angle).

## Claims Included in the Carousel

- Paying only the Minimum Amount Due, even on time, does not incur a late fee but does cause loss of the interest-free period, with interest charged on the outstanding balance from the transaction date (Slide 4). Source: RBI Master Direction (secondary-source corroborated). [VERIFY against primary text.]
- Minimum Amount Due is often a small percentage of the total bill, illustrated at "close to 5 percent" for a rounded ₹50,000 example (Slide 5). Explicitly framed as illustrative, with a note that the actual formula varies by bank. [VERIFY against current bank MITC.]
- Typical Indian credit card interest rates run roughly 36 to 48 percent per year, or 3 to 4 percent per month (Slides 5 and 6). Framed with "roughly" / "can." [VERIFY against current bank rate cards.]
- On-time minimum-due payment protects the credit report from a missed-payment mark, but does not protect the cardholder from interest (Slide 7). This is a logical consequence of claims 1 above, not a separate factual claim requiring its own citation.
