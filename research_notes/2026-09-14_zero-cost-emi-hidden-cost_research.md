## Research notes

1. **Zero percent interest is not a real lending product in India.**
   Multiple secondary sources consistently report that RBI acted (widely
   dated to September 2013) against "zero percent" EMI schemes on credit
   cards for consumer durables, stating the interest element was being
   camouflaged as a processing fee, and that any manufacturer/seller
   discount should reduce the sale price the loan is based on, not be
   used to dress up the interest rate as zero. This is consistent across
   independent outlets, so the *mechanism* is used on-slide. The exact
   circular date/number is marked **[VERIFY]** since the primary PDF could
   not be read this run.

2. **"No Cost EMI" today works by moving a seller-funded discount
   upfront** so that after the card issuer's interest is added back, the
   total paid lands close to the non-EMI price. A separate processing fee
   is commonly charged on top by the issuing bank and is not always shown
   prominently at the point of purchase. Specific bank-by-bank processing
   fee amounts vary too much to state as fact and are **excluded from
   on-slide copy**; only the general mechanism ("a processing fee often
   still applies") is used.

3. **GST is commonly reported as applying to the interest and
   processing-fee components** of an EMI conversion, not to the principal
   purchase price. Multiple sources state this, but the exact current
   rate is **[VERIFY]** before publishing, since GST rates on financial
   services have been subject to revision (GST 2.0 changes reported
   through 2025). No specific GST percentage is used on-slide; the copy
   only states that GST can apply to the interest/fee portion, with
   "check current terms" style language.

4. **Cancelling, returning, or foreclosing a No Cost EMI purchase commonly
   reduces the refund/benefit by the discount amount** that made the deal
   "no cost" in the first place. This is documented in payment-processor
   refund documentation and individual brand/bank offer terms and
   conditions (mechanism is consistent across sources, though the exact
   amount and process vary by issuer and platform). Used on-slide only as
   a general "you usually lose the discount first" statement, not a
   specific bank's policy. **[VERIFY]** current policy wording per issuer
   before publishing if a specific bank is ever named.

## Claims included in the carousel (on-slide)

- Zero percent interest is not something Indian banks are actually
  permitted to lend at; what looks like a waived interest rate is
  usually a seller discount repackaged, with a processing fee and GST
  still able to apply. (General mechanism, sourced above.)
- Cancelling, returning, paying off early, or missing a due date is
  commonly when the "no cost" discount is lost first. (General mechanism,
