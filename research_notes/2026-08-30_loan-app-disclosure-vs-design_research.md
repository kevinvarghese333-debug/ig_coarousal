# Research Notes: How Loan Apps Make Small Loans Feel Harmless

## Topic Selected

How instant loan apps make small, short-tenure borrowing feel effortless,
even after RBI forced full cost disclosure into the process. Central idea:
**RBI now forces every lending app to publish the real cost of a loan in
one document. The app's own design still makes sure the borrower never
really stops to read it.**

- Priority order match: (1) timely and verifiable Indian regulatory

## Secondary Sources (used to corroborate, not as sole source)

- Lexology / Obhan & Associates, "Rewriting the Rules of Digital Lending:
  RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2025." Confirms: Directions effective
  May 8, 2025 (date of publication); reporting of all Digital Lending
  Apps (DLAs) on RBI's Centralised Information Management System (CIMS)
  required by June 15, 2025; compliance for RE-LSP arrangements
  involving multiple lenders effective November 1, 2025. Source date:
  2025 (accessed 2026-08-30).
- Argus Partners, "RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 – An Overview."
  Corroborates the cooling-off/look-up period: borrowers get a
  cooling-off window, length set by the regulated entity's own board
  (a minimum period applies), to exit the loan by paying only principal
  and proportionate interest, without other penalty. Source date: 2025
  (accessed 2026-08-30). [VERIFY: exact minimum number of days as stated
  in the published Direction before using a specific day-count on any
  future slide; this run intentionally does not put a specific number of
  days on-slide.]
- Legal500 / Juris Corp, "The RBI's Digital Lending Directions, 2025: A
  Unified Code for a Fragmented Sector?" Corroborates the Regulated
  Entity (RE) / Lending Service Provider (LSP) structure: a lending app
  is frequently an LSP acting on behalf of a bank or NBFC (the actual
  Regulated Entity), and the RE's identity must be disclosed to the
  borrower. Source date: 2025 (accessed 2026-08-30).
- Lexology, "New digital lending directions issued by the Reserve Bank of
  India: A comprehensive overhaul of compliance norms." Corroborates the
  phased effective dates above. Source date: 2025 (accessed 2026-08-30).

## Claims Included in the Carousel (all cross-checked against 2+ sources)

1. RBI's consolidated digital lending rules (Digital Lending Directions,
   2025) took effect from their date of publication, May 8, 2025.
   Slide 5. Confirmed by 2 independent legal-analysis sources.
2. A Key Fact Statement (KFS) is mandatory and must show the Annual
   Percentage Rate (APR), i.e. the true annualised cost of the loan, as
   one figure. Slide 5. Confirmed directly from RBI's own circular text
   (via search snippet) and by secondary legal sources.
3. Most lending apps are not themselves the lender. They typically
   operate as a Lending Service Provider (LSP) on behalf of a bank or
   NBFC, which is the actual Regulated Entity extending the loan.
   Slide 4. Confirmed by 2 independent legal-analysis sources and
   consistent with RBI's long-standing digital lending framework
   (in place since the 2022 working-group guidelines and carried into
   the 2025 Directions).
4. Borrowers have a cooling-off window in which they can exit the loan
   by repaying only the principal and proportionate interest, with no
   other penalty. Slide 6. Confirmed by 2 independent legal-analysis
   sources. On-slide copy deliberately avoids stating an exact number of
   days, since the minimum length is set by each lender's own board and
   this run could not confirm the regulator-mandated floor against the
   primary text directly.

## Claims Removed or Kept Deliberately Vague

- Removed: any specific APR percentage or fee amount for a "typical"
  small-ticket loan app. APR varies loan to loan and lender to lender;
  stating a number would misrepresent an individual product's actual
  cost. The carousel instead tells the reader where to find their own
  loan's real number (the KFS).
- Removed: an exact minimum day-count for the cooling-off period. Kept
  as "a cooling-off window" without a specific number, marked [VERIFY]
  above.
- Removed: the exact date the RBI's public directory/list of verified
  Digital Lending Apps became visible to consumers. Search sources gave
  two slightly different dates (June 15, 2025 reporting deadline vs. a
  July 1, 2025 "operational" date quoted by a secondary source); rather
  than risk an inaccurate date on-slide, no directory launch date is
  used in the carousel at all.
- Not used: any claim about a specific company, app, bank or NBFC by
  name. This carousel deliberately stays generic to the RBI framework and
  does not name or imply wrongdoing by any specific lender or app.
