## Research notes (mechanism)

- Today's "No Cost EMI" (as offered by e-commerce platforms, brands and card-issuing banks/NBFCs) is a different, retailer-funded structure from the 2013-era bank consumer-durable-loan schemes the RBI notification addressed. It commonly works like this:
  - The seller/brand agrees to absorb the interest that would otherwise be charged on the EMI, in exchange for the buyer giving up an upfront cash discount that would have applied on outright payment.
  - In effect, the "discount you would have got" and the "interest you would have paid" are the same pool of money, moved around so the sticker price stays constant and the interest line reads zero.
  - Processing fees, if charged by the bank/NBFC/card issuer for converting a purchase into EMI, are separate from this interest-discount swap and are charged in addition, where applicable. Fee amounts vary widely by issuer, card type and offer, and are not stable enough to state as a fixed figure. **[VERIFY current processing fee for any specific bank/card before quoting a number; not used in carousel copy for this reason.]**
  - Whether GST applies to a given fee/charge component depends on how that component is structured by the specific lender. This session could not confirm one universal, current rate that applies identically across all no-cost EMI offers, so no specific GST percentage is stated in the on-slide copy. **[VERIFY with a current CBIC/GST notification or the specific lender's fee schedule before quoting a GST percentage on a slide.]**

## Claims included in the carousel (verified or safely hedged)

1. "No-cost EMI has been checked by the regulator" claim, in general terms: RBI has, in the past, formally objected to interest being labelled as "zero percent" when it is actually recovered from the customer through another route (framed generally as regulatory scrutiny; specific circular number is not stated on-slide because the exact reference could not be independently re-verified from the primary source in this session).
2. The core mechanism: on many no-cost EMI offers, the interest is paid for by giving up the cash discount you would otherwise receive for paying upfront. This is described using hedged language ("often", "can", "usually") rather than presented as universal across every offer, since terms vary by seller and lender.
3. Processing fees and other charges may still apply on top, and can attract GST, described in general hedged terms without a specific rupee or percentage figure.
4. The behavioural point (no-cost EMI makes it easier to buy something you would have hesitated to pay for outright) is a reasoned interpretation of established consumer-behaviour material on instalment framing, not a cited statistic, and is presented as an observation/insight rather than as a sourced statistic.
