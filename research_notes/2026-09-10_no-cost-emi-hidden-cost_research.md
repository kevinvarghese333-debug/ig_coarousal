# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Hidden Cost

## Topic selected

Why "No Cost EMI" / "Zero Cost EMI" is not actually free. The interest does
not disappear, it is repositioned into a foregone cash discount, a
processing fee, and GST on that fee, and it changes buying behaviour.

  angle (published_log.csv is empty, this is the first carousel produced

- Common and expensive money mistake, directly tied to a moment almost the

   fee." Reported consistently by Business Standard, Moneylife and

2. **RBI Master Direction - Credit Card and Debit Card - Issuance and
   Conduct Directions, 2022** (last amended March 2024). Governs
   disclosure of EMI/loan-conversion terms on credit cards; cardholders
   have the right to opt out and issuers must disclose interest and fees
   in the Most Important Terms & Conditions (MITC).
   - https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12300
   - https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/92MDCREDITDEBITCARDC423AFFB5E7945149C95CDD2F71E9158.PDF
   - [VERIFY: could not open the RBI page directly this session; used for
     the general, stable fact that EMI-conversion terms must be disclosed
     and are opt-out, not for any specific fee number.]

3. **Mechanism of "No Cost EMI" (subvention model):** the bank still
   charges its normal interest rate on the loan. The seller/platform pays
   that interest amount to the bank upfront as a "subvention," and
   recovers it by removing the cash discount that would otherwise apply,
   and/or through the base price. This is the consistent explanation
   across ClearTax, Zerodha Varsity and 1PageFinance.
   - https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-no-cost-emi
   - https://zerodhavarsity.substack.com/p/the-actual-cost-of-no-cost-emi
   - https://1pagefinance.substack.com/p/-the-science-of-no-cost-emi

4. **GST on the fee/interest component:** GST at the standard 18% rate
   applies to financial services, including processing fees charged on
   EMI conversions and to any notional interest/fee the bank levies. This
   is the standard GST slab for financial services in India since the
   2017 GST rollout and is stable, well-documented information, not a
   fee that varies by bank.
   - https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-no-cost-emi

## Claims included in the carousel

- No-cost EMI does not mean the bank lends for free; the interest is
  still charged, just not itemised as "interest" to the buyer.
- The most common way the cost is recovered: the buyer loses the cash
  discount they would have received for paying in full ("subvention").
- A processing fee plus GST on that fee is a common extra layer on
  EMI conversions, in addition to any lost discount.
- The RBI's long-standing position (2013 circular, still cited by
  current commentary) is that "zero percent interest" does not exist as
  a lending product, and interest is sometimes folded into a fee instead.
  [VERIFY tag kept in draft next to this claim since the primary RBI page
  could not be opened directly this session.]
- Practical rule: compare the total amount paid across the EMI tenure
  against the one-time cash price, not the monthly instalment number
  alone, before choosing "No Cost EMI."

## Claims removed because they were not adequately verified

- Specific rupee figures for processing fees (blogs cite ranges like
  ₹199-999) were removed from on-slide copy. Fees vary by bank, card and
  merchant tie-up and are not a stable, single verifiable number, so the
  slides describe the fee mechanism ("a processing fee, plus GST on it")
  rather than quoting a specific amount.
- Any claim naming a specific bank, e-commerce platform or product by
  name was excluded. The worked example in Slide 5 uses round,
  clearly-labelled illustrative numbers (not attributed to a real
  transaction or company) purely to demonstrate the arithmetic of a lost
  discount.
- A claim about EMI foreclosure charges being capped at 3% of the
  outstanding amount was found in search results but concerns a
  different product context (loan foreclosure, not EMI-conversion
  no-cost schemes) and was dropped as off-topic and not fully verified
  for this specific use case.
