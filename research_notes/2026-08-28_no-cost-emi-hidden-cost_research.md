# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Is Not a Free Purchase

## Topic Selected

Why "No Cost EMI" (marketed as 0% interest EMI) is not actually free. The
interest cost does not disappear. It is usually absorbed by removing a
cash discount the buyer would otherwise get, or by folding the cost into
the listed price, or by GST charged on the processing fee.

## Research Notes

- In September 2013, the RBI told banks that "the very concept of zero
  percent interest is non-existent" and that the interest cost on these
  schemes was typically camouflaged and recovered from the customer
  through the processing fee, rather than genuinely waived.
- RBI directed banks to keep processing fees and interest rates uniform
  across a product/segment regardless of whether a customer chose the
  EMI route, precisely because the "0%" label was being used to obscure
  where the cost actually sat.
- Retailers pushed back after the ruling because "zero percent EMI"
  schemes were a major festive-season sales driver, which itself is
  evidence the cost of the scheme was real and material, not a true
  freebie absorbed painlessly by someone else.
- Industry and consumer-finance commentary since 2013 (including the
  bankbazaar.com and cardcheck.in explainers found in this search) is
  consistent in describing the "no cost EMI" label used today as the
  rebranded version of the same underlying schemes: the interest cost is
  built into the product's listed price, or the buyer forfeits a cash
  discount that a cash/one-time payment would have received.
- This carousel does not claim any specific bank's current processing
  fee, GST rate, or interest rate, because those vary by bank, lender,
  card network and retailer and change over time. The mastermind brief
  explicitly disallows fragile, variable fee/rate figures unless clearly
  verified and current, so none are used as fact.

## Claims Included in the Carousel (as fact, on-slide)

1. In 2013, the RBI stated that zero percent interest does not exist and
   that the cost of "0% EMI" schemes was often recovered from the
   customer rather than genuinely waived. — Supported by sources 1, 2, 3
   above (three independent contemporaneous reports).
2. No-cost EMI schemes typically recover their cost either through the
   listed price of the item, or by removing a cash discount that a
   one-time payment would otherwise receive. — Supported by sources 1, 2
   and the general mechanism described in bankbazaar.com and
   cardcheck.in explainers.
3. The general framework: "if a business voluntarily gives up ₹0 in
   interest and ₹0 in fees, and the price stays the same, someone in
   that chain is still absorbing a cost" is presented as a reasoning
   principle, not a specific statistic, and requires no external source.
