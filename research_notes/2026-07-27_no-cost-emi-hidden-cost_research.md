# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Hidden Cost

## Core mechanism (cross-verified across multiple sources)

- "No cost EMI" works through **interest subvention**: the bank still charges
  interest on the loan, but the merchant pays that interest amount to the
  bank upfront as a rebate/subvention, instead of the customer paying it in
  monthly instalments. The bank is never lending for free.
- RBI has publicly taken the position that the "no cost EMI" label is
  misleading because interest is not actually zero. It is often
  "camouflaged" and recovered through processing fees or built into the
  product price. RBI directed banks to disclose a uniform, transparent
  interest rate and processing fee on such schemes rather than advertise
  0%.
- The common way merchants fund the subvention: they simply do not offer
  the same upfront/cash discount to no-cost EMI buyers that they offer to
  full-payment buyers. Choosing EMI over paying in full effectively means
  giving up that discount. Economically, the forfeited discount **is** the
  interest, just relabelled.
- On credit-card no-cost EMI conversions specifically, banks routinely levy
  a **processing fee** even when interest is nil, and GST (18%) is charged
  on the interest/finance component of a regular EMI conversion, which
  contributes to the "no cost" plan sometimes still carrying a fee line
  on the statement.

Solid, consistent across sources, safe to state as mechanism-level fact:
- Interest subvention is how no-cost EMI is funded.
- RBI has criticised the "no cost EMI" framing as misleading / interest
  being camouflaged as fees.
- Forfeiting a cash/instant discount is the most common way the cost is
  recovered from the buyer.
