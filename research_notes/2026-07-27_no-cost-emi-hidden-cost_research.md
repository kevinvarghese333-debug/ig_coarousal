# Research Notes: No-Cost EMI Hidden Cost

## Topic selected

Why "zero-cost" or "no-cost" EMI is not actually a free decision.

## Research notes

- In September 2013, RBI communicated to banks that "the very concept of zero percent interest is non-existent" and that fair practice requires processing charges and the rate of interest to be kept uniform across a product or segment, regardless of whether the customer pays upfront or via EMI.
- RBI's stated concern: in "zero percent" EMI schemes on credit cards, the interest cost was often folded into the price of the product or charged as a processing fee instead of being shown as interest, making the scheme non-transparent.
- RBI also addressed the "lost discount" complaint: customers who pay upfront/cash for a product are often offered a cash discount that customers choosing the EMI route do not receive. RBI's position was that banks should not let this distort pricing and that discounts should not simply disappear for the EMI buyer.
- Practical result described consistently across sources: a "no cost EMI" scheme usually recovers its cost through one or more of: (a) building the interest into the listed product price, (b) a processing fee, (c) withdrawal of a cash/upfront-payment discount.
- Processing fees on EMI/credit schemes attract GST. Financial-service fees (including loan processing fees) are commonly taxed at the 18% GST slab under India's GST structure.
