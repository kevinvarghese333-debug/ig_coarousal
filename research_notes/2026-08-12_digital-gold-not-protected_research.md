# Research Notes: The ₹100 Digital Gold Button Is Not Protected

## Topic Selected

Digital gold, bought in small amounts through UPI apps (Paytm, PhonePe,
Google Pay and similar), is not regulated by SEBI or RBI, and most buyers
assume the protections of a bank deposit or a stock-market investment apply
to it. They do not.

## Research Notes

- SEBI issued a public warning, reported around 8-11 November 2025, that
  digital gold products offered on platforms such as Paytm, PhonePe,
  Google Pay and CaratLane are not regulated under its framework, and do
  not fall under RBI's purview either. The product sits outside both the
  securities-law bucket and the banking/payments bucket, so neither
  regulator directly oversees it.
- Because of this gap, digital gold has no mandated disclosure norms, no
  statutory grievance redressal mechanism, and no capital safeguards of
  the kind that apply to mutual funds, stocks, gold ETFs or sovereign gold
  bonds.
- SEBI's language, as reported, was that such offerings "may entail
  significant counterparty and operational risks."
- The gold itself is typically sourced through one of three private
  providers: MMTC-PAMP (a JV involving government-backed MMTC), SafeGold
  (Digital Gold India Pvt Ltd, which powers PhonePe, Paytm and Amazon Pay),
  or Augmont. These providers say the gold is stored in insured vaults,
  but reporting notes there is no law requiring standardised, mandatory
  third-party audits confirming that the physical gold backing every
  rupee held actually exists. This is phrased carefully on-slide: the risk
  is about the absence of a regulatory guarantee, not an accusation that
  any specific provider is currently failing to hold gold.
- UPI-based digital gold buying has grown quickly through 2025: reported
  figures include a rise from roughly 21 million transactions in April
  2024 to around 100 million transactions in August 2025, and transaction
  value reaching roughly ₹2,290 crore in October 2025 according to
  NPCI-linked reporting. Because exact figures varied slightly between the
  sources found in this session (different base months and slightly
  different totals), the carousel uses the directionally consistent,
  clearly-attributed claim that "digital gold buying through UPI has grown
  very fast," and avoids stating one single precise transaction count as
  an exact, undisputed number. The clearest specific figure, "roughly 100
  million transactions in a single month by autumn 2025," is used with an
  "around" qualifier and cited to NPCI-linked reporting via the sources
  above.

## Claims Included in the Carousel

1. Digital gold sold through UPI apps is not regulated by SEBI or RBI.
   Support: sources 1-5.
2. This means no mandated disclosure norms, no statutory grievance
   redressal, and no capital safeguards of the kind that apply to
   regulated investments. Support: sources 1-4.
3. SEBI's November 2025 warning described "significant counterparty and
   operational risks." Support: sources 1, 2, 4 (consistent phrasing
   reported across outlets).
4. Digital gold buying through UPI has grown very fast through 2025,
   phrased with "around" rather than a single precise figure. Support:
   search-result summaries citing NPCI-linked data, cross-checked across
   two independent queries.
5. The gold is typically issued by one of a small number of private
   providers (MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold, Augmont) who say it is vault-stored and
