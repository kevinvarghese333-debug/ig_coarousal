# Research Notes: The Credit Card "Minimum Due" Trap

## Topic selected

Why paying only the credit card "minimum amount due" on time still lets interest quietly pile up on the whole bill, including new purchases.

## Why it was selected

- This is priority topic #2 from `carousel_topic_bank.md`: "Why people who pay their credit-card bill on time still get into money trouble." No topic has been published or drafted in the last 30 days (published_log.csv is empty; this is the first production run).
- It is a common and expensive money mistake (Step 2, priority 3 in the routine): a huge number of Indian credit card users pay only the "minimum due" believing they are being responsible, without realising it costs them the interest-free period.
- Mechanism-driven and behavioural, matching the brand's "explain the thing behind the thing" position.
- Verifiable against a primary regulatory source (RBI Master Direction on Credit Card and Debit Card issuance).
- High save/share potential: it is a decision rule ("pay in full or lose the interest-free period on everything") that people forward to family members who carry card balances.

   - Core rule reflected in the FAQ (per search snippet, 2026-08-02): if a cardholder does not clear the **total amount due** by the payment due date, the interest-free credit period is lost, and interest may be levied **from the date of each transaction** on the outstanding amount, not just from the due date.

2. Cross-checked against three independent secondary explainers (Moneyview, Finowings, Arthzo — all accessed 2026-08-02) summarising the same RBI Master Direction provision. All three independently describe the same mechanism: partial payment (including minimum due) revokes the interest-free period on the entire outstanding balance and on new transactions until the statement is paid in full.

3. RBI guidance on Minimum Amount Due composition (per secondary sources summarising RBI's 2022 Master Direction and later clarifications): the Minimum Amount Due is structured to cover the full interest, fees and taxes charged for the period, plus a small part of the principal, specifically so that unpaid interest does not compound into new interest ("interest on interest") in the next cycle. ## Claims included in the carousel

- Paying only the minimum due keeps the account "not overdue" and protects the credit score, but it does not protect the interest-free period. **Verified** against RBI Master Direction (via FAQ snippet + 3 independent secondary sources).
- If the total amount due is not paid in full, interest is charged from the transaction date on the outstanding amount, including on new purchases made during that cycle. **Verified**, same sources as above.
- The minimum due is calculated to cover interest, fees and taxes plus a small part of principal, not to shrink the debt meaningfully. **Verified at a mechanism level**; no specific percentage used on-slide.
- Credit card interest rates are high compared to most other borrowing, and the exact rate is set by the issuer and stated in the cardholder's Most Important Terms and Conditions (MITC)/card statement. **General/qualified claim only** — no specific APR number is used on-slide because rates vary by issuer, card variant and are revised periodically.

## Claims removed because they were not adequately verified

- Any specific credit card APR/interest rate figure (e.g. "36% to 45% per annum"). Removed from on-slide copy. Rates are issuer- and card-specific, change over time, and RBI does not cap them at a single published figure. Slide copy instead says "the rate is almost always higher than a personal loan" and points the reader to "check your card's MITC," which is accurate without inventing a number.
- A newly reported "3-day grace period" before late-payment penal charges apply. Search results gave conflicting effective dates (some describing it as already in force for 2025-26, one source describing an April 2027 implementation date), so the timeline could not be pinned down confidently in this session. This claim is excluded entirely from the carousel. It also is not central to this carousel's idea, since the grace period (per every source, including the conflicting ones) explicitly does NOT extend the interest-free period, so leaving it out does not weaken the central point.
- Any specific rupee example tied to a real bank or card product. Not used, to avoid implying a claim against a specific issuer without their statement in hand.
